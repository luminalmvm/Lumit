Eleven hundred commits since 0.2.0, and the largest release Lumit has had. Every panel
has been redrawn to the design it was specified against, the node graph and the audio
workspace both exist for the first time, Lumit hosts OFX effects and CLAP and VST3
instruments, After Effects projects open directly, and the tracker, Roto brush and Puppet
tools have landed. Over a hundred effects now ship built in.

This is still a beta. Plenty below has arrived recently enough that it wants real projects
run through it, and the known gaps are listed at the end.

## What is new

### The interface, redrawn

- Every panel has been rebuilt to the drawings: the Timeline, Viewer, Project panel,
  Effect controls, Settings, the welcome screen, the export and composition dialogues.
- Lumit has its own type and palette now - Hanken Grotesk and Geist Mono, spruce as the
  accent, with clay one click away in Settings.
- Two shapes, Sharp and Round, and two row densities, Regular and Compact. All four are
  settings.
- Chrome can read as words or as icons, and the icon set is Lumit's own.
- Dialogues are one pattern throughout, and every one of them is backed by the engine
  rather than drawn and hoped for.

### The Timeline

- Two modes on one outline: **Layers** for bars and summary keys, **Graph** for the
  curves of whatever is picked.
- An **Animated** filter on the outline's own bar, and pressing U opens what is animated.
- A time navigator spans the panel, and the ruler reads as one band with the cache bar on
  its floor.
- A keyframe's shape says its interpolation, half by half, so a ramp reads without opening
  anything.
- Marquee selection from any ground, additive with Shift, and a property's name selects
  all of its keys. Every multi-selection edit is one edit and one undo.
- Dragging one bar of a selection drags the whole selection.
- The switch columns drag, and give up their cells in a named order as the panel narrows.
- The rows in view are the rows that get built, so a long comp scrolls at the same speed as
  a short one.

### The node graph

- A second view of the effect stack that can also wire effects together, with a driver
  graph beside it. The **Nodes** workspace is built around it.
- Wire types have colours, and a points stream is a first-class type.
- **Custom shader**: write WGSL and it becomes an ordinary effect, with its rows read out
  of its own text. Entering one works like entering a precomp.
- Split and Combine cross between a colour and its four numbers.
- A node's preview folds into the Viewer as an "at effect" chip rather than being a second
  viewer.

### The Audio workspace

- A **Mixer** with a strip per sounding row of the composition, metered on the callback
  that makes the sound, and a master fader ahead of a limiter.
- Pan, as a constant-power balance.
- Fades are keyframe pairs; a clip join is two opposed equal-power ramps, and a Sequence
  layer sounds.
- Beat detection writes markers and numbers bars from a grid the project keeps.
- A spectral lane per layer, drawn from a tiled spectrogram, beside the waveform lane.
- **Lower behind** ducks one layer under another, wired through a Volume socket on the
  Layer out box.
- **Detach audio** copies a layer's sound onto a muted sibling.
- An audio-only export takes no picture settings, in the dialogue and in the engine.

### Plugins

- **Lumit is an OFX host.** Plugins installed for other applications are found at start-up
  and sit beside the built-in effects, grouped, searchable and starrable like any other.
- Each plugin runs in its own process, so one that crashes costs a frame rather than the
  session. A plugin Lumit cannot drive is a line in a report, not a silence.
- **CLAP and VST3 instruments and effects** host as inserts in a layer's own audio chain,
  on the same broker.
- A third-party After Effects effect imports as the vendor's own OFX plug-in when it is
  installed, and as the closest Lumit effect when it is not.

### After Effects projects

- Lumit reads `.aep` files directly, and the Bridge panel is still there as the backstop
  for what a file does not store.
- Keyframes carry across value for value, with their eases and spatial tangents; masks,
  mattes, parenting, markers, blend modes and both of AE's time controls come with them.
- The effect map is a shipped table, and an import report names what changed and what
  could not come at all.
- An imported camera keeps its motion, and an expression that came from AE is switched off
  rather than installed and left to fail.

### Tracking

- **Camera track** solves a shot: pose per frame, focal per segment, a point cloud you can
  select from, and a Camera layer linked to the solve. It works on a Precomp layer too.
- **Planar track** follows a surface and hands its answer to a Corner pin.
- New in this release: the same track can follow **one point or two**, and
  **Create transform keys** bakes it onto a layer's Position, Rotation and Scale - added to
  what is already there rather than stamped over it.
- A track that stops being followable is solved as far as it went, and says so.

### Roto brush and Puppet

- **Roto brush**: scribble on a frame, and the matte propagates through the shot on the
  in-tree optical flow. The first scribble solves the frame it touched.
- **Puppet**: pins on a conforming mesh with an as-rigid-as-possible solve, four tools
  armed in the Viewer.

### Paint, shapes and text

- Paint strokes land on any layer that draws, including a Precomp, and carry the pressure
  they were drawn with.
- A stroke can draw itself on, trimmed by length, and blends by the layer list.
- Shape modifiers: Trim paths, Repeater, Offset path, dashes, gradient fill, and a boolean
  combine of two shapes.
- A shape's path morphs by the mask's own keys.
- **Text animators** - one selector, two shapes and five property groups - plus text on a
  mask path, and conversion to shapes or to points.

### Particles and the points family

- **Particulate** evaluates in closed form: no simulation state, every frame
  random-access, and the particle cap is the dial that bounds it. Its particles live in 3D
  and the composition's camera sees them.
- A points stream is carried on a wire, and six effects produce or consume one: Grid,
  Scatter, Emit from image, Clone to points, Trail and Connect points.

### Effects

- Over a hundred ship built in, across nine categories - Generate and Transition are new.
- **Every effect can be driven by a matte**, through one row that picks its channel, and a
  Mix row that picks its blend.
- Curves is a real curve editor, and Levels draws its histogram.
- Expression controls - slider, angle, checkbox, colour and point - are effects like any
  other.
- The physical lens flare gained spectral ghosts, Fresnel edges, a field-dependent
  starburst and per-element coatings.
- Every distance and every point is measured in pixels at composition size. Nothing is a
  percentage of the frame any more.

### Colour, export and media

- Sixteen-bit export is sixteen real bits, through a deep display target.
- Five built-in colour spaces the file states, and a resize that picks its own filter.
- The export dialogue is one page with a table of contents; a section the format cannot use
  is disabled and says nothing about it.
- The export queue is the engine's, and reorders without an undo step.
- **Proxies**: a second media reference at one resolution point, with the row saying which
  file is being read.
- A folder of numbered stills imports as one footage item.
- Media is looked for by name beside the project, and a relink rewrites the longest prefix
  it can.

### Smaller things

- **Guide layers**: a per-layer switch the Viewer honours and no exported file carries.
- **Layer groups** as the light fold, with Precompose still one click away.
- **Layer styles** as a second, order-locked list on the layer.
- A **History** panel over the operation journal, jumped one step at a time.
- An **Easing** panel of curve tiles that apply on click, and easings you draw follow you
  rather than the project.
- Rulers and guides on the panel, with the magnet back.
- Snapshots photograph the visible region at the resolution it is drawn at.
- A workspace you save is a file of its own.
- One search surface for effects and compositions, on Ctrl+Space.
- Every Project row carries a hue square that sets its label, and a folder's colour tints
  what it holds.
- A composition remembers where you were, and a precomp opens on the frame it is showing.
- Escape steps back one rung at a time rather than dropping everything.
- Translation has moved off Crowdin and onto the site.

## Performance

- The interface is now a measured product requirement: 60 fps minimum, budgeted for 120.
- The Timeline builds only the rows in view and reuses them across a scroll.
- A drag asks the engine nothing about a document it is not changing, and an edit's
  follow-on work is one wave rather than a call per item.
- Each effect's output is kept, so editing the last effect in a stack re-runs only that
  one. A precomp's frames cache as one unit, and layers under a full-frame opaque layer are
  not rendered at all.
- The idle cache fill wraps the work area and keeps going into RAM.
- A lost graphics device is rebuilt rather than survived by luck.

## Known gaps

- Colour management is half built: the OCIO implementation is in, the surfaces around it
  are not finished.
- Custom shader nodes - the graph inside a shader - are still to come; the shader effect
  itself is here.
- Lights and camera depth of field are not built. Layers are planes in space.
- Roto brush propagation is well over its frame budget on the classical path, so long
  shots are slow.
- The Windows installer is unsigned, so SmartScreen still warns. macOS builds are signed
  and notarised.

## Fixed

- The razor cuts through an eased speed ramp without moving the keys either side.
- Changing a composition's frame rate leaves the playhead where it was in time.
- An animated aperture no longer stops the lens flare's frames from caching.
- A beat source picked by name is always heard, whatever the comp mix is doing.
- A layer added into a composition that has a solo up is born soloed.
- Tile's output window is centred on the tile centre.
- A click on a row that is already the lone selection is not a second command.
- A project saved under a bare name gets `.lum`.
