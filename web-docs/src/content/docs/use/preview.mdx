## Play

Press play, or `space` by default. The playhead will begin moving and the Viewer will update.
`K` stops playback.

`L` plays forwards and `J` plays backwards. Each further press doubles the speed, up to
eight times, and a press the other way steps back down. Sound plays at normal speed
forwards only.

Playback starts at the playhead and runs to the end of the **work area**, which is the
whole composition until it is narrowed. Started from past the work area's end, it plays on
to the end of the composition and stops.

When playback stops, the playhead goes back to where it started. **Edit ▸ Settings ▸
Timeline ▸ Playhead stays where playback stopped** leaves it where the picture stopped.
Dragging the playhead in the Timeline stops playback and leaves the playhead where it was
dragged.

A `J` or `L` shuttle leaves the playhead where it stops. A backwards shuttle stops at the
start of the work area.

### At the end of the work area

The loop mark on the [Viewer bar](/panels/viewer/#the-strip-below) sets what happens there.
Click it to step through the three, or right-click it to pick one.

| Mode | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Loop** | Starts again from the work area's start. |
| **Play once** | Stops. |
| **Ping-pong** | Plays the work area backwards, then forwards again. The backward pass has no sound. |

### Moving the playhead

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `Ctrl+→`, `Page Down` | Next frame. |
| `Ctrl+←`, `Page Up` | Previous frame. |
| `Shift+Page Down`, `Shift+Page Up` | Forward or back ten frames. |
| `Home`, `End` | The first or the last frame of the composition. |
| `Shift+Home`, `Shift+End` | The start or the end of the work area. |
| `I`, `O` | The selected layer's in or out point. |
| `B`, `N` | Set the work area's start or end at the playhead. |

The transport buttons on the Viewer bar go to the first frame, step a frame either way,
play or pause, and go to the last frame. Click the clock beside them to type a time.

### Playback speed

The **Playback speed** picker on the [Viewer](/panels/viewer/#the-strip-above) plays at
**1×**, **0.5×** or **0.25×** of the composition's rate. Every frame is shown, and the sound is slowed with its
pitch kept. The picker reads in the warning colour while it is not 1×.

The speed is not saved, and never reaches an export. A `J` or `L` shuttle plays at its own
speed.

### Sound

Sound plays with the picture. Under **Every frame** it pauses while the picture is behind.
**Mute**, in the transport's right-click menu and on the Viewer's
[deck](/panels/viewer/#the-deck), silences the output. It is not remembered between
launches.

With several views in the Viewer, one view plays. See
[Several views](/panels/viewer/#several-views).

## The cache

The cache has three tiers: VRAM, RAM and disk. You can alter the memory allocated to each of 
these individually under **Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Preview and cache**.

Lumit stores rendered frames to help speed up playback. Frames that are cached are shown by 
the **cache bar** under the time ruler: VRAM and RAM display as a green line, and frames
stored on the disk are blue.

Either colour drawn dimmed is a frame cached at a lower resolution than the Viewer is
showing. Nothing drawn is a frame that is not cached.

While nothing is playing, Lumit goes on rendering the work area into the cache, starting
at the playhead. It does not do this while the Viewer's exposure is off zero or tone
mapping is on, or while a [3D viewport](/use/camera/#3d-viewport) is the active view.

When the computer runs short of memory, Lumit gives back the least recently used half of
the RAM tier. The budget stays as it is set.

The [status line](/use/workspaces/#the-status-line) shows how full each tier is, and the
Viewer's [deck](/panels/viewer/#the-deck) shows how much of the work area is cached.

## Preview resolution

Lower the preview resolution to render previews faster: full, half, third, quarter, or auto. 
Changing the preview resolution has no impact when exporting.

| Resolution | Renders |
| --- | --- |
| **Auto** | Only as many pixels as the panel can show at its size. |
| **Full** | The composition's own size. This is what a composition starts on. |
| **Half**, **Third**, **Quarter** | That fraction of the composition's width and height. |

The resolution is kept for each composition. `Ctrl+J`, `Ctrl+Shift+J` and `Ctrl+Alt+J`
choose Full, Half and Quarter, and **View ▸ Resolution** lists all five.

Zooming the picture does not change the resolution it is rendered at.

Lumit also has two preview resolution options in the Viewer bar. **Every frame** will
render each frame in the composition, pausing audio if playback isn't realtime.
**Adaptive res** will instead dynamically alter the resolution to try and maintain
realtime playback; if even quarter resolution isn't enough, it will only display frames it
manages to render in time.

Personally I'd normally recommend **Every frame** as the default option to use.

The same choice is **Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Preview and cache ▸ When the machine cannot keep
up**. **Quality tier** beside it reads the resolution adaptive playback has settled on, and
**Reset** restarts it.

While the preview is rendered smaller than the composition, the reading at the right of
the Viewer bar shows both sizes, as `1920×1080 → 960×540`.

## The progress bar

While waiting on a slow frame, a progress bar appears on the Viewer's bottom bar, to the left
of the reading, and fills as the frame passes through decoding, building, and compositing.

It names the stage it is on: *Preparing*, *Reading media*, *Reading the composition*,
*Simulating*, *Compositing*, *Showing* or *Rendering*. It does not appear during playback,
or for a frame that arrives within 150 milliseconds.

## Adaptive degradation

Under load, Lumit reduces quality on its own to keep responding. It may drop resolution
while scrubbing effect values as you change them.

While a value is dragged, the picture is rendered at the first of full, half, third and
quarter resolution that has no more pixels than 640×360, and at quarter where none does.
Lens flares draw at **Draft** quality. The picture is rendered at the Viewer's own
resolution again on release. **Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Preview and cache ▸ Full resolution while
dragging** renders a drag at the Viewer's own resolution.

A drag whose frames take longer than 50 milliseconds is rendered coarser again, and finer
once they are quick.

- **Adaptive resolution limit**: how coarse a slow drag may go. **Auto** stops at an
  eighth, and **1/2**, **1/4**, **1/8** and **1/16** stop there.
- **Wireframe while dragging**: a drag draws each layer as the outline of its rectangle,
  with no effects, layer styles, mattes or motion blur. Off by default.
- **Live update**: on by default. Off, a drag draws no frame and the Viewer keeps the last
  one until the release. It is not saved.

The first two are in **Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Preview and cache** and on the Viewer's
[deck](/panels/viewer/#the-deck). **Live update** is in the picture's
[right-click menu](/panels/viewer/#the-right-click-menu).

## Suspend updates

**Suspend updates**, in the picture's right-click menu, keeps the Viewer on the picture it
has while edits go on. A red bar along the bottom of the picture reads **Viewer updates
suspended**. Choosing it again draws the frame at the playhead.

`Caps Lock` does the same: on, updates are suspended, and off, they resume. **Edit ▸
Settings ▸ Preview and cache ▸ Caps Lock suspends Viewer updates** turns that off. The eye
mark on the Viewer's deck is the same switch, and is red and shut while updates are
suspended.

## Footage at shown size

A footage layer shown smaller than its own size is decoded for the preview no larger than
it is shown. An export decodes at full size. The layer is decoded at its own size while it
has a parent, is 3D or an adjustment layer, has masks, paint, a puppet, layer styles, a
node graph or effects, has an expression on its Scale, or sits in a composition whose
Precomp layer has [Collapse](/use/layers/#collapse-transformations) on.

**Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Preview and cache ▸ Decode footage at shown size** turns it off. It is
on by default, and the **Shown size** mark on the Viewer's deck is the same switch.

## The region of interest

A [region of interest](/panels/viewer/#region-of-interest) renders one rectangle of the
frame and leaves the rest.

## If playback is slow

1. Lower the preview resolution.
2. Let the cache fill on a first pass, then play again.
3. Turn off heavy effects while you block out timing.
4. Precompose a finished section so it caches as one unit.
