Audio plays in preview, and exporting will retain audio unless specifically turned off.

The **Audio** workspace arranges Lumit for sound: the [Mixer](/panels/audio/#mixer) on the left,
the [Audio panel](/panels/audio/#audio-panel) on the right, and the
[Audio timeline](/panels/audio/#audio-timeline) across the bottom.

## What is heard

- A layer plays its sound while its audible switch is on.
- While a layer that makes sound is soloed, only the soloed layers are heard. A soloed layer
  with no sound silences nothing.
- A retimed layer or clip plays its sound at its speed. See
  [Sound](/use/retime/#sound) for **Keep pitch**.
- The **Mute** mark on the [Viewer's deck](/panels/viewer/#the-deck) silences the output
  and leaves the mix as it is.
- During playback the [status line](/use/workspaces/#the-status-line) reads the output's
  peak, in dB.

The output device is set in [Settings ▸ Audio](/use/settings/#audio). A named device that
is not connected plays through the system default until it is back.

## Audio layers

An **Audio layer** is sound with no picture. Placing an audio file in a composition makes
one. To take just the sound of an item that has a picture too, right-click the item in the
Project panel and choose **Add audio only**. **Layer ▸ New ▸ Audio**, `Ctrl+Alt+Shift+A`,
adds an empty one, which takes clips in the [Audio timeline](/panels/audio/#audio-timeline).

An Audio layer has no visibility switch.

With **Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Sound moves in whole frames** off, an Audio layer dragged by
its bar lands where the pointer is, between frames.

**Detach audio** puts a layer's sound on an Audio layer of its own, directly below, and
turns the original's audible switch off. It is on a layer's right-click menu in the
Timeline and in **Layer ▸ Audio**. A layer with no sound is **refused**, and the status
line says so.

## Volume and Pan

| Property | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Volume** | The layer's level in decibels. 0 dB leaves the sound as it is. The Audio panel's field runs from −60 to +12 dB. The Mixer's fader runs down to silence, which reads **-inf**. |
| **Pan** | Where the sound sits between left and right: −100 is full left, 0 is centre and 100 is full right. It reads **C**, **L 50** or **R 30**. |

Both take keyframes. A value changed while the property is animated lands on the keyframe
under the playhead, or makes one there.

- In the Timeline, twirl a layer open and then its **Audio** group for **Volume**.
- The [Audio panel](/panels/audio/#selected-layer) sets Volume, Pan and fades for the
  selected layer.
- The [Mixer](/panels/audio/#mixer) has a fader and a pan pot for every layer that makes sound,
  and the composition's master fader.

A layer's [audio effects](#audio-effects) run before its Volume and Pan.

## Waveforms

In the Timeline, the **Audio** group of a layer with sound holds a **Waveform** twirl,
which opens a lane drawing the layer's sound. The drawing is trimmed and retimed along with
the layer. Clips inside an open [Sequence layer](/use/sequence-layers/) draw theirs inside
their own box.

`L` opens the Audio group of the selected layers. Pressed twice within half a second it
opens the waveform as well, and a third press shuts the layer. **Reveal Volume** in
[Settings ▸ Shortcuts](/use/settings/) opens the Audio group alone, and has no key until it
is given one.

The chip on the Waveform row sets what the lane draws. Click it to step through the three.

| Chip | The lane draws |
| --- | --- |
| **Wave** | One waveform. |
| **Stack** | Three waveforms over one another: bass, middle and treble. |
| **Spectral** | A spectrogram. Time runs along the lane, frequency up it from 0 to 12 kHz, and brightness is the level. |

The chip's choice belongs to the layer and is not saved with the project.

**Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Waveforms** sets how waveforms are drawn.

| Setting | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| **Style** | **Plain** draws one wave. **Frequency** splits it into low, mid and high bands. A lane whose chip has not been clicked follows it. |
| **Anchor** | **Centre** draws the wave about the middle of the row. **Bottom** draws it up from the bottom. |

### The volume band

The line across a waveform lane is the layer's Volume, with a diamond on each keyframe. It
runs from −60 dB at the foot of the lane to +12 dB at the top, and is dragged, keyed and
right-clicked as a volume [envelope](/use/cutting/#envelopes) is.

Once a composition has been edited in the Audio timeline, its Audio layers fold away in the
Timeline under a [Sound mix](/panels/timeline/#sound-mix) row.

## Audio streams

A file can carry several **audio streams**, such as a screen recording with the game on
one and the microphone on another. A file placed in a composition plays every stream,
mixed. The Project panel's preview card states how many streams a file has.

Right-click a layer in the Timeline, or a clip in the Cut timeline or the Audio timeline.
The commands appear when the file has more than one stream.

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Audio streams** | Lists the file's streams by number, each with its name or its language where the file gives one. The layer plays the ticked ones, mixed, and one always stays ticked. With several layers or clips selected, every one takes the ticks, as one undo step. |
| **Split audio streams** | Gives each stream a row of its own. |
| **Merge audio streams** | Puts the streams back on one row, mixed. |

**Split audio streams** and **Merge audio streams** are also in **Layer ▸ Audio**, on any
layer. Where there is nothing to split or merge, the status line says so.

- A split Footage layer keeps its picture and its first stream. Each other stream becomes
  an Audio layer directly below, with the same span, Volume and effects.
- A split clip keeps its first stream. Each other stream becomes a
  [linked clip](/use/cutting/#linked-clips) on an audio-only Sequence layer.
- A split row is named after its stream where the file names it, and after its number
  otherwise.
- Merging removes the Audio layers of the same file that start at the same time, along
  with their own Volume keyframes and effects. Locked layers stay.
- Merging a layer that was never split sets it to every stream of its file.

**Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Several audio streams arrive as** sets what such a file
becomes when it is placed in a composition. **One layer, mixed** is the default.

| Choice | What is placed |
| --- | --- |
| **The first stream** | One layer playing the first stream. |
| **One layer, mixed** | One layer playing every stream. |
| **A layer each** | A layer for each stream. |

A layer in a project from Lumit 0.7.0 or earlier plays the first stream until it is set to
others.

The source view and the Project panel's preview follow the same setting. They play the
first stream, or every stream mixed for either of the other two choices.

## Scrubbing

Hold `Ctrl` and drag along the ruler of the Timeline, the Cut timeline or the Audio
timeline to hear the composition as the playhead moves. Each frame the playhead lands on
plays a twelfth of a second of the mix from that frame. `Ctrl`+click on the ruler plays the
frame under the pointer.

Scrubbing makes no sound during playback.

## Beat markers

Lumit can read a composition's audio and place a **beat marker** on each detected beat.

- **Composition ▸ Detect beats** listens to the composition's mix and replaces its beat
  markers. Other markers are kept. A sound layer's own menu has it too, and listens to that
  layer alone.
- **Composition ▸ Clear beat markers** removes all beat markers in the composition.
- The **Beats** section of the [Audio panel](/panels/audio/#beats) sets what is listened
  to, how keenly, over what range and at what tempo.

Detection runs in the background behind a progress card, and the status line says how many
markers were placed and at what tempo. Where a tempo was found, the ruler numbers the bars,
four beats to the bar.

A composition with nothing sounding is **refused**, and the status line says so.

## Audio effects

An audio effect is an entry in a layer's effect stack. **Effect ▸ Audio** adds one to the
selected layer, and [Effect controls](/panels/effect-controls/) lists a layer's audio
effects under an **Audio** heading at the foot of the stack. A clip can carry audio effects
of its own, added in the [Audio timeline](/panels/audio/#a-clips-header).

- A clip's audio effects run before its layer's, and both run before Volume and Pan.
- On a Sequence layer the layer's audio effects process each clip separately.
- On a Precomp layer they process the whole sound of the nested composition.

The built-in ones each have a page under [Effects ▸ Audio](/effects/#audio):
[Gain](/effects/audio/gain/), [Stereo width](/effects/audio/stereo-width/),
[Parametric EQ](/effects/audio/parametric-eq/), [Graphic EQ](/effects/audio/graphic-eq/),
[Compressor](/effects/audio/compressor/), [Limiter](/effects/audio/limiter/),
[Gate](/effects/audio/gate/), [Distortion](/effects/audio/distortion/),
[Delay](/effects/audio/delay/), [Reverb](/effects/audio/reverb/),
[Chorus](/effects/audio/chorus/), [Phaser](/effects/audio/phaser/),
[Tremolo](/effects/audio/tremolo/), [Vibrato](/effects/audio/vibrato/) and
[Wah](/effects/audio/wah/).

Installed CLAP and VST3 plugins are offered beside them. See
[Audio plugins](/use/audio-plugins/).

## Audio offset

**Settings ▸ Audio ▸ Audio offset** lines the picture up with sound that is heard late on
this machine, from −1000 to 1000 ms. Above zero holds the picture back; below zero holds
the sound back. It applies while playing, and not to export.
