A **Shape layer** holds vector shape groups with fills, strokes, and path operations.

<Shot file="shape-layer.png" caption="The contents of a shape layer in the Timeline." eager/>

## Draw a shape

1. Deselect all layers.
2. Pick a shape or pen tool.
3. Draw in the Viewer.

If a layer is selected the tools draw a [mask](/use/masks/) on it.

When using a shape tool while drawing, if you want to maintain the shape's proportions,
hold `Shift` while creating the shape.

## Fills and strokes

Each shape group carries its own fill and stroke, set from the toolbar when the shape
is drawn. The fill's colour is editable afterwards from the Timeline, on the group's
own **Fill** row.

## Gradient fills

A shape group's first rows are **Fill**, the colour inside its path, and **Gradient**,
which is Flat, Linear or Radial. Choose a ramp and its rows appear: the **Gradient colour**
it ends at, **Gradient stops**, an opacity for each end, and the two points that aim it.

Linear ramps along the line between the points; radial ramps outwards from the first,
with the second on the outer edge.

**Gradient start opacity** is how opaque the Fill end is and **Gradient end opacity** how
opaque the Gradient colour end is, as a percentage. The ramp fades evenly between them, and
an end at 0 is clear.

The points and the opacities are animatable; the colours are not.

### Gradient stops

Click the ramp on the **Gradient stops** row to open its editor. The handles at either end
are the Fill and the Gradient colour.

- Click the ramp to add a stop between them.
- Drag a stop to move it. Drag it away from the ramp, or press **Remove**, to take it off.
- **Colour**, **Opacity** and **Position** set the selected stop.

Each stop also has two rows in the Timeline, **Stop 1 position** and **Stop 1 opacity** for
the first, and both are animatable. Editing a keyed stop in the editor sets a keyframe at the
playhead.

Two stops at the same position make a hard edge. A gradient holds up to 16 stops between
its ends.

## Combine two shapes

Every shape group after the first in the list carries a **Combine** row, saying how it joins
the one **above** it: **Apart**, **Union**, **Subtract**, **Intersect** or **Exclude**. Apart
is the default, and draws it on its own.

A run of shapes all set to something other than Apart folds top to bottom.

**The run wears the first item's paint and modifiers.** The ones after it lend their path
and nothing else, so their own fill, stroke and dash rows are left off the panel.

A shape fills the same way after a combine as before it.

## Animate the shape itself

A shape group's **Path** row carries the shape, and its stopwatch animates it. Key one path,
move the playhead, drag a vertex, and the shape morphs between the two.

Vertices are paired by **order**, not by feature; where one path has fewer points, it is
subdivided to match the denser one first. Whether a path is open or closed is held across
the morph.

:::note[Limitation]
A shape row's keyframes appear as diamonds on its Timeline lane but have no curve in the
[graph editor](/panels/graph-editor/) yet. That is true of the trim, dash and repeater rows
as well as of the path.
:::

## Shape operations

Right-click a shape group in the Timeline for **Add** and **Remove**, which list the
operations it can be given and the ones it has. An operation's rows appear under the group
once it is added. Every row is animatable.

They apply in a fixed order: smooth, twist, deform, then offset, trim and the repeater. The
stroke taper narrows the outline last.

### Smooth

**Smooth** rounds off the whole path, jags and corners alike. Its one row, also **Smooth**,
is how far along the path each point is averaged with its neighbours, in pixels. 0 leaves
the path alone.

The path never bulges past its own points; a large smooth shrinks it a little instead. An
open path's ends stay where they are.

### Twist

**Twist** turns the shape about an axis and flattens it, as if seen side-on. One end of the
shape stays put and the other turns by the angle, so a circle becomes a figure eight at
180°.

- **Twist angle**: how far the far end turns, in degrees. 0 leaves the shape alone, and a
  new twist starts at 10°.
- **Twist rotation**: how far the twisted shape is spun about the axis, in degrees. A full
  turn loops.
- **Twist axis**: the direction of the axis, in degrees.
- **Twist centre x** and **y**: the point the axis passes through. It starts in the middle of
  the shape.

### Deform

**Deform** moves the outline with a smooth noise field. The **Deform** row picks
**Displace**, which pushes the outline in and out along itself, or **Scale**, which scales
each point's distance from the middle of the shape.

- **Deform amount**: how far, in pixels for Displace and as a percentage for Scale.
- **Deform scale**: the size of the noise's features, in pixels.
- **Deform evolution**: moves through the noise, in degrees. Key it to animate the outline.
- **Deform seed**: picks a different noise.
- **Deform detail**: how often the noise is read along the path, per feature. Lower values
  bend the outline more broadly.

### Stroke taper

**Stroke taper** narrows a shape's outline towards its ends, like a brush stroke. It is
offered only for a shape with a stroke.

- **Taper scope**: **Each path** tapers every path of the shape on its own; **Whole shape**
  runs one taper along all of them in order.
- **Start taper** and **End taper**: how much of the width is gone at each end, as a
  percentage. 100 comes to a point.
- **Start length** and **End length**: how far along the path each narrowing runs, as a
  percentage of its length. When the two add up to more than 100 they are shrunk together.
- **Taper ease**: how rounded the narrowing is. 0 is a straight ramp.

The taper follows the trimmed path, and each dash takes the width the outline has where it
falls.

## Trim paths

Twirl a shape group open in the Timeline and it carries **Trim start**, **Trim end**
and **Trim offset**. Start and end are a percentage of the path's own length; offset
slides that piece along the path in degrees, and 360 is one full lap of a closed shape.

The fill is trimmed with the outline, and an end below the start draws nothing.

## Dashed outlines

A shape with a stroke carries **Dash**, **Gap** and **Dash offset** rows, in pixels.
Dash is the length of each mark, Gap the space after it, and the pair repeats along
the path. Both start at zero — a solid outline. Key the offset and the dashes move along
the path.

## Offset paths

The first row under a shape group is **Offset path**, one number in pixels. Positive
pushes the outline outwards; negative pulls it in. Corners come out round.

Pull the outline in by more than the shape is thick somewhere and it can cross itself,
leaving a small loop.

## The repeater

Every shape group carries a **Copies** row. Turn it above one and the group is drawn
that many times, each copy one more step along than the last, and nine more rows
appear to describe the step: **Repeater position x** and **y**, **Repeater rotation**,
**Repeater scale**, the **Repeater anchor** the turn and the scale happen about, and
**Start opacity** and **End opacity**, which the copies fade evenly between.

**Copy offset** says which copy the original art is, so a negative number puts copies
behind it. A copy is a scaled drawing, not a scaled path — halve the scale and the outline
and the dashes halve with it.

The layer's box grows to hold the copies, and the layer's position stays pinned to that
box's corner. The count stops at 100 copies.
