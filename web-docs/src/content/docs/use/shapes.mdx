A **Shape layer** holds vector shape groups with fills, strokes, and path operations.

<Shot file="shape-layer.png" caption="The contents of a shape layer in the Timeline." eager/>

## Draw a shape

1. Deselect all layers.
2. Pick a shape or pen tool.
3. Draw in the Viewer.

If a layer is selected the tools draw a [mask](/use/masks/) on it.

When using a shape tool while drawing, if you want to maintain the shapes proportions 
hold `shift` while creating the shape.

## Fills and strokes

Each shape group carries its own fill and stroke, set from the toolbar when the shape
is drawn. The fill's colour is editable afterwards from the Timeline, on the group's
own **Fill** row.

## Gradient fills

A shape group's first rows are **Fill**, the colour inside its path, and **Gradient**,
which is Flat, Linear or Radial. Choose a ramp and three more rows appear: the
**Gradient colour** it ends at, and the two points that aim it.

Linear ramps along the line between the points; radial ramps outwards from the first,
with the second on the outer edge. Switching a ramp on aims it at the shape's own box
to start with, so there is always something to see. The points are animatable, so a
gradient can sweep; the two colours are not, and there are two of them rather than a
stop list.

## Combine two shapes

Every shape group after the first in the list carries a **Combine** row, saying how it joins
the one **above** it: **Apart**, **Union**, **Subtract**, **Intersect** or **Exclude**. Apart
is the default, and draws it on its own.

A run of shapes all set to something other than Apart folds top to bottom, so three items
read as the first joined to the second and that result joined to the third. The first item of
a run has nothing in front of it and draws alone.

**The run wears the first item's paint and modifiers** — it has to wear somebody's, and the
first item is the shape you started with. The ones after it lend their path and nothing else,
so their own fill, stroke and dash rows are left off the panel.

A shape fills the same way after a combine as before it, so a five-pointed star drawn in one
stroke keeps its hollow middle rather than filling in when you union something to it.

## Animate the shape itself

A shape group's **Path** row carries the shape, and its stopwatch animates it. Key one path,
move the playhead, drag a vertex, and the shape morphs between the two.

Vertices are paired by **order**, not by feature. Equal point counts run one for one; where
one path has fewer points, it is cut into as many pieces as the denser one has first, so
nothing bulges or flattens on the way. A triangle morphing into a square pairs point one with
point one and the flat side grows wherever that puts it — rotating a path's start point is
the lever for making it grow somewhere else.

Whether a path is open or closed is held across the morph rather than tweened.

:::note[Limitation]
A shape row's keyframes appear as diamonds on its Timeline lane but have no curve in the
[graph editor](/panels/graph-editor/) yet. That is true of the trim, dash and repeater rows
as well as of the path.
:::

## Trim paths

Twirl a shape group open in the Timeline and it carries **Trim start**, **Trim end**
and **Trim offset**. Start and end are a percentage of the path's own length, so
trimming a circle to 0–50% leaves half of it; offset slides that piece along the
path in degrees, and 360 is one full lap of a closed shape.

Key **Trim end** from 0 to 100 and the shape draws itself on. The fill is trimmed
with the outline — a half-trimmed circle fills as a half circle — and an end below
the start draws nothing, which is what the first frame of a write-on looks like.

## Dashed outlines

A shape with a stroke carries **Dash**, **Gap** and **Dash offset** rows, in pixels.
Dash is the length of each mark, Gap the space after it, and the pair repeats along
the path. Both start at zero — a solid outline — so typing a Dash is what turns the
dashes on. Key the offset and the dashes march along the path.

## Offset paths

The first row under a shape group is **Offset path**, one number in pixels. Positive
pushes the outline outwards and the shape gets fatter; negative pulls it in. Corners
come out round, and the shape keeps its character — unlike scaling, which changes the
proportions of everything at once.

Pull the outline in by more than the shape is thick somewhere and it can cross itself,
leaving a small loop. The fill rule hides most of that; where it shows, use a smaller
number.

## The repeater

Every shape group carries a **Copies** row. Turn it above one and the group is drawn
that many times, each copy one more step along than the last, and nine more rows
appear to describe the step: **Repeater position x** and **y**, **Repeater rotation**,
**Repeater scale**, the **Repeater anchor** the turn and the scale happen about, and
**Start opacity** and **End opacity**, which the copies fade evenly between.

A position step gives a row of ticks; a rotation step about an anchor gives a clock
face; both together with a scale under 100% gives a spiral. **Copy offset** says which
copy the original art is, so a negative number puts copies behind it. A copy is a
scaled drawing, not a scaled path — halve the scale and the outline and the dashes
halve with it.

The layer's box grows to hold the copies, and the layer's position stays pinned to
that box's corner, so a repeater keyed to grow upwards or leftwards moves the art as
it plays. Step down and to the right, or key the layer's position to match. The count
stops at 100 copies.
