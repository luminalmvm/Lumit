Open the settings window from **Edit ▸ Settings…**.

<Shot file="settings.png" caption="The settings window, on the Appearance page." eager/>

The search box in the title strip finds a setting by name on any page, and lists what it
finds under the page names. On the Shortcuts page it searches the shortcuts instead.

## General

**Language**, which follows the system unless you choose otherwise. Whether the welcome
screen appears on launch. **New project template**, a project that every new one starts as
a copy of. And **Reset workspace**, which puts the arrangement you are on back to how it
shipped.

**Keep running in the background**: closing the window leaves Lumit in the system tray, or
the menu bar on macOS, where paired phones still see it. **Open Lumit** on the tray icon's
menu brings the window back and **Quit Lumit** ends Lumit. Opening Lumit again also brings
the window back, which is the only way on a desktop with no tray, such as GNOME as it
ships. **Open Lumit when you sign in** appears once that is on, and starts Lumit in the tray
when you sign in to the computer.

Beneath those, whether Lumit checks for updates, and what it found last time it checked.

## Account

The profiles on this machine, the Lumit account the one in use is signed in to, and what
of it is synced. See [Profiles and accounts](/use/profiles/).

## Appearance

- **Theme**: seven built-in schemes grouped dark and light, plus your own. **Customise…**
  opens the theme editor; themes can be exported and imported. **Accent** and **Shape** tune
  whichever scheme is in force. Shape is the interface style: **Studio**, **Desk** or
  **Lantern**. **Label case** sets how panel titles, headings and other labels are cased:
  **Style's choice**, **All caps**, **First letter** or **Lower case**. Left to the style,
  Studio writes the first letter as a capital, Desk is lower case and Lantern is all caps.
- **Interface**: [**Scale**](/use/workspaces/#how-large-everything-draws), 75 % to 200 %.
  **Tooltips**, on or off. **Motion**, for how much controls animate. **Compact**, which
  tightens the row spacing throughout. And **labels**, deciding whether the bars carry
  words, icons, or icons everywhere.
- **Viewer**: whether the scopes and an effect's own graphs take the theme's colours or their
  own, and whether the surround behind the picture is neutral or themed.
- **Panels**: whether footage rows in the Project panel carry thumbnails, whether layer names
  are drawn on the lane bars, whether [markers](/use/markers/) draw lines down the rows, and
  whether a minimap of the bars is drawn under the lanes. **Wires** sets how each wire in a
  node graph runs between its two sockets: **Curved**, **Angled**, which turns only at 45 or
  90 degrees, or **Hanging**, which droops like a slack cable.

## Timeline

Whether [Retime](/use/retime/) opens to the Value or the Speed lens and
whether it reads in seconds, whether video is placed in a [Sequence
layer](/use/sequence-layers/) automatically, where a video's sound goes in the
[Cut timeline](/use/cutting/), whether its tracks stand in any order, whether pasted layers
keep their original time rather than pasting at the playhead, and whether the playhead stays
where playback stopped.

Then the panel habits: whether Transform appears in Effect controls, whether Effect controls
shows its keyframes beside the parameters, whether an ease opens in a popup or the
[Easing panel](/use/workspaces/), and whether sound moves in whole frames.

Last, **Node graphs**: whether a right-click, `Tab` or `Shift+A` on a node graph canvas opens
[node search](/use/nodes/). Each is on by default and can be turned off on its own.

## Viewer

Whether the picture is smoothed when zoomed in, and whether the tone-map button appears on
the Viewer bar, which can be used to try and help see details in area's of brightness greater than one.

## Audio

The output device. Follows the system unless you name one.

## Autosave

How often a spare copy is written, and how many are kept. Setting the interval to nothing
turns autosave off.

## Export

What the export dialogue opens on: a preset, a filename template, and whether it asks for a
destination each time or uses a folder you name.

## Preview and cache

- **Playback**: every frame, or adaptive resolution.
- **Hardware video decoding**: on by default. If importing video freezes the computer,
  turn it off and open the project again.
- **Cache budgets** for all three tiers: rendered frames in memory, preview frames on the
  graphics card, and frames parked on disk. The disk cache's location is set here too, per
  machine or per project.

## Shortcuts

Every command and its shortcut. Rebind by clicking one; conflicts are flagged. Keymaps export
and import.

- **Preset** loads a whole keymap: Lumit's own, **After Effects** or **Vegas**. The Vegas
  one puts split on `S`, snapping on `F8`, zoom on `↑` and `↓`, and the Cut timeline's
  switches on `Ctrl`+`L`, `Ctrl`+`Shift`+`X` and `Alt`+`F8`.
- **After Effects shortcuts ▸ Import…** reads a shortcut file saved by After Effects, the
  `.txt` in its `aeks` folder. Commands Lumit has no match for are skipped.
- **Keyboard** shows which keys have a shortcut. A filled key works anywhere and an outlined
  key works in one panel. Click **Ctrl**, **Alt** or **Shift** on it to see the keys they
  change, and point at a key to read what it does.

**Dragging** sets the modifier that breaks or joins handles in the
[graph editor](/use/graph-editor/): `Alt` or `Ctrl`.
