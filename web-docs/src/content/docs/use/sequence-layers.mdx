A **Sequence layer** holds a run of **clips** cut back-to-back on one row.

<Shot file="sequence-layer.png" caption="A Sequence layer: clips cut back-to-back on one row." eager/>

**Clip** is only used in reference to an item within a Sequence layer. Everywhere else, the word
is [layer](/use/layers/).

## Why use one

A Sequence layer suits cutting more than compositing. One row holds a whole run of shots,
so the timeline remains clean.

## Make one

- **Layer ▸ New ▸ Sequence**, `Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S`, adds an empty Sequence layer.
- **Convert to sequence layer**, on a Footage layer's right-click menu and in the **Layer**
  menu, turns the layer into a Sequence layer holding one clip of what it showed. A retimed
  layer is **refused**.
- **Convert to footage layer** turns a Sequence layer of one clip back. A layer of several
  clips, or one whose clip plays a composition, is **refused**.
- With **Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Video arrives as a Sequence layer** on, footage with a
  moving picture is placed as a Sequence layer of one clip. A still is placed as a Footage
  layer.
- Footage placed in the [Cut workspace](/use/cutting/) lands on Sequence layers.

An audio-only Sequence layer holds sound clips. See the
[Audio timeline](/panels/audio/#audio-timeline).

## Each clip carries its own

- Source: a footage item or a composition.
- Trim, in and out.
- [Retime](/use/retime/).
- A fade in and a fade out.
- An effect stack, and on a picture clip a blend mode.

Two clips may overlap, never three. The overlap is a dissolve on a picture layer and a
crossfade on an audio-only one. A gap between clips is transparent, or silent.

## Edit clips

To edit a Sequence layer's clips or their speed, double-click the Sequence layer in the
Timeline. The row opens in place, with the clips across the top and their speed envelope
below. Double-click the clips to shut it.

- Drag a clip's edge to trim it.
- Drag a clip along the row to move it. It overwrites what it lands on.
- With the [Razor](/panels/toolbar/) armed, click a clip to cut it there. `Shift`+click
  cuts every layer at that frame. A cut between two clips is an **edit point**.
- Right-click a clip for the menu it has in the [Cut timeline](/use/cutting/), with **Copy
  the layer's cuts** and **Paste the layer's cuts** for its [shape](#sequence-shapes).
- Right-click the row's empty ground for **Paste**, **Close gap**, the same two rows and
  **Close Sequence view**.
- `Ctrl+,` and `Ctrl+.` move the playhead to the previous and the next edit point.

Under the clips is the **speed envelope**, a line per clip in per cent. See
[Speed on a clip](/use/retime/#speed-on-a-clip). Drag the divider above it to make it
taller or shorter. Double-click the divider, or right-click it for **Reset height**, to put
it back.

Editing a clip's speed never moves its start or changes its length.

The razor cuts through an eased speed ramp. What it refuses is a cut on a
clip's own ends, or a retime driven by an expression.

## Sequence shapes

A **shape** is where a Sequence layer's cuts fall and how each clip is retimed, without the
footage. Pasted onto another Sequence layer it cuts and retimes that layer to match, and
the layer keeps its own footage.

- **Copy sequence shape** and **Paste sequence shape** are on a Sequence layer's right-click
  menu in the Timeline. **Copy the layer's cuts** and **Paste the layer's cuts**, in the
  open row, are the same commands.
- The layer plays the source of its first clip throughout.
- A shape longer than the layer it is pasted onto is cut off at the layer's end.
- The copied shape is kept until Lumit closes.

## What applies to the whole layer

Masks, transforms and switches on a Sequence layer apply to its **whole output**, once
the clips have been retimed. So do the layer's own effects.

A clip also carries an effect stack and a blend mode of its own, set in the
[Cut workspace](/use/cutting/#effects-and-blend-mode-on-a-clip).

**Layer ▸ Enable Retime** is disabled on a Sequence layer, where it reads *Retime (open the
layer to ramp its clips)*.
