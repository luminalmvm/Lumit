An **effect** is one stage in a layer's **effect stack**. The stack composites top-to-bottom
applying in that order.

<Shot file="effect-menu.png" caption="The Effect menu." eager/>

## Apply an effect

1. Select a layer.
2. Choose **Effect**, then a category, then the effect.

You can also choose effects via the [Effects console](/use/fx-console/), or dragged onto 
a layer from the Effects & Presets panel. With nothing selected, the whole menu is disabled.

## Edit the parameters

Select the layer and open the **Effect Controls** panel. Every parameter there can be
animated: turn on its stopwatch to begin. See [Keyframes](/use/keyframes/).

## Order matters

Effects run in stack order. A exposure ahead of a glow does not look like a glow ahead of a
exposure. Drag an effect up or down the stack to reorder it.

## Apply one effect to many layers

Effects on **adjustment layers** apply to the composite of everything
below them. See [Layers](/use/layers/).

## Every effect has a Matte

Every effect's controls has the following: a layer picker labelled **Matte**, with 
an **Invert** checkbox beside it. Select a layer and that layer's brightness 
decides how much of the effect each pixel gets. White applies the effect in full and 
black gets none. Invert reads the same picture the other way round. The row is not set 
by default, and most effects work fine without it.

A matte is different from a mask on the effect. A mask can hide what an effect does, 
whereas a matte drives the effect itself. For a handful of effects it applies more than
just strength, and their own effect pages explain this in the parameter table:

- **Gaussian blur** narrows its radius.
- **Turbulent displace** scales its turbulence.
- **Glow** gates pixels used to find area's of glow.
- **Depth of field** reads it as a depth pass.
- **Lens flare** reads it as where to find its light sources.
- **Set matte** reads it as the alpha itself, which is the whole effect.

Lumit renders the matte layer on its own at the composition's size. Beside the picker, 
the **Layer source** dropdown has the option to read that layer raw, with its masks, 
or with its effects applied. If the same layer the effect is *on*, it reads that layer's 
own picture at that point in the stack.

## The built-in roster

Each effect has its own page under [Effects](/effects/), with an example of what it does 
and a table with every parameter and its range, default and units. 

| Category | What the family is for |
| --- | --- |
| [Blur & sharpen](/effects/blur-sharpen/) | Softening and detail lift. |
| [Colour](/effects/colour/) | Grading: exposure, balance, contrast, and colour altering. |
| [Distortion](/effects/distortion/) | Warp pixels. |
| [Generate](/effects/generate/) | Effects that make a picture instead of changing one. |
| [Stylise](/effects/stylise/) | Glow, flare, relief and blocks. |
| [Temporal](/effects/temporal/) | Effects that read frames at other times, or change which frame is drawn. |
| [Transition](/effects/transition/) | Wipes, driven by a completion parameter you keyframe. |
| [Utility](/effects/utility/) | Keying, mattes, transform, and other useful effects. |

### The three kinds of motion blur

These are easy to confuse:

| What | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motion blur **switch** | A layer switch | Smears one layer based on its transforms. |
| **Fast motion blur** effect | An effect | Calculates estimated motion vectors for what it's applied on, and generates motion blur based on that. |
| **Motion blur** effect | An effect | Re-renders the scene below at sub-frame times and averages out to calculate almost true to life motion blur. |
