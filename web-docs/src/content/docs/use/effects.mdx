An **effect** is one stage in a layer's **effect stack**. The stack composites top to bottom,
applying in that order.

<Shot file="effect-menu.png" caption="The Effect menu." eager/>

## Apply an effect

1. Select a layer.
2. Choose **Effect**, then a category, then the effect.

An effect is also applied from:

- **Effect ▸** on a layer's right-click menu in the Timeline.
- **Add effect** in the header of [Effect controls](/panels/effect-controls/), which lists
  the same categories.
- The [Effects console](/use/fx-console/), `Ctrl+Space`.
- [Effects & presets](/panels/effects-presets/), by a double-click, or by a drag onto a
  layer in the Timeline, onto Effect controls, onto a clip in the Cut timeline or onto the
  [node graph](/use/nodes/).
- The command palette, where every effect is a row.

With nothing selected, the whole Effect menu is disabled, and the palette's effect rows
read **Select a layer first**.

The first row of the Effect menu applies the last effect again (`Ctrl`+`Alt`+`Shift`+`E`),
and **Remove all effects** (`Ctrl`+`Shift`+`E`) clears the selected layers' stacks.
**Learn noise** (`Alt`+`L`) and **Only what is removed** (`Alt`+`N`) act on
[Denoise](/effects/blur-sharpen/denoise/#commands) and the sound repair effects.

A [driver](/use/nodes/#drivers) chosen from any of these lands on the layer's node graph,
not in its stack. The **Drivers** menu lists the drivers alone and puts one on the first
selected layer's graph. The [Compositing](/effects/#compositing) boxes and a bare
**Node graph** are refused on a stack.

A clip on a Sequence layer carries an effect stack of its own. See
[Cutting](/use/cutting/#effects-and-blend-mode-on-a-clip). A layer group's header carries
one too, shown in Effect controls while the header is selected. A driver is refused on a
clip and on a group header.

Effects on a **null layer** change no picture. Their parameters still animate and can be
read by [expressions](/use/expressions/).

### Several layers

| Applied from | Goes on |
| --- | --- |
| The Effect menu, a layer's right-click menu, the command palette, a double-click in Effects & presets | Every selected layer, as one undo step. |
| The Effects console | The primary layer. |
| **Add effect** in Effect controls | The layer the panel is showing. |
| A drag | The layer or clip it is dropped on. |

## Edit the parameters

Select the layer and open the **Effect Controls** panel, `F3`. Every parameter there can
be animated: turn on its stopwatch to begin. See
[Effect Controls](/panels/effect-controls/) and [Keyframes](/use/keyframes/).

Right-click a parameter and choose **Drive with** to wire a driver onto it:

| Row | Wires |
| --- | --- |
| **Wiggle** | A [Wiggle](/effects/drivers/wiggle/), on a number. On a transform row it writes an expression. |
| **Audio level ▸**, **Beat trigger ▸** | An [Audio level](/effects/drivers/audio-level/) or a [Beat trigger](/effects/drivers/beat-trigger/), on a number, listening to **Whole composition** or to one layer that carries sound. |
| **Colour cycle** | A [Colour cycle](/effects/drivers/colour-cycle/), on a colour. |
| **Expression…** | An [Expression](/effects/drivers/expression/) box, and opens its text. |
| **Remove driver** | Takes the wire and its boxes away. Listed while a wire feeds the row. |

Each is one undo step. The row then reads **driven**, and a click on that word opens the
driver in the Node panel. A driver that cannot be wired says so on the bottom strip. An
effect on a clip or a group header has no **Drive with**.

### Points on the picture

With an effect selected, each of its points wears a mark on the picture in the Viewer.
With the Selection tool a mark is dragged, and `Shift` holds the drag to one axis. A point
worked out by an expression or fed by a wire does not move. Right-click a mark for
**Move to centre**, **Reset point** and **Reveal in Effect controls**.

## Order matters

Effects run in stack order. An exposure ahead of a glow does not look like a glow ahead of an
exposure. Drag an effect's heading up or down the stack to reorder it, or right-click the
heading for **Move ▸**, which holds **Move up**, **Move to top**, **Move down** and
**Move to bottom**. With several effects picked, the menu moves them together.

## Switch off, reset, rename and remove

On an effect's heading in Effect controls:

- **The tick** switches the effect off and on. Off, the picture passes through it and its
  values are kept. Dragging from one tick across others sets each to match the first.
- **Reset** puts every parameter back to its default and removes its keyframes, as one
  undo step. The arrow at the end of a row resets that parameter alone.
- **Rename** on the right-click menu, or `Enter` with one effect picked, names the effect.
  `Escape` leaves the name as it was.
- **×**, **Delete** on the right-click menu, or `Delete` removes the picked effects.
- **Bypass** on the right-click menu is the tick. `Ctrl+D`, or **Duplicate**, copies the
  picked effects, each copy right after its original.

## Copy and paste

Click an effect's heading to pick it. `Ctrl`-click toggles one, `Shift`-click picks the run
between, and `Ctrl+A` with the panel focused picks the whole stack. A click on empty panel
clears the pick.

- `Ctrl+C`, or **Copy** on the heading's right-click menu, copies the picked effects with
  their values and keyframes, in stack order. **Cut** copies and removes them. An effect's
  row in the Timeline has the same menu.
- `Ctrl+V` adds them to the end of the stack of the layer Effect controls is showing.
  **Paste** on a heading's menu puts them right below that effect. The earliest copied
  keyframe lands on the playhead and the rest keep their spacing.

Copied effects are the same document as a [preset](/use/presets/).

## Past the layer's edges

A layer's picture is the size of the layer, and a shape or text layer's is the size of its
art. Some effects reach past it, and the layer grows to hold them:

- **Drop shadow**, always.
- **Gaussian blur**, **Channel blur**, **Directional blur** and **Glow**, with **Repeat edge
  pixels** off. Added to a shape or text layer, they start with it off.
- **Tile**, with an output larger than the layer.

An effect after one of these works on the grown layer. Its points and mask paths stay
where they were on the layer. A **Matte** is black past the layer's edges. On an
adjustment layer nothing reaches past the frame.

## Adjustment layers

Effects on **adjustment layers** apply to the composite of everything
below them. See [Layers](/use/layers/).

## Matte

A picture effect's controls have a layer picker labelled **Matte**, with a **Channel**
dropdown and an **Invert** checkbox beside it. Audio effects, drivers and controls have
none, and an effect from an extension has **Mix** alone. It sits with the **Mix** row under
**Compositing** at the foot of the effect. Channel picks what is read from the matte
layer: its brightness by default, or its alpha or one of the colour channels. Select a
layer and that layer's brightness decides how much of the effect each pixel gets. White
applies the effect in full and black gets none. Invert reads the same picture the other
way round.

**Compositing** is out of the way until the effect's heading menu opens it. Once
something in it is off its default it stays in the list, and shut it reads the mix, the
blend and the matte layer.

<Shot file="effect-compositing.png" caption="Compositing shut on one effect, reading its mix, and open on the next." />

A matte is different from a mask on the effect. A mask can hide what an effect does,
whereas a matte drives the effect itself. For a handful of effects it applies more than
strength:

- **Gaussian blur** narrows its radius.
- **Turbulent displace** scales its turbulence.
- **Glow** gates pixels used to find areas of glow.
- **Denoise** scales how much noise it takes.
- **Depth of field** reads it as a depth pass.
- **Lens flare** reads it as where to find its light sources.
- **Set matte** reads it as the alpha itself, which is the whole effect.

Lumit renders the matte layer on its own and reads it where it sits in the
composition. If it is the same layer the effect is *on*, it reads that layer's own picture
at that point in the stack.

## The built-in roster

Each effect has its own page under [Effects](/effects/), with a table of every parameter
and its range, default and units.

| Category | What the family is for |
| --- | --- |
| [Blur & sharpen](/effects/#blur--sharpen) | Softening and detail lift. |
| [Colour](/effects/#colour) | Grading: exposure, balance, contrast, and colour altering. |
| [Distortion](/effects/#distortion) | Warp pixels. |
| [Generate](/effects/#generate) | Effects that make a picture instead of changing one. |
| [Points](/effects/#points) | Effects that make, change and draw [points](/use/nodes/#mattes-and-points). |
| [Stylise](/effects/#stylise) | Glow, flare, relief and blocks. |
| [Temporal](/effects/#temporal) | Effects that read frames at other times, or change which frame is drawn. |
| [Transition](/effects/#transition) | Wipes, driven by a completion parameter you keyframe. |
| [Utility](/effects/#utility) | Keying, mattes, transform, and other useful effects. |
| [Audio](/effects/#audio) | Effects that change a layer's sound. They draw nothing. |
| [Compositing](/effects/#compositing) | Merge, Switch, Time offset, Split channels, Combine channels, Repeat start and Repeat end. They work only inside a [node graph composition](/use/node-graphs/), and the Effect menu never offers them. |
| [Controls](/effects/#controls) | Values for [expressions](/use/expressions/) to read. |
| [Drivers](/effects/#drivers) | Values other effects follow, wired on a graph. The Effect menu files them under Controls. |
| [Siceliot](/effects/#siceliot) | Drivers that give each copy [Clone to points](/effects/points/clone-to-points/) stamps its own value. |

### The three kinds of motion blur

These are easy to confuse:

| What | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motion blur **switch** | A layer switch | Smears one layer based on its transforms. |
| **Motion blur** effect | An effect | Calculates estimated motion vectors for what it's applied on, and generates motion blur based on that. |
| **Accumulation motion blur** effect | An effect | Re-renders the scene below at sub-frame times and averages out to calculate almost true to life motion blur. Slow, as every sample is a full render. |
