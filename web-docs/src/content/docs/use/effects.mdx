An **effect** is one operation in a layer's **effect stack**. The stack runs
top-to-bottom.

<Shot file="effect-menu.png" caption="The Effect menu, category by category." />

## Apply an effect

1. Select one or more layers.
2. Choose **Effect**, then a category, then the effect.

The **Effect** menu applies to every selected layer at once. With nothing selected, the
whole menu is disabled.

You can also drag an effect from the Effects & Presets panel onto a layer.

## Edit the parameters

Select the layer and open the **Effect Controls** panel. Every parameter can be
animated. Turn on a stopwatch to start. See [Keyframes](/use/keyframes/).

## Order matters

Effects run in stack order. A blur before a glow does not look like a glow before a
blur. Drag an effect up or down the stack to reorder it.

## Apply one effect to many layers

Put the effect on an **adjustment layer**. It then applies to the composite of
everything below it. See [Layers](/use/layers/).

## Every effect has a Matte

At the bottom of every effect's controls sits one row in the same place on all of
them: a layer picker labelled **Matte**, with an **Invert** checkbox beside it.
Point it at a layer and that layer's brightness decides how much of the effect
each pixel gets - white in full, black not at all, grey part way. Invert reads it
the other way round. Leave it unset, which is the default, and nothing extra
happens or costs anything.

This is not the same as masking the effect. A mask hides what an effect did; a
matte drives the effect itself, and a handful of effects mean something deeper by
it than strength. Their own pages say so in the parameter table:

- **Gaussian blur** narrows its radius, so grey is genuinely less soft rather
  than a wide blur faded back.
- **Turbulent displace** scales its push, so grey is warped a little rather than
  fully warped and shown faintly.
- **Glow** gates which pixels may seed the halo, so light still spills outward
  from them.
- **Depth of field** reads it as a depth pass, so mid-grey can be perfectly sharp.
- **Lens flare** reads it as where to find its light sources.
- **Set matte** reads it as the alpha itself, which is the whole effect.

The matte layer is rendered on its own at this composition's size, so nothing
needs aligning, and the **Layer source** dropdown beside the picker decides
whether you read that layer raw, with its masks, or with its own effects applied.
Point the row at the layer the effect is *on* and it reads that layer's own
picture at that point in the stack.

## The built-in roster

Fifty-three effects ship today. Each has its own page under
[Effects](/effects/), with a full parameter table.

**Blur and sharpen** - Channel blur, Depth of field, Directional blur, Gaussian
blur, Radial blur, Sharpen, Unsharp mask.

**Colour** - Brightness, Colour balance, Contrast, Curves, Exposure, Gamma, Hue
and saturation, Hue shift, Invert, Levels, LUT, Saturation, Temperature, Tint,
Vibrancy, Vignette.

**Distortion** - Block glitch, Chromatic aberration, Datamosh, Lens distort,
Mirror, Offset, RGB split, Scanlines, Shake, Tile, Turbulent displace.

**Generate** - Fill, Fractal noise, Gradient, Noise.

**Stylise** - Drop shadow, Flash, Glow, Lens flare, Light wrap, Sprite flare.

**Temporal** - Echo, Fast motion blur, Motion blur, Posterize time.

**Transition** - Linear wipe, Radial wipe.

**Utility** - Matte key, Set matte, Transform.

**Matte key** is Lumit's keyer: pull a greenscreen or a chosen colour to
transparency, with threshold and softness controls.

### The three kinds of motion blur

They are easy to confuse:

| What | Where | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motion blur **switch** | A layer switch | Smears one layer along its own transform. |
| **Motion blur** effect | The effect stack | Re-renders the scene below at sub-frame times and averages. |
| **Fast motion blur** effect | The effect stack | Smears motion already inside the footage, in one pass. |
