**Retime** maps a layer's time to its source's time. It is how Lumit plays footage at another
speed, ramps between speeds, holds a frame or runs backwards.

<Clip name="retime-envelope" caption="A point added to a clip's speed envelope, and the clip's end dragged down to 25%."/>

## Ways to change speed

| Way | Where | Works on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [**Speed envelope**](#speed-on-a-clip) | Under a Sequence layer's clips | Clips |
| [**Speed…**](#speed-on-a-clip) | A clip's right-click menu | Clips |
| [**Retime**](#retime-on-a-layer) | **Layer ▸ Enable Retime**, `Ctrl+Alt+T` | Footage and Precomp layers |
| [**Stretch…**](#stretch) | A layer's right-click menu in the Timeline, or `Shift`+drag either end | Footage and Precomp layers |
| [**Freeze frame at playhead**](#freeze-a-frame) | A layer's right-click menu in the Timeline | Footage and Precomp layers |
| [**Interpret footage…**](#interpret-footage) | The Project panel's right-click menu | Footage items |
| [**Posterize time**](/effects/temporal/posterize-time/) | **Effect ▸ Temporal** | Any layer |

The **Retiming** workspace puts the Easing panel beside the Viewer, with the Timeline across
the bottom.

## Speed on a clip

Each clip in a [Sequence layer](/use/sequence-layers/) has its own speed. Double-click the
Sequence layer's row in the Timeline to open it. Under the clips is the **speed envelope**: a
line per clip, in per cent.

- Drag a line up or down to change the speed. On a clip that has never been retimed the
  whole line moves as one.
- Double-click a line, or `Ctrl`+click it, to add a point. It takes the speed already there.
- Drag a point sideways to move it in time, in whole frames. A clip's first and last points
  stay on its ends.
- Hold `Shift` or `Alt` while dragging to move a point in time only.
- Hold `Ctrl` while dragging to change the speed a tenth as fast, with the point held at its
  time. `Ctrl` also suspends the snap to **Every frame**.
- `Alt`+click a point to remove it. A clip keeps at least two.
- Double-click a point to put it back to 100%.
- Drag across empty space in the strip to select points, with `Shift` to add to them.
  Selected points move together, across clips too.
- Drag the divider above the strip to make it taller.

| On a point's right-click menu | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Set speed…** | Types the point's speed. |
| **Reset to 100%** | Puts the point back to 100%. |
| **Delete point** | Removes the point. Not on a clip's first or last. |

| On the line's right-click menu | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Add point** | Adds a point where the click was. |
| **Linear** | The speed changes evenly to the next point. |
| **Fast** | The speed changes most just after the point. |
| **Slow** | The speed changes most just before the next point. |
| **Smooth** | The speed eases out of the point and into the next. |
| **Sharp** | The speed changes fastest near both points. |
| **Hold** | The speed stays as it is until the next point, then jumps. |
| **Reset speed** | Sets the whole clip back to 100%. |

Below 0% the clip plays backwards.

A clip keeps its start and its length. A change of speed changes which source frames fill it.
Lengthening a retimed clip carries on at the speed its last point has.

| On a clip's right-click menu | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Speed…** | Sets the whole clip to one speed, from 1% to 10000%. |
| **Reset speed** | Sets the whole clip back to 100%. |
| **Copy this clip's shape** | Copies the clip's cut and ramp, without its media. |
| **Copy the whole row's shape** | Copies where every cut falls and how each piece is ramped. |
| **Paste shape onto this layer** | Cuts and ramps this Sequence layer to match. |

The razor cuts through a ramp. In the [Cut timeline](/use/cutting/) the same line is drawn
across each picture clip.

## Retime on a layer

Footage and Precomp layers have a **Retime** of their own. Turn it on with
**Layer ▸ Enable Retime**, `Ctrl+Alt+T`, or **Enable Retime** on the layer's right-click
menu. A Sequence layer has none: its clips carry theirs.

Turning it on adds a **Retime** row above Transform, with two linear keyframes at the layer's
in and out points. Nothing changes until a key moves. The row reads the moment of the source
showing, as timecode at the footage's own frame rate. **Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Editing ▸
Retime values in seconds** shows seconds instead.

**Disable Retime** removes the map, and so does turning off the row's stopwatch. The layer
keeps the frame it showed at its in point and plays on from there at normal speed.

A [node graph](/use/node-graphs/) placed as a Precomp layer retimes the same way. Inside a
graph, a **Time offset** box shifts one branch.

The ends of a retimed layer's bar drag freely. A drag trims or extends the layer and never
changes its speed.

**Edit ▸ Split layer** on a retimed layer puts a keyframe at the cut on both halves, so the
curve keeps its shape.

## The graph editor

Select the Retime row and open the [graph editor](/use/graph-editor/) (`Shift+F3`). The
**Value** and **Speed** buttons at the bottom pick the lens.

| Lens | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Value** | Which moment of the source shows, in seconds. A rising line plays forwards, a flat line holds, a falling line plays backwards. |
| **Speed** | How fast the source runs. |

<Clip name="retime-speed-graph" caption="The Retime row in the Value lens, then the Speed lens: a point added at five seconds and dragged down to 44%."/>

**Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Editing ▸ Retime opens to Speed** makes the Speed lens a speed
envelope in per cent, the same as a clip's, and opens the Retime row straight into it. With
it off, the Speed lens is the ordinary speed graph of any property, with **Keyframe speed…**
on a key's menu.

In the speed envelope:

- Double-click the line, or `Ctrl`+click it, to add a point.
- Double-click a point to put it back to 100%.
- Right-click a point for **Set speed…**, which types its **Frame** and **Speed**, **Reset to
  100%**, the curve to the next point, and **Delete key**. Speed runs from −10000% to 10000%.
- `Alt`+click a point to remove it.
- Hold `Ctrl` while dragging to change the speed a tenth as fast.
- The first point stays where it is. Moving a later point moves the frames after it.

<Shot file="speed-ramp.png" caption="A speed ramp in the graph editor, seen through the Speed lens."/>

**Every frame** is a dashed line at the speed where footage with a higher frame rate than the
composition shows each of its frames once. Below it frames repeat. A dragged point snaps to it
and the line lights. It appears only for footage faster than the composition.

## Stretch

**Stretch…** plays a layer at a new speed and gives it the length that speed implies.
Right-click the layer's row in the Timeline and choose **Stretch…**, or hold `Shift` and drag
either end of the layer's bar. Dragging the start holds the end where it is. In the Audio and
Cut workspaces, `Shift`+drag on either edge of a clip stretches the clip.

The dialogue takes a **New speed**, from 1% to 10000%, or a **New duration** in frames.
Editing either moves the other. The layer's in point holds and its end moves.

Stretch turns Retime on if it was off, and scales an existing Retime about the in point. With
several layers selected each gets the same speed. A Sequence layer cannot be stretched; its
clips have **Speed…**.

## Freeze a frame

Right-click the layer's row and choose **Freeze frame at playhead**. The frame showing is held
for one second, everything after it moves one second later, and the layer keeps its length.

The hold is two ordinary keyframes on the Retime row. It is refused on a Sequence layer, and
on or outside the layer's ends.

A point at 0% in a speed envelope also holds, and so does a **Hold** keyframe in the Value
lens.

## Play backwards

There is no Reverse command. A speed below 0% plays backwards: drag a point under 0%, or type
a negative speed into **Set speed…**. In the Value lens a line that falls plays backwards.

**Stretch…** and **Speed…** stop at 1%.

## Frames in between

The **In-between frames** setting on a footage layer's Source card decides what shows when a
retime lands between two source frames:

| Mode | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Nearest** | Repeats the closer frame. Sharp, but it stutters. |
| **Blend** | Crossfades the two frames. Smooth, but it ghosts. |

<Clip name="retime-flow" caption="The same footage at 25%: Nearest repeats each frame four times, Flow makes the frames between."/>

The third answer is **Flow**, which makes the frame in between. It is the **Flow** switch in
the layer's **Modes** column, and **Layer ▸ Flow**. Turning it on adds a **Flow** group to
Effect controls and to the layer's rows in the Timeline:

| Control | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Engine** | **Built in**, or **RIFE** from the model pack in **Settings ▸ Addons**. |
| **Flow resolution** | **Native**, **Half** or **Quarter**: the size the motion is measured at. |
| **Input rate** | The rate the footage is read at, below its own: **On 2s (12)** for animation on twos. **Auto** is the file's own rate. Keyframeable. |
| **Vector detail** | **Low** to **Ultra**. |
| **Smoothness** | How even the motion field is, 0 to 100. |
| **Occlusion** | Where a pixel shows in only one of the two frames: **Visible only** takes that frame, **Blend** weights both. |
| **Fallback** | **Blend** or **Nearest**, where flow is not confident enough to make a frame. |
| **HUD guard** | Blends still, detailed areas, such as a game's interface, instead of flowing them. On by default. |
| **Always on** | Flows even where every comp frame already lands on a source frame. |

Flow works only between 0% and the speed where every source frame is shown once.

Flow and In-between frames are set on footage layers. **Convert to sequence layer** gives
every clip the footage layer's setting.

A machine without the RIFE pack previews with the built-in engine and says so on the row, and
an export of that layer is **refused**. Turning Flow off returns the layer to Nearest and keeps
the group's settings.

**Retime blend** in the export dialogue's overrides exports with these settings, or with every
layer and clip on Nearest.

## Sound

A retimed layer or clip plays its sound at its speed, a Precomp's whole mix included. **Keep
pitch**, on the right-click menu of a layer or clip with sound, holds the pitch. It is on by
default. Off, the pitch rises and falls with the speed, as on tape. Every clip on a layer
follows the layer's setting.

**Detach audio** on the layer's right-click menu moves its sound to an Audio layer of its own.
**Convert to sequence layer** is refused on a retimed layer.

## Overrun

A retime that asks for source time past the end of the media, or before its start, holds the
last or first frame. Overrun never moves layer ends or edit points.

## Interpret footage

**Interpret footage…** on the Project panel's right-click menu (`Ctrl+Alt+G`) reads a file at
another **Frame rate**. Every layer and clip using it plays the same pictures at the new rate.
See [Importing](/use/importing/).

## From After Effects

An [imported](/use/importing/) layer's Time Remap becomes its Retime. A time stretch becomes
the matching straight-line Retime, and a negative one runs backwards. Frame Mix becomes
**Blend** and Pixel Motion becomes **Flow**.
