**Retime** maps a layer's time (or clip time, inside a Sequence layer). Use it
to ramp speed or hold a frame. It can also run footage backwards.

## What can be retimed

- **Footage layers** have their own Retime.
- **Precomp layers** have their own Retime.
- Inside a [**Sequence layer**](/use/sequence-layers/), each cut clip has its Retime.

## Speed

**Speed** is the rate the retime map runs at:

| Speed | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 100% | Normal. |
| 0% | A **freeze**. The frame holds. |
| −100% | Reverse. |

Speed is easiest to work with through the **Speed** lens of the graph editor rather than
the Value lens, which shows which moment of the source is on screen instead.

## Make a speed ramp

<Shot file="speed-ramp.png" caption="A speed ramp in the graph editor, seen through the Speed lens."/>

1. Turn on Retime for the layer.
2. Add a keyframe where the ramp starts.
3. Add another where it ends.
4. Shape the curve between them in [the graph editor](/use/graph-editor/).

## Overrun

**Overrunning** is when the map asks for source time past the end of the media, or before its
start. Lumit clamps the boundary frames and marks the region in the timeline.

Overrun never moves layer boundaries or edit points.

## Frame interpolation

The **In-between frames** setting on a footage layer's Source card decides what shows when
a retime lands between two source frames:

| Mode | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Nearest** | Repeat the closer frame. Sharp, but it stutters. |
| **Blend** | Crossfade the two frames. Smooth, but it ghosts. |

The third answer is **Flow**, which measures the motion between the two frames and
synthesises the one in between. It is the **Flow** switch in the layer's switch cluster
rather than an entry in that dropdown. Turning it on reveals a **Flow** group in the
Effect Controls panel: measurement resolution, input rate, vector detail, smoothness, how
occlusions are handled, what to fall back to, a guard for on-screen overlays, and a switch
to force it on where it would otherwise stand down.

Turning Flow off returns the layer to Nearest and keeps the group's settings.

Frame interpolation is a render setting either way. It does not change the retime map.

## Stretch

**Stretch** plays the layer at a new speed and gives it the length that implies.
Right-click the layer's row in the Timeline and choose **Stretch…**.

The dialogue takes a **new speed** in per cent or a **new duration**, and editing either
moves the other. The layer is anchored at its **in point**.

Stretch rescales the Retime map itself, so an existing Retime is kept — every key's time is
scaled about the in point.

A **clip** inside a Sequence layer has **Speed…** instead, and no duration field — a
clip's place and length are fixed. **Reset speed** on the same menu puts it back to 100%.

## Freeze a frame where you are

Right-click the layer's row and choose **Freeze frame at playhead**. The moment showing is
held for one second, everything after it is pushed that far later, and the map is cropped
back to the layer's own out point — the layer's length never changes. The tail may newly
overrun.

The hold is two ordinary keyframes once it is made, so it drags and eases like anything
else. It is refused on a Sequence layer, and at a moment on or outside the layer's own
ends.
