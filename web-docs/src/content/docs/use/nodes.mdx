The **Node graph** draws the selected layer's [effect stack](/use/effects/) as boxes joined
left to right by wires. It is the same stack the Effect Controls panel lists, seen another
way — and it can do one thing the list cannot: wire a **driver** into a parameter, so the
value comes from somewhere else instead of from its own keyframes.

<Shot file="graph-panel.png" caption="The Node graph: a layer's image chain wired left to right, with a driver feeding one of its parameters." eager/>

Open it from **Window ▸ Node graph**, or switch to the **Nodes** workspace, which gives the
graph most of the window. The panel follows the layer selected in the Timeline; with nothing
selected it says so and draws nothing.

## The image chain

Every graph starts with three boxes it derives rather than stores: **Source**, the layer's
own picture; one box per effect, in stack order; and **Layer out**. The wires between them
are the picture's path through the layer.

That path is the stack, always. An effect's **Input** takes exactly one wire, and it is the
effect above it — so there is no gesture here that branches the picture, skips a stage, or
merges two chains. Whatever you build in the graph, the Effect Controls list can show it
honestly, because the list is what the graph is drawing.

| Gesture | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Drop a node onto a wire | Inserts it at that point in the stack. |
| Drag a box's body | Moves it. A drag that starts inside the selection moves the whole selection. |
| Drag from a socket | Pulls out a wire; drop it on a socket that takes the same kind of value. |
| `Delete` | Removes the picked boxes. |

Two switches sit in the panel's toolbar. **Auto-wire** joins a node to the wire in hand as it
lands, rather than leaving it loose. **Heal** reconnects the neighbours of a deleted box, so
the chain closes over the gap instead of breaking.

## Getting about the canvas

- **Middle-drag** pans. The wheel zooms about the pointer, and the reading beside it says how
  far in you are.
- **Frame all** fits every box on screen.
- A **primary drag on empty canvas** sweeps a selection box, taking every box wholly inside
  it. Click to replace the selection, `Ctrl`-click to toggle one, `Shift`-click to add, and
  `Ctrl+A` for everything on the canvas.
- **`Tab`** opens the node search. Press it with a wire in hand and the results are filtered
  to the kinds that wire can join, and picking one connects it.

Each box carries two small badges. **B** bypasses it — the box draws with a dashed border
and the picture passes through untouched. **E** exposes it, growing a hollow socket for
every parameter so there is something to wire into; a socket that already has a wire is
drawn whether the box is exposed or not.

## Drivers

A **driver** makes no picture. It makes a value — a wobbling number, the loudness of the
music, a slowly turning colour — and a wire from a driver into a parameter's socket makes
that parameter follow the value. "The glow pulses with the music" is one wire rather than
an expression.

| Driver | Hands out |
| --- | --- |
| [**Wiggle**](/effects/drivers/wiggle/) | A number wandering by an Amount at a Frequency. Seeded per node, so it is the same wobble every time you play it. |
| [**Audio level**](/effects/drivers/audio-level/) | The loudness of a named layer's sound at this moment, over a window you set, plus the same reading over the low end alone. |
| [**Colour cycle**](/effects/drivers/colour-cycle/) | A colour whose hue turns at a Rate from a Phase, at the Saturation and Brightness you give it. |
| [**Math**](/effects/drivers/math/) | Two numbers and an operation. An expression you can see. |
| [**Remap**](/effects/drivers/remap/) | Maps a number from one range onto another, with a clamp. |
| [**Smooth**](/effects/drivers/smooth/) | Its input, softened over Time. |
| [**Points sample**](/effects/drivers/points-sample/) | Reads a points stream: how many points are alive, and how far the nearest one is from a position. |
| [**Layer points**](/effects/drivers/layer-points/) | Another layer's points, brought into this graph. |

Add one from the `Tab` search — they are grouped under **Drivers** — and wire its output to
a parameter's socket.

Two of them read something outside this layer, and they name it rather than reaching for it
with a wire: Audio level has an **Audio** row and Layer points a **Points layer** row, both
ordinary layer pickers. Wires never cross between layers.

A driven parameter says so wherever it is drawn. Its row in Effect Controls reads *driven*
and names the driver in place of a value, and its stopwatch keyframes are overridden for as
long as the wire is there. Pull the wire and the keyframes are exactly where you left them.

## Wires and their colours

A wire's colour is the kind of value it carries, so the **Types** legend in the corner of the
canvas is the whole of what you need to read one. Seven kinds share five colours, grouped as
the legend groups them:

- **image · matte**
- **number**
- **colour**
- **shape · points**
- **audio**

The colours come from the theme, so a scheme of your own changes them and the legend still
reads. Dropping a wire on a socket of another kind is refused, and nothing is committed.

## Mattes and points

An effect's **Matte** socket takes a wire as well as the layer picker on its row. The one
thing the graph can do that the row cannot is feed an effect the layer's **own** masked
picture at that point in the chain — the Source box's matte output. While that wire is
there it is what the effect reads, and the Matte row says so by name.

**Particulate** and the rest of the points family hand their points out on a socket of their
own, for another box to read. A box whose Points socket has nothing in it draws a **No
stream** mark and behaves as if it had been given an empty one — nothing anywhere near,
nothing to draw.

## Seeing the picture at a box

Selecting exactly one effect — a box here, or a heading in Effect Controls, they are one
selection — puts an **at &lt;effect&gt;** chip over the Viewer's picture. Turn it on and the
Viewer renders the composition with that layer's stack stopping there, at full quality
through the ordinary path. It is the finished picture with the rest of the stack cut off,
not a preview of one.

A driver offers no chip. It makes a number, not a picture.

## The Nodes workspace

<Shot file="nodes-workspace.png" caption="The Nodes workspace: the graph across most of the window, a small Viewer and the Node panel down the right." />

The **Nodes** workspace puts the graph where the Timeline usually is: the graph takes the
centre and left with a short Timeline underneath it, and the right-hand column carries a
small Viewer — keeping its whole bar — over the **Node panel**, which lists the picked box's
parameter rows. It carries no Project panel, on the reasoning that you arrive here with the
layer already chosen.

The Node panel follows the last box you picked. Its heading counts how many of that box's
parameters are being driven.

:::note[What the graph does not draw]
There are no branching image chains and no merge node: joining two pictures is what layers
and [blend modes](/use/blend-modes/) already do. The Layer out box draws an **Audio** input
that takes no wire — a layer's sound comes from its own footage, and wiring audio into a
layer's output is not built. And the graph draws the document, never the engine's internal
evaluation of it.
:::
