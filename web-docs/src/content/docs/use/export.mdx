**Export** writes a deliverable file. Lumit says *export* for what you produce, and
*render* only for what the engine does internally.

## Export a composition

1. Select the composition.
2. Choose **File ▸ Export**, or press `Ctrl+Alt+M`.
3. Set the format, preset, range, and destination.
4. Start it.

One composition is exported at a time, from the dialogue.

## Export does not block you

Export runs away from the interface. You can keep editing while it writes.

## Presets

| Preset | For |
| --- | --- |
| **YouTube 1080p60** | H.264, high bitrate. |
| **YouTube 1440p60** | HEVC; the scene's quality trick. |
| **Vertical 1080×1920** | Shorts, TikTok, Reels. |
| **PNG sequence** | One lossless still per frame, with alpha. |

## Codecs and encoders

Video exports as **H.264** or **HEVC**. Lumit probes for a hardware encoder - NVENC,
AMF, or QSV - and picks one automatically, with a software fallback that is always
available. Audio is AAC. A PNG sequence carries no audio and no bitrate; it is
lossless.

## Export is always full quality

[Adaptive degradation](/use/preview/) and preview resolution affect preview only. An
export always renders at full quality.

Export may **bake** internally - flattening retimes, rasterising, pre-compositing - to
go faster. Baking happens inside the export pipeline and never alters your project.

:::note[Not built yet]
An export queue - several items queued up, each with its own range, preset and
filename - is planned but not built. So are ProRes and DNxHR intermediate codecs,
free width and height (sizes are preset-driven today), and an export priority control.
:::
