Lumit tracks in two ways. [Camera track](/effects/utility/camera-track/) works out **where
the camera was**, and returns a Camera layer that moves the way the real one did.
[Planar track](/effects/utility/planar-track/) calculates **the movement of a surface, or of
one or two points**, and hands you a [Corner pin](/effects/distortion/corner-pin/) keyed to
it or keyframes on another layer's transform.

Both are effects, applied to the layer holding the shot, and both keep working while you
carry on editing.

## Camera track

1. Select the footage layer and apply **Effect ▸ Utility ▸ Camera track**.
2. Press **Analyse**. The status row counts the frames as they go, and **Cancel** stops it.

The analysis is of the **whole source footage**, not of the layer, and every layer using
that footage shares the solve. Re-analyse after changing a setting.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Feature density** | How many points are followed. More is slower but copes better with hard footage. |
| **Use masks** | Leaves the layer's masks out of the track. |
| **Show points** | Draws the solved points over the picture. |

### A track that stops part-way

Where the shot stops being followable, Lumit stops there and solves the part that worked.

A thin bar above the status line shows the analysed span against the length of the
footage, and the line says how far it got.

A camera following a partial track moves inside the span and holds its last position past
the end.

### The camera

**Create camera** beside the status makes a [Camera layer](/use/camera/) that follows the
solve. Its Transform heading wears a *Following the solve* badge, and its position and
zoom are derived from the shot on every frame.

Past the end of what was solved the badge reads *Holding the last solved frame*. If the
tracked layer is deleted or its media goes offline it reads *The solve could not be found*
and the camera holds where it was.

### Nudging it

A linked camera's own transform and zoom rows still take edits, and what you type there is
a **offset**.

**Convert to keyframes** bakes solve and correction together into one key per frame and 
ends the link.

### The point cloud

With **Show points** on, the solved points draw over the picture on the tracked layer.
Select some and two buttons appear over the picture: **Create null** and **Create solid**,
each placed at the average position of what you picked and facing the camera.

## Planar track

1. Select the footage layer and apply **Effect ▸ Utility ▸ Planar track**.
2. Set **Follow**, and place its geometry on the source's **first frame**. That frame is
   the reference, and every later frame is measured against it.
3. Mask out anything that crosses what is being followed. The layer's own masks still
   apply inside it.
4. Press **Analyse**.
5. Set **Pin layer** to the layer you want moved, and press **Create corner pin** or
   **Create transform keys**.

**Feature density** and **Use masks** work as they do on the Camera track.

### What Follow means

| Follow | What is followed | Rows it reads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Surface** | one flat thing, as four corners | the four corner points |
| **One point** | one search box | **Point 1**, **Region size** |
| **Two points** | two search boxes, each on its own | **Point 1**, **Point 2**, **Region size** |

Two points need not be on one surface, or on one object.

### What the buttons write

**Create corner pin** appends a [Corner pin](/effects/distortion/corner-pin/) to the **Pin
layer** with one keyframe per frame. They are ordinary keyframes. A layer that already
carries a Corner pin keeps it.

**Create transform keys** keyframes that layer's own Position, Rotation and Scale instead.
The keys are added to what the layer already had.

One point carries a position and nothing else, so it writes Position alone. Surface and
two points write all three.

### Reading the status

*Tracked over 400 frames* is a whole track. *Tracked 180 of 400 frames* means what was
being followed stopped being followable, and the span bar shows how far it reached.

A second line may read *Re-anchored twice, so the far end may drift a little*. When too
few of the reference frame's features survive, the measurement adopts a recent frame
instead and carries a little error forward.

*What is inside the shape is not one flat surface* means the four points enclose something
that is not flat, or whose parts move against each other. Following points instead does not
ask that question.

:::note[Limitations]
The quad and the points are rows in the panel; dragging them on the picture is not built,
and neither can be keyframed. The Planar track analyses a footage layer only, where the
Camera track will also analyse a Precomp layer.
:::
