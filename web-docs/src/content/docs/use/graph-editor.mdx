The graph editor draws a property as a line of value, or rate of change, against time.

<Shot file="graph-editor.png" caption="The graph editor, showing the value graph." eager/>

## Open a curve

1. Press **Graph** in the Timeline, or `Shift+F3`.
2. Click a property's name in the outline. `Ctrl`+click or `Shift`+click adds more.

**Reveal in graph**, on a property row's right-click menu, does both.

## The three views

| View | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Value graph** | The value against time. |
| **Speed graph** | The rate of change against time. |
| **Acceleration graph** | How fast the speed changes, in the property's unit per second squared. |

All three show the same underlying data. Editing one affects the others. **Value**,
**Speed** and **Acceleration** in the graph's top right corner switch between them.

The scope button at the bottom steps through **Selected curves**, **Layer curves** and
**Composition curves**: the selected properties, every animated property of the selected
layers, or every animated property in the composition. Unselected curves are dashed; click
one to select it, or `Shift`-click or `Ctrl`-click to add it.

## Edit a curve

- Drag a keyframe to change its value and time.
- Drag a handle to change the curve's shape.
- Hold `Alt` as a handle drag begins to move that handle without its partner, or to join two
  that were apart.
- **Join handles**, on a keyframe's right-click menu, joins handles that were apart.
- Turn on **Break handles** at the bottom of the graph editor to move one handle at a time
  with no key held. It is off each time Lumit starts.
- Select several keyframes to move them together. Dragging a handle on one moves the same
  handle on each by the same amount.
- Double-click the curve to add a keyframe, and `Alt`+click a keyframe to remove it.
- Double-click a keyframe to type its frame, value and influence.

To change a keyframe to linear, easy ease, or hold, press **Linear**, **Easy ease** or
**Hold** at the bottom of the graph editor. The default shortcut for easy ease is `F9`.
**Auto**, **Clamp** and **Free** set the selected keyframes' tangents.

Right-click a keyframe for its interpolation, **Tangents**, **Ease…**, **Keyframe speed…**,
**Join handles**, **Copy**, **Paste**, **Copy ease**, **Paste ease** and **Delete keyframe**. The
menu acts on every selected keyframe. A click on a keyframe that is not selected selects it
first.

Right-click a curve to add a keyframe there or set the stretch between two keyframes. Right-click
a handle for **Flatten**. Right-click the empty plot for the view: the three graphs, the three
scopes, framing and **Snap**.

With more than one curve on the graph, a list in its corner names them. Click a row to hide or
show its curve. `Alt`-click to solo it.

**Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Shortcuts ▸ Dragging** changes `Alt` to `Ctrl`.

Every control and gesture is listed under [Graph editor](/panels/graph-editor/).

## Apply one shape to many keyframes

1. Select the keyframes.
2. Press **Easing…** at the bottom of the graph editor, on the value graph.
3. Click a shape in the [Easing panel](/panels/easing/), or draw one and press **Apply**.

## The acceleration graph

In the acceleration graph each keyframe has an in dot and an out dot. Dragging a dot up or
down changes that side's influence and keeps its speed, as far as the influence can reach.
Dragging it sideways moves the keyframe in time. A selected keyframe's handle sets that side's
influence and keeps its speed.

Hollow rings in the speed and acceleration graphs mark where a curve turns. The acceleration
graph reads each curve's acceleration at the playhead in its corner.

Double-click a keyframe in the acceleration graph to type its numbers:

- **Frame** and **Value**: where the keyframe sits.
- **In** and **Out**: the acceleration each side has at the keyframe, in the property's unit per
  second squared.
- **Speed profile**: from rest, speeds up by **Acceleration** to **Cruise speed**, holds it, and
  slows by **Deceleration** to rest at the end **Value** or **Frame**. A keyframe marks the end
  of each stretch.
- **Gravity**: leaves at **Start speed** and changes speed by a constant **Acceleration**, to the
  end **Value** or **Frame**.

Speed profile and Gravity make the next keyframe the end, or add one. Typing another number
replaces the motion. Lumit refuses a motion that passes the end of the composition, the
keyframe after the next, or the property's range, and the box says so.

## Retime in the graph editor

A Retime curve opens on the value graph. With **Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Editing ▸
Retime opens to Speed** on, and in the **Retiming** workspace, it opens on the speed graph. See
[Retiming and speed](/use/retime/#the-graph-editor).
