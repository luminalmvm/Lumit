A composition's sound runs from each layer that makes a sound, through any **Bus layers**, to
the **master**. The [Mixer](/panels/audio/#mixer) shows each as a strip, and the
[Audio timeline](/panels/audio/#audio-timeline) as a track.

## Buses

A **Bus layer** has no sound of its own. It sums what is routed to it, plays it through its
audio effects, Volume and Pan, and passes it on. It sounds for the whole composition,
whatever its in and out points.

- **Layer ▸ New ▸ Bus**, **New layer ▸ Bus** on the Timeline's ground menu, or
  `Ctrl`+`Alt`+`Shift`+`B` makes one. The selected layers that make a sound are routed into
  it, as one undo step.
- **Insert bus**, on the ground menus of the Mixer, the Cut timeline and the Audio timeline,
  makes an empty one.
- **New bus** on a Mixer strip's output chip makes one and routes the strip into it.

## Routing

Each layer that makes a sound, and each bus, has one **output** and any number of
**sends**.

- **Output** is the master or one bus. The sound goes there after Volume and Pan.
- A **send** takes a copy of the sound to another bus at a level of its own, which can be
  keyed. **Post-fader** takes it after Volume and Pan, **Pre-fader** before them. A new send
  is post-fader at 0 dB.
- **Mute send** silences a send and keeps it. A muted send is dimmed, and a solo does not
  bring it back.

**Output ▸**, **Send to ▸** and **Mute send ▸** are on the right-click menu of a Mixer strip,
an Audio timeline track head and a Cut timeline sound track's head. **Mute send** is also on a
send's menu in the Mixer, on its row in the Timeline's **Sends** fold and on its lane in the
Audio timeline.

A bus can feed another bus. A bus that would bring the sound back round to where it started
is greyed in the lists, and such a route is **refused**: the status line says *That route
would send the sound round in a circle.*

## Solo and mute

- **Mute** silences a layer, or a bus and the sound passing through it.
- **Solo**: with anything soloed, only what is soloed is heard, through the buses it feeds
  and its sends. A soloed bus brings in what feeds it.
- **Solo safe** keeps a layer heard whatever is soloed. `Ctrl`-click **S** in the Mixer, or
  choose **Solo safe** on the strip's menu. A solo safe **S** wears a ring. A solo safe bus
  does not bring in what feeds it.
- `Alt`-click **S**, or `Alt`+`S` in the Mixer, solos that strip and clears every other
  solo, as one undo step.

The Mixer dims the strips a solo silences.

## Gain and Volume

**Gain** is a layer's level before its audio effects and Volume, from −60 to +24 dB. It is one
value, never keyed. A clip's **gain** is the same for one clip. **Volume** is the fader, after
the effects, and can be keyed. A Bus layer has no Gain.

| Where | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| The **Gain** pot on a Mixer strip | Gain, from −24 to +24 dB |
| The **Gain** row in the Timeline's **Audio** group | Gain, from −24 to +24 dB |
| The line across a sound clip | The clip's gain. See [Cutting](/use/cutting/#gain). |

- **Normalise** sets the Gain of a layer that is one file, and the gain of each clip of a
  Sequence layer, so its loudest sample lands at **Normalise to** in
  [Settings](/use/settings/#audio). Volume and its keys are left as they are.
- **Normalise loudness ▸** sets each clip's gain so its measured loudness lands at −14, −16
  or −23 LUFS. A layer's menu has it when the layer has clips.
- **Normalise together** and **Normalise loudness together ▸**, with several layers or clips
  selected, move them all by one change. The loudest lands on the target and the rest keep
  their balance. **Normalise loudness together ▸** needs every selected layer to have clips.
- **Reset gain** puts a layer's Gain back at 0 dB, and on a clip's menu the clip's gain.

Waveforms are drawn after the clip's gain and the layer's Gain.

## Automation

A layer's or bus's Volume, Pan and send levels, and the master's Volume, are recorded as keys
while the composition plays. How is set by each strip's **automation mode**.

| Mode | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Read** | Plays the keys. A control let go is written as it is when stopped. |
| **Touch** | Writes while a control is held, then glides back to the keys over **Touch return**. |
| **Latch** | Writes from the first move until playback stops, holding the last value after it is let go. |
| **Write** | Writes every Volume, Pan and send of the strip from the start of playback until it stops, held or not. The strip then switches to **Touch**. |
| **Trim** | A move shifts the keys already there by the change, then glides back. |

The mode is set from the chip on a Mixer strip, the Master strip and an Audio timeline track
head, from **Automation ▸** on a strip's or track head's menu, and with `A` in the Mixer.
**Automation ▸** on the Mixer's ground menu sets every strip. **Write** is drawn in the error
colour, **Touch**, **Latch** and **Trim** in the warning colour, and **Read** in the accent
colour while the strip has keys.

Recording comes from the Mixer's faders, Pan pots and send bars, and in the Audio timeline
from a lane's line and value field and from the Volume and Pan lines over clips. A move is
heard at once.

- A pass ends when playback stops or pauses, the playhead is moved, or a loop goes round.
  Its points become linear keys that replace the keys in the stretch it wrote, and the curve
  either side stays as it was. Where the pass starts or ends off the curve, it ramps to it
  over 20 ms inside the pass.
- Everything written in one pass is one undo step, **Record automation**.
- While a pass writes, the fader's knob and the stretch on the lane are drawn in the error
  colour.
- While playing, the faders, pots and send bars follow the keys.
- **Touch return** is in [Settings ▸ Audio](/use/settings/#audio).

## Lower behind

**Lower behind** brings a layer's or bus's level down while another layer or bus is heard,
and back up when it stops. It follows the other's level after its fader, and works on top of
Volume and its keys.

- **Lower behind ▸** on a strip's or track head's menu lists the composition's other layers
  that make a sound, and its buses. A pick lowers the selected layers behind it by 12 dB.
- The Audio panel's **Lower behind…** opens a popover with the same list and, once one is
  picked, its settings.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Amount** | How far the level drops, from −60 to 0 dB. −12 dB to begin with. |
| **Threshold** | How loud the other has to be before it drops, from −60 to 0 dB. −36 dB to begin with. |
| **Attack** | How quickly it drops, from 1 to 500 ms. 20 ms to begin with. |
| **Release** | How quickly it comes back, from 10 to 5000 ms. 600 ms to begin with. |
| **Remove** | Takes Lower behind off. |

A source that would bring the sound back round to the layer is **refused**. A thin bar beside
a strip's meters in the Mixer shows how far the level is held down.

## Loudness

A composition's **loudness target** is the loudness its mix is made for, with a true peak it
stays under. **Loudness target ▸** is on the Master strip's menu, the Audio timeline's Master
track and **Composition ▸ Audio**.

- **Off**, **−14 LUFS**, **−16 LUFS**, **−23 LUFS** or **−24 LUFS**, each with a true peak of
  −1 dBTP.
- **Custom…** asks for an **Integrated loudness**, from −40 to −5 LUFS, and a **True peak**,
  from −9 to 0 dBTP.

The Master strip shows the target as a mark across its short-term loudness bar, and beside
its integrated loudness. Both are coloured against it: within 1 LU in the success colour,
louder in the warning colour. The Audio panel draws it as a line across its loudness history.
The master's limiter holds the mix under the target's true peak, or under −0.3 dB with no
target. An [export](/use/export/#loudness-and-stems) of the composition starts on the target.

**Normalise mix…**, beside it in the same three menus, measures the whole mix through the
limiter and moves the master fader, or every master key by the same amount, so the mix meets
the target. It measures again, and moves once more where the limiter kept it over half a unit
off. With no target it asks for one. The measure can be cancelled. The status line gives the
loudness measured after, or says the mix is silent and nothing was changed. It is one undo
step, **Normalise mix**.
