The [Roto brush](/effects/utility/roto-brush/) cuts a moving subject out of a shot
from a few scribbles.

## 1. Stroke the base frame

1. Add a **Roto brush** effect to the footage layer.
2. Pick the **Roto brush** tool (`Alt+W`). **Size** on the options bar sets the
   scribble's width.
3. Scrub to a frame where the subject separates well. It does not have to be the first
   frame.
4. Drag through the subject. The stroke draws green, and the matte appears when you
   release.
5. Where the matte grabs too much, hold `Alt` and drag through the background — a red
   stroke.

The frame you stroked first becomes the **base frame**; the effect's card shows it,
and **Set to current frame** moves it. If you draw no background stroke, the frame's
border counts as background.

Set **View** on the card to **Boundary** to see the edge drawn over the picture, or
**Matte** to see the matte itself.

## 2. Propagate

Press **Propagate** on the effect's card. The matte is carried outward from the base
frame in both directions as a background job, and you can keep working while it runs.
The card shows how far the span reaches; **Cancel** keeps the frames already solved.

## 3. Correct a frame

Scrub the result. Where the matte leaks or drops a limb:

1. Scribble **across** the wrong region — foreground where it lost the subject, `Alt`
   where it grabbed background.
2. Propagate again. Only the frames from the correction outward are re-solved.

Corrections carry forward, away from the base frame.

## 4. Refine the edge

**Refine radius** on the card sets how wide the softened band round the edge is. Where
one spot needs more, pick the **Refine edge** tool (with the Roto brush on the strip) and
paint over it; the band widens where you painted.
