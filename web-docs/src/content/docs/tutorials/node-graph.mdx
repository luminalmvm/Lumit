Two jobs the [node graph](/use/nodes/) does that no panel of numbers can: it makes a
parameter follow something else in the composition, and it lets one effect hand its work
to another. Both are a wire.

<Shot file="graph-panel.png" caption="A layer's chain with a Wiggle driver wired into Glow's Threshold." eager/>

Open the graph from **Window ▸ Node graph**, or switch to the **Nodes** workspace, which
gives it most of the window. It follows whichever layer is selected in the Timeline.

## Make a glow pulse with the music

The composition has a music layer and a layer you want to react to it.

**1. Put a Glow on the layer.** Select it and add **Effect ▸ Stylise ▸ Glow**. It appears
in the graph as a box between Source and Layer out, because the graph is drawing the
effect stack.

**2. Add the driver.** Click empty canvas and press `Tab`. The search lists the
[drivers](/effects/#drivers) — effects that make a value rather than a picture. Type
`audio` and pick **[Audio level](/effects/drivers/audio-level/)**.

**3. Point it at the sound.** A driver never reaches across to another layer with a wire,
so Audio level has an **Audio** row instead: pick the music layer on it. The box now hands
out two numbers — **Amplitude**, the whole sound, and **Low**, the bottom end alone.

**4. Give yourself something to wire into.** An effect only draws sockets for its
parameters once you ask. Click the **E** badge in Glow's header and a hollow socket appears
beside every one of its parameters.

**5. Fit the range.** Loudness runs between nought and about a half; Glow's Intensity wants
something you choose. Press `Tab` again and add a
**[Remap](/effects/drivers/remap/)**. Drag from Audio level's **Low** socket to Remap's
**Value** socket, then set **In high** to about `0.4` and **Out low** / **Out high** to the
quiet and loud ends of the glow you want — say `0.5` and `3`.

**6. Wire it home.** Drag from Remap's output to Glow's **Intensity** socket. Play the
composition back.

The Intensity row in [Effect controls](/panels/effect-controls/) now reads *driven* and
names the driver where its number was. Any keyframes it had are overridden while the wire
is there and are exactly where you left them when you pull it.

:::tip
Wire the amplitude straight into a parameter first and watch what it does before adding the
Remap. Half of driving something from audio is finding out what range the music actually
produces.
:::

## Feed one effect's points into another

Some effects hand out more than a picture. The points family produces a **stream** — a
set of positions — on a teal socket, and another effect reads it. That is one effect wired
into another, and it is the only kind of wiring between two effects there is: the picture
itself always runs straight down the stack.

**1. Add the producer.** On a layer, add **Effect ▸ Generate ▸ Grid**. It draws a lattice
of soft discs, and hands the same lattice out on a **Points** socket.

**2. Add the consumer, below it.** Add **Effect ▸ Generate ▸ Clone to points**. It must sit
**after** Grid in the stack — a data wire only ever runs downstream, because a consumer
reads the stream at its own place in the chain. Its **Points** socket is drawn whether the
box is exposed or not: there is no row for it in Effect controls, so the socket is the only
way to reach it. Until something is wired in, the box says **No stream** and draws nothing.

**3. Wire them.** Drag from Grid's Points socket to Clone to points' Points socket. Both
are teal, so they take each other; dropping a wire on a socket of another kind is simply
refused.

**4. Choose what gets cloned.** Set **Clone layer** on the Clone to points row to the layer
you want stamped at every point.

**5. Take the lattice out of the picture.** You probably want the copies, not the discs.
Turn Grid's **Mix** down to nought: the stream is still handed out while nothing is drawn.

From there, [Connect points](/effects/generate/connect-points/) draws a mesh over the same
lattice, [Trail](/effects/generate/trail/) leaves a tail behind each one, and
[Scatter](/effects/generate/scatter/) or
[Emit from image](/effects/generate/emit-from-image/) can stand in for Grid as the producer.
Any of them will feed the same socket.

## Two things worth remembering

**The stack is the picture's path, always.** There is no branching and no merging on the
canvas: an effect's **Input** takes exactly one wire and it is the effect above it. The
Effect controls list and the graph are the same thing drawn two ways.

**Colour is the whole legend.** A wire's colour is the kind of value it carries — blue for
image and matte, amber for numbers, magenta for colour, teal for shapes and points, green
for audio. The key sits in the corner of the canvas, and nothing else on it is colour-coded.
