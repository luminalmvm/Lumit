A [camera](/use/camera/) sees the layers that have the 3D switch on. This walk-through
spaces a few layers in depth, moves a camera past them, and blurs the ones out of focus.

**1. Turn on 3D.** Select the layers and choose **Layer ▸ 3D layer**.

**2. Space the layers.** Select one layer, press `P` in the Timeline, and set the third
box on **Position**. A larger z is further from the camera. Do the same for each layer.

**3. Add the camera.** Choose **Layer ▸ New ▸ Camera**.

**4. Look at the layers from outside.** On the Viewer's bottom bar, open **3D view** and
choose **Custom view 1**. Pick **Orbit camera** from the Camera tools on the toolbar and
drag on the picture. The layers and the camera are drawn as outlines.

**5. Go back to the camera.** Open **3D view** and choose **Active camera**.

**6. Key the start.** Move the playhead to where the move starts. Twirl the camera open in
the Timeline and turn on the stopwatch on **Position**.

**7. Key the end.** Move the playhead to where the move ends and change the camera's
**Position**. Its z moves it towards the layers, and its x and y move it across them.

**8. Turn on depth of field.** Select the camera, choose **Layer ▸ 3D ▸ Camera settings**,
tick **Depth of field** and press **Apply**.

**9. Focus.** Under **Camera options** on the camera, set **Focus distance** to the
distance from the camera to the layer to keep sharp: that layer's z less the camera's z.

**10. Set the blur.** Raise **Aperture** on the camera until the other layers are soft
enough.
