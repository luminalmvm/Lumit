A Vegas velocity envelope is a **speed envelope** in Lumit. It sits under the clips of a
[Sequence layer](/use/sequence-layers/), one line per clip, in per cent. Everything else on
this page is in [Retiming and speed](/use/retime/).

## Vegas to Lumit

| Vegas | Lumit |
| --- | --- |
| Velocity envelope | The speed envelope under a Sequence layer's clips |
| Playback rate, in Event properties | **Speed…** on the clip's right-click menu |
| `Ctrl`+drag an event's edge | Not built. **Stretch…** on a layer's right-click menu |
| Reverse | A speed below 0% |
| Insert freeze frame at cursor | A point at 0%, or **Freeze frame at playhead** on a layer |
| Resample mode: Disable resample | **In-between frames ▸ Nearest** |
| Resample mode: Force resample | **In-between frames ▸ Blend** |
| Optical flow | **Flow** |

A velocity envelope in Vegas runs from −100% to 300%. A speed envelope runs from −10000% to
10000%.

## 1. Set Lumit up the Vegas way

On the first run, choose **Vegas** under **Editing style**. Tick **Use the Vegas shortcuts**
for the keys you know.

Already past the first run, the same three choices are in Settings:

- **Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Editing ▸ Video arrives as a Sequence layer**
- **Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Editing ▸ Retime opens to Speed**
- The **Vegas** preset in **Settings ▸ Shortcuts**

## 2. Put the footage on a Sequence layer

Drag the footage from the Project panel into the composition. With **Video arrives as a
Sequence layer** on, it arrives as one.

A footage layer already in the composition becomes one with **Convert to sequence layer** on
its right-click menu.

## 3. Open the layer

Double-click the Sequence layer's row in the Timeline. The clips open in place, and the speed
envelope runs under them at 100%. Drag the divider above the envelope to make it taller.

<Clip name="retime-envelope" caption="A point added where the slow motion starts, and the clip's end dragged down to 25%." />

## 4. Add points

Double-click the line where the ramp should start, and again where it should end. A new point
takes the speed already there, so nothing changes yet.

## 5. Set the speed

Drag the second point down. The readout beside it gives the speed. Hold `Ctrl` for a tenth of
the movement.

For an exact number, double-click the point and type it into **Set speed…**.

Between two points the speed changes in a straight line. The clip keeps its start and its
length.

## 6. Play it

Press `Space`. A clip with spare media after its end can be lengthened by dragging its edge,
and carries on at the speed of its last point.

## Hold a frame

Drag a point to 0%. The frame there holds until the next point.

## Play backwards

Drag a point below 0%, or type a negative speed into **Set speed…**.

## One speed for the whole clip

Right-click the clip and choose **Speed…**. **Reset speed** puts it back to 100%.

For a layer, right-click its row and choose **Stretch…**. It sets a speed and gives the layer
the length that goes with it.

## Smoother slow motion

Footage played slower than its own frame rate repeats frames. **Flow** makes the frames in
between.

<Clip name="retime-flow" caption="The same footage at 25%: Nearest repeats each frame four times, Flow makes the frames between."/>

Flow is a switch on footage layers: the **Flow** cell in the **Modes** column, or
**Layer ▸ Flow**. **Convert to sequence layer** gives every clip the setting the footage layer
had. See [Frames in between](/use/retime/#frames-in-between).

## Sound

A retimed clip is silent. **Detach audio** on the layer's right-click menu, before retiming,
moves the sound to an Audio layer of its own at normal speed.
