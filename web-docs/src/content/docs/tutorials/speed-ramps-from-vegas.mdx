A Vegas velocity envelope is a **speed line** in Lumit, drawn across each picture clip in the
[Cut timeline](/use/cutting/), in per cent. The same line is the speed envelope under a
[Sequence layer](/use/sequence-layers/)'s clips in the Timeline. Everything else on this page
is in [Retiming and speed](/use/retime/).

## Vegas to Lumit

| Vegas | Lumit |
| --- | --- |
| Velocity envelope | The speed line across a clip in the Cut timeline |
| Fade types on an envelope segment | **Linear**, **Fast**, **Slow**, **Smooth**, **Sharp** and **Hold** on the line's right-click menu |
| Paste event attributes | **Speed ▸ Copy speed** and **Paste speed** on a clip's right-click menu |
| Playback rate, in Event properties | **Speed ▸ Speed…** on the clip's right-click menu |
| `Ctrl`+drag an event's edge | The same in Cut with the Vegas preset, or **Stretch…** on a layer's right-click menu |
| Reverse | A speed below 0% |
| Insert freeze frame at cursor | A point at 0%, or **Freeze frame at playhead** on a layer |
| Resample mode: Disable resample | **In-between frames ▸ Nearest** |
| Resample mode: Force resample | **In-between frames ▸ Blend** |
| Optical flow | **Flow** |

A velocity envelope in Vegas runs from −100% to 300%. A speed envelope runs from −10000% to
10000%.

## 1. Set Lumit up the Vegas way

On the first run, choose **Vegas** under **Editing style**. Tick **Use the Vegas shortcuts**
for the keys and mouse drags you know. Lumit opens in the **Cut** workspace.

Already past the first run, the same choices are in Settings:

- **Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Editing ▸ Video arrives as a Sequence layer**
- **Settings ▸ Timeline ▸ Editing ▸ Retime opens to Speed**
- The **Vegas** preset in **Settings ▸ Shortcuts**, which also sets **Clip drags in Cut** to
  **Vegas**

## 2. Put the footage in the Cut timeline

Drag the footage from the Project panel onto the Cut timeline. Its picture lands on a picture
track with the speed line across it at 100%, and its sound on a track below. **S** splits the
clip at the playhead. The **Speed** switch on the strip shows and hides the lines, and so does
`Ctrl+Shift+E` with the Vegas preset.

In the Timeline the same line runs under a Sequence layer's clips: double-click the layer's
row to open it.

## 3. Add points

Double-click the line where the ramp should start, and again where it should end. A new point
takes the speed already there.

## 4. Set the speed

Drag the second point down. The readout beside it gives the speed. Hold `Ctrl` for a tenth of
the movement with the point held at its time, or `Shift` to move it in time only.

For an exact number, right-click the point and choose **Set speed…**. Double-click a point to
put it back to 100%.

## 5. Shape the ramp

Right-click the line between two points and choose its curve: **Linear**, **Fast**, **Slow**,
**Smooth**, **Sharp** or **Hold**. The clip keeps its start and its length.

## 6. Give the ramp to other clips

Right-click the ramped clip and choose **Speed ▸ Copy speed**. Pick the other clips, right-click
one, and choose **Speed ▸ Paste speed**. Each takes the ramp stretched to its own length.

## 7. Play it

Press `Space`. A clip with spare media after its end can be lengthened by dragging its edge,
and carries on at the speed of its last point.

## Hold a frame

Drag a point to 0%. The frame there holds until the next point.

## Play backwards

Drag a point below 0%, or give it a negative speed with **Set speed…**.

## One speed for the whole clip

Right-click the clip and choose **Speed ▸ Speed…**. **Speed ▸ Reset speed** puts it back to
100%.

For a layer, right-click its row and choose **Stretch…**. It sets a speed and gives the layer
the length that goes with it. Holding `Shift` while you drag either end of the layer does the same.

## Smoother slow motion

Footage played slower than its own frame rate repeats frames. **Flow** makes the frames in
between.

<Clip name="retime-flow" caption="The same footage at 25%: Nearest repeats each frame four times, Flow makes the frames between."/>

Flow is a switch on footage layers: the **Flow** cell in the **Modes** column, or
**Layer ▸ Flow**. **Convert to sequence layer** gives every clip the setting the footage layer
had. See [Frames in between](/use/retime/#frames-in-between).

## Sound

In the Cut timeline a picture clip's sound is a clip of its own, and plays at normal speed
whatever the picture's speed. In the Timeline a retimed clip is silent. **Detach audio** on
the layer's right-click menu, before retiming, moves the sound to an Audio layer of its own at
normal speed.
