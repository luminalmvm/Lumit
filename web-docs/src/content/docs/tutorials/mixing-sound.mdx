The **Audio** workspace mixes a composition's sound: the [Mixer](/panels/audio/#mixer) sets
levels, the [Audio timeline](/panels/audio/#audio-timeline) cuts and fades clips, and the
[Audio panel](/panels/audio/#audio-panel) works on the selected layer.

The project has a music file and two voice recordings, and a composition is open.

**1. Open the Audio workspace.** Choose **Window ▸ Workspace ▸ Audio**.

**2. Put the sound on tracks.** Click the **Project** tab beside the Mixer. Drag the music
onto empty ground in the Audio timeline, then the first voice recording. Each becomes an
Audio layer with a track of its own.

**3. Set the levels.** Click the **Mixer** tab and press `Space` to play. Drag the music's
fader down. If **LIM** lights on the Master strip, lower the faders and click **LIM** to put
it out.

**4. Trim the music.** In the Audio timeline, drag the right edge of the music's bar to the
frame where it should stop.

**5. Fade it out.** Drag the handle at the top right corner of the music clip to the left.
The readout gives the fade's length in seconds. Right-click the fade and choose **Slow**.

**6. Join the second recording.** Drag the second voice recording from the Project panel
onto the voice track, so that it starts before the first one ends. The overlap is a
crossfade. Drag the later clip's start to make it longer or shorter.

**7. Lower the music behind the voice.** Click the music track's name to select it. In the
Audio panel, click **Lower behind…** and choose the voice layer. The music drops by up to
18 dB while the voice sounds.

**8. Change how far it drops.** Open **Window ▸ Panels ▸ Node graph** with the music layer
selected. The three boxes the button added are there. Set **Out high** on the
[Remap](/effects/drivers/remap/) box to the level the music should fall to, in dB.
