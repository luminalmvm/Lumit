Puppet bends a layer without cutting it apart: a mesh is laid over the layer, pins hold
or move spots of it, and the picture bends between them.

## 1. Place pins

1. Select the layer.
2. Pick the **Puppet position pin** tool (`Ctrl+P`). A wireframe appears over the
   layer: the mesh, cut to the picture's opaque shape.
3. Click to place a pin. Put one on each part that should hold still, and one on each
   part that will move.

A click outside the mesh, or on a layer with nothing opaque, is refused. **Density** and
**Expansion** on the options bar set how fine the mesh is and how far past the edge it
reaches.

## 2. Drag

Drag a pin. The wireframe follows the pointer, and the picture takes the deformation
when you release. Everything between the pins bends rather than stretches, and the pins
you left alone hold their spots.

One drag is one undo step; `Escape` abandons a drag in flight.

## 3. Keyframe

A layer with pins grows a **Puppet** heading in the Timeline, a row per pin, a row per
number under each pin. Animate a pin like any other property:

1. Turn on the stopwatch on the pin's position.
2. Move the playhead, drag the pin, repeat.

The keyframes take the same interpolation, easing and graph editing as everything else.
Rename a pin on its row; delete it from the row's right-click menu.

## 4. Starch, overlap and bend

The other three pin tools share the strip slot with the first:

- **Starch pin**: stiffens the region round it. **Amount** sets how strongly; the pin
  row's value column sets how far it reaches.
- **Overlap pin**: says which part draws in front when the picture folds over itself.
- **Bend pin**: dragging around the pin turns the region about it, and `Alt`-dragging
  resizes it. Both numbers keyframe like the rest.
