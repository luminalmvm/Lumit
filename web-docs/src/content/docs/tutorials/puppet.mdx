[Puppet](/use/puppet/) bends a layer without cutting it: a mesh is laid over the layer,
pins hold or move areas of it, and the picture bends between them.

The composition has a cutout on a layer of its own, with a transparent background.

**1. Select the layer.** Click it in the Timeline.

**2. Pick the Puppet position pin tool.** Press `Ctrl+P`. A wireframe appears over the
layer: the mesh, cut to the picture's opaque shape. **Density** and **Expansion** on the
toolbar set how fine the mesh is and how far past the edge it reaches.

**3. Pin what holds still.** Click the mesh once on each part that should stay where it is.

**4. Pin what moves.** Click the part that will move.

**5. Drag that pin.** The wireframe follows the pointer, and the picture bends when you
release.

**6. Turn on the pin's stopwatches.** Twirl the layer open in the Timeline, then **Puppet**,
then the pin, and click the stopwatch on **Position x** and on **Position y**.

**7. Set the next pose.** Move the playhead and drag the pin again. A keyframe is set at the
playhead. Repeat for each pose.

**8. Stiffen a part.** Press `Ctrl+P` again for the **Puppet starch pin** tool and click the
part. Raise the pin's **Amount** in the Timeline. The number on the pin's own row is how far
it reaches, in pixels.

**9. Put one part in front of another.** Press `Ctrl+P` again for the **Puppet overlap pin**
tool and click the part. Set its **Amount** above 0 to draw it in front, or below 0 to draw
it behind.

**10. Turn a part in place.** Press `Ctrl+P` again for the **Puppet bend pin** tool and
click to place a pin. Drag the pin to turn the mesh round it, or `Alt`-drag to scale it.

`Delete` removes the pin under the pointer.
