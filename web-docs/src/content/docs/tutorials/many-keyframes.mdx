Lumit treats a selection of keyframes as one thing — a **block** — so sliding a run,
stretching its timing or easing the lot is one drag or one press.

Work in the Timeline's **Layers** mode. Everything below is the same in Graph mode.

## 1. Get the keyframes on screen

- Select the layer and press `U`. It opens showing only the rows that carry keyframes.
  Press it again to shut the layer.
- **ANIMATED** on the Timeline's toolbar is the same as a standing filter: while it is lit,
  the outline draws animated rows and nothing else, for every layer at once.

<Shot file="layers-selected.png" caption="Two layers, one twirled open, with keyframes on Position and Opacity." eager/>

## 2. Catch them

Drag on empty lane space — anywhere the bars and the keyframes are not — and a box sweeps
out. Every keyframe it covers is selected on release, across as many rows as the box
reaches.

- **Hold `Shift` or `Ctrl` as the drag begins** and the box adds to what you already had,
  which is how you build a selection out of rows that are not next to each other.
- **Click empty lane space** to select nothing.

Two or more keys become a block: a box with a small handle at each end, and a badge saying
how many keys it holds and how many frames it spans — `12 keys · 48 f`. One key is a key,
not a block; it has its own drag already.

## 3. Slide the whole run

Drag any keyframe in the selection and the whole block goes with it, the same distance.
The shape of the run is kept — every key keeps its place relative to the others rather
than each finding its own frame.

The **magnet** on the bar under the lanes decides whether the run lands on whole frames.
With it on, the key in your hand snaps and the rest follow it.

## 4. Stretch the timing

Take hold of a handle at either end of the box and drag.

- The end you did **not** touch stays exactly where it is.
- The end you dragged goes where you put it.
- Every key in between keeps its share of the span, so a move that eased in the middle goes
  on easing in the middle.

The dragged end snaps to the same things a single key does — a whole frame, a marker,
the playhead, a key on another row — so a run can be pulled onto a beat and stick to it. A
block cannot be squeezed below one frame; two keys cannot share a time.

## 5. Shape the movement

Click the block's **badge**, or **EASE** on the bar under the lanes, and the Ease popover
opens on the selection.

| Row | What it asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Curve** | A named shape — easy ease, slow start, slow finish, anticipate, overshoot, snap, heavy ease, or one you saved. |
| **Influence** | How far each side of the curve reaches, in per cent, in and out separately. |
| **Stagger** | Frames between one row's keys and the next, top down or bottom up. Nought leaves every row where it is. |

**Apply** lands the shape and the box stays up, so easing is "try it, look at it, try
another" rather than a decision you commit to blind. **Open graph** hands the same selection
to [the graph editor](/use/graph-editor/), where the curve can be drawn by hand instead of
chosen.

A stagger is how you fan a title's letters, or a row of icons, out of one keyframed move:
give every layer the same animation, select the lot, and stagger by three frames.

## 6. The rest of the strip

The bar under the lanes carries the commands that act on whatever keys are selected:

- **LINEAR**, **HOLD**, **EASE**, **BEZIER** — the interpolation of every selected key.
- **REVERSE** — the block plays backwards where it stands. Values travel with their keys,
  and each key's ease is mirrored with it, so a move that eased slowly out of itself eases
  slowly into itself once reversed.
- **COPY** and **PASTE AT PLAYHEAD** — take a run of keys off one property and put it down
  on another at the time you are sitting on.

## 7. When one axis needs its own timing

Position and Anchor point come as one row with a box per axis, and one stopwatch: keying
the row keys both axes together.

Right-click the property's **name** in the outline and choose **Separate axes**. The row
becomes a row per axis, each with its own stopwatch, its own keyframes and its own curve in
the graph editor. **Combine axes** on the same menu puts them back.

Scale starts **linked** instead. **Unlink axes** gives it two boxes on one row; **Separate
axes** goes the whole way to two rows.
