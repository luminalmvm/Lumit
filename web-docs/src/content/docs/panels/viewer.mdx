The Viewer shows the composition at the playhead. It has a strip above the picture and a
strip below it, and nothing on either of them changes what an export writes.

<Shot file="viewer.png" caption="The Viewer, with a text layer selected and its transform gizmo showing." eager/>

## The strip above

The panel's name and the composition's sit at the left. Four pickers sit at the right.

| Picker | Sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Magnification** | How big the picture is drawn. Fit, with its percentage beside it, or a fixed step from 25% to 400%. The wheel zooms too, anchored on the pointer. |
| **Preview quality** | The **preview resolution** — full, half, third, quarter or auto — and the **playback** behaviour, every frame or adaptive. |
| **Playback speed** | How fast playback runs: **1×**, **0.5×** or **0.25×**. See [Preview and playback](/use/preview/#playback-speed). |
| **Colour pipeline** | The display transform the picture is shown through. Where the project loads a colour configuration it grows a section per display, each of its views a row. **Project settings…** sits at its foot. |

The magnification steps are **Fit**, 25%, 50%, 100%, 200% and 400%. Fit follows the panel
as it is resized. After a wheel or Zoom tool step the picker reads the true percentage,
from 2% to 3200%.

Magnified, the picture is drawn in square pixels. **Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Viewer ▸ Smooth the
picture when zoomed in** blends them.

The preview quality menu stays open while rows are picked. See
[Preview and playback](/use/preview/#preview-resolution) for what each row does, and
[Colour management](/use/colour-management/) for the colour pipeline.

The same commands are on the **View** menu and the keyboard.

| Command | Shortcut |
| --- | --- |
| **View ▸ Zoom in** | `Ctrl+=` |
| **View ▸ Zoom out** | `Ctrl+-` |
| **View ▸ Fit** | `Shift+/` |
| **View ▸ Resolution ▸ Full** | `Ctrl+J` |
| **View ▸ Resolution ▸ Half** | `Ctrl+Shift+J` |
| **View ▸ Resolution ▸ Quarter** | `Ctrl+Alt+J` |

Zoom in and Zoom out double and halve the magnification about the middle of the panel.
**Auto** and **Third** have no shortcut.

A narrow strip drops the composition's name. A narrower one keeps the magnification and
folds the other pickers into one mark.

## The strip below

<Shot file="viewer-bar.png" caption="The Viewer's bottom bar: the ways of looking, the snapshot, the exposure, the transport, and the composition's size."/>

| Control | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Transparency board** | Draws a chequerboard behind alpha. Off, the mark is filled and the composition's background shows. |
| **Guides** | One menu for everything drawn over the picture: the grid, title and action safe, layer controls, the region of interest, and the composition background. In a 3D composition it adds **3D ground** and **3D axes**. In a node graph, **Picture bounds** outlines how far the picture reaches past the frame, with its size in pixels. The outline is in the warning colour where it reached as far as a box keeps. **File ▸ Project settings… ▸ Node graph overscan** lets a box keep more, at the cost of memory. |
| **Channel** | RGB, red, green, blue, or alpha. |
| **3D view** | With something in depth in the composition. What the picture is looked through: the active camera, Front, Back, Left, Right, Top, Bottom, or Custom 1 to 3. With two or four views, each keeps its own, and on a 3D composition new views open on Top, Front and Right. |
| **Take snapshot** | Photographs the picture. |
| **Show snapshot** | With a snapshot taken. Puts the photograph back over the live picture while it is held. |
| **Exposure** | The number shows on hover, or while it isn't zero. Drag the number; click the mark to go back to zero. |
| **Transport** | First frame, previous frame, play or pause, next frame, last frame. |
| **Clock** | The playhead's time, counted from the composition's [**Start timecode**](/use/compositions/). Click it to type one; a time past either end clamps. |
| **Loop** | What playback does at the end of the work area: loop, play once, or ping-pong. Click to step through the three. |
| **Reading** | The composition's size. While the preview is made smaller than the composition, the size reads as the pair, source → preview, in the warning colour. |

The snapshot photographs **what is on screen**, at the resolution it is drawn at. Holding
Show puts it back over exactly the part of the picture it came from.
**View ▸ Compare ▸ Snapshot** wipes the live picture against it, the snapshot right of the
line. **Compare ▸ Composition ▸** puts another composition on the far side instead.

A view keeps one snapshot. Taking another replaces it.

While a frame takes long enough to notice, a slim bar left of the reading fills as it
is made, with a word for what the engine is doing. It goes when the frame arrives, and
shows nothing during playback.

A single channel is shown in grey. Alpha is shown as a greyscale matte. **Exposure** runs
ten stops either way, in tenths. `Ctrl+'` switches the transparency board.

The magnification, the pan and the channel belong to the view. The preview resolution, the
exposure, the guides, the overlays and the region of interest belong to the composition.

### Right-click menus on the strips

Every control on both strips has a right-click menu. Right-click an empty stretch of the
bottom strip to arrange the strips.

| Right-click | Rows |
| --- | --- |
| The magnification | **Fit**, **Zoom in**, **Zoom out**, and the fixed steps. |
| The preview quality | The preview resolutions and the two playback behaviours. |
| The playback speed | **1×**, **0.5×**, **0.25×**. |
| The transparency board | **Transparency grid**, **Composition background…**. |
| **Guides** | The rows its click opens. |
| The channel | **RGB**, **Red**, **Green**, **Blue**, **Alpha**. |
| The 3D view | The rows its click opens. |
| The snapshot marks | **Take snapshot**, **Show snapshot**, **Clear snapshot**. |
| The exposure mark | **Reset exposure**. |
| The transport | **Play** or **Pause**, **To start**, **To work area start**, **Previous frame**, **Next frame**, **To work area end**, **To end**, **Loop ▸**, **Playback speed ▸**, **Mute**. |
| The clock | **Copy time**, **Copy frame number**, then the transport's rows. |
| The loop mark | **Loop**, **Ping-pong**, **Play once**. |
| The reading | **Composition background…**, **Composition settings…**. |
| An empty stretch | **Style's choice**, **Split, top and bottom**, **One bar, at the top**, **One bar, at the bottom**, **Deck**. |

**Show snapshot** in a menu keeps the snapshot up until it is chosen again.

### When the Viewer is narrow

A Viewer docked into a sidebar has less width than the bar wants, so pieces leave in
order: the reading, then the loop mark. The ways of looking then fold into one overflow
mark, and the clock goes last. The five transport buttons are the last items remaining.

The overflow mark is **More controls**. It opens the ways of looking in a floating strip.

The two strips can also be gathered into one, at the top or at the bottom, under
**Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Viewer**.

### The deck

**Viewer bars** in the same settings group also offers **Deck**, which the Desk and
Lantern styles use unless told otherwise. The ways of looking, the magnification and the
colour pipeline sit in one strip above the picture, and everything about playing sits in
a strip under it:

| Control | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Transport** and **Clock** | As on the bar. |
| **Frame count** | The playhead's frame out of the composition's length, as `F12/300`. |
| **Playback** | Every frame, or adaptive resolution. |
| **Preview resolution** | Auto, full, half, third or quarter. |
| **Playback speed** | 1×, 0.5× or 0.25×. |
| **Loop** | As on the bar. |
| **Mute** | Silences the sound output. It is not remembered between launches. |
| **Cache ready** | How much of the work area is cached at the size the Viewer shows, as a meter and a percentage. It shows once something is cached. |
| **Progress** | The [progress bar](/use/preview/#the-progress-bar), at the right. |

A deck too narrow for its row scrolls sideways.

## Guides, grid and rulers

The **Guides** menu on the bar lists the marks drawn over the picture. Its mark is lit
while any of them is showing.

| Row | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Transparency grid** | The same switch as the transparency board. |
| **Grid** | Divides the frame into eighths each way. |
| **Title/action safe** | Two centred rectangles, at 90% and 80% of the frame. |
| **Rulers** | Strips along the top and left edges, counting composition pixels. `Ctrl+R`. |
| **Layer controls** | The wireframes, handles, motion paths, hover highlight and effect points. `Ctrl+Shift+H`. |
| **Picture bounds** | In a node graph composition. |
| **3D ground**, **3D axes** | With something in depth in the composition. See [Cameras and 3D](/use/camera/#in-the-viewer). |
| **Snap to guides** | Whether a dragged layer lands on lines. The magnet on the [toolbar](/panels/toolbar/#snapping) is the same switch. |
| **Snap to grid** | Adds the grid's lines to what a drag lands on. `Ctrl+Shift+'`. |
| **Snap to layers** | Adds the edges and middles of the other layers. |
| **Lock guides** | Stops guides being dragged. |
| **Clear guides** | Removes every guide from the composition. |
| **Region of interest** | Arms the next drag to sweep one. Reads **Clear region of interest** while one is set. |
| **Composition background…** | Opens a colour picker for the composition's background. |

**View ▸ Show grid**, **Show ruler** and **Show wireframe** are the same switches as
Grid, Rulers and Layer controls. **View ▸ Snap to grid** and **Snap to layers** are the
same switches as the rows here.

With the layer controls off, layers still select and drag.

The composition background is the one row that edits the document. It is an undo step and
an export writes it.

### Guides

Press in a ruler and drag onto the picture to pull out a guide: a horizontal one from the
top ruler, a vertical one from the left. Drag a guide to move it. Drop it back on a ruler,
or off the picture, to remove it.

Guides stay when the rulers are hidden. They are kept with the project, per composition.
Undo does not touch them, and no export draws them. A locked guide still catches a dragged
layer.

The ruler's numbering steps with the magnification. **Units** in a ruler's right-click
menu counts in pixels or in per cent of the frame.

## Region of interest

Choose **Guides ▸ Region of interest**, then drag a rectangle on the picture. Lumit
composites that part of the frame only, and outlines it. A drag of a few pixels clears the
region instead.

**Guides ▸ Clear region of interest** goes back to the whole frame. The region is kept
per composition and never reaches an export.

## In the picture

Select a layer and the Viewer draws its wireframe with a transform gizmo. The handles move,
scale and rotate it. See [Transforming layers](/use/transform/#the-gizmo). The tag at the
top left names what is selected, with a count of the other selected layers beside it.

The outline, the handles and the motion path follow the layer's parents. A keyed shape path
is outlined where it is at the playhead.

A layer whose visibility switch is off, or that is locked, has no box and takes no click.

### Moving a layer

With the Selection tool armed:

- **Drag** a layer to move it. Several selected layers move together, as one undo
  step.
- **Drag** the mark on a selected effect's point, such as a Centre, to move the point.
- **Shift** keeps the drag to one axis.
- **The arrow keys** nudge the selected layers one pixel, or ten with `Shift`, while the
  Viewer is the active panel.
- **Double-click** a text layer to edit it, or a Precomp layer to open its composition.

A press inside a selected layer drags that layer, even where another layer covers it.

While **Snap to guides** is on, a moved layer lands on the guides and the edges and middle
of the composition, with **Snap to layers** ticked on the edges and middles of the other layers,
and with **View ▸ Snap to grid** ticked on the grid. A line across the picture marks what it
landed on. `Ctrl` suspends snapping. A locked layer or a guide layer is not snapped to.

A layer whose position is keyframed does not move under a drag. A nudge sets a keyframe
at the playhead.

### Effect points

With an effect selected, or one of a point's rows, each of its points is marked on the
picture. Only points in pixels or per cent get a mark. **Guides ▸ Layer controls** hides
the marks.

- Only the Selection tool drags a mark. **Shift** keeps it to one axis.
- A point with a wire on either half does not drag, and neither does one an expression
  works out.
- A keyed half takes a key at the playhead.
- The drag is one undo step.

### Moving about

- **The wheel** zooms, anchored on the pointer.
- **A middle-button drag** pans the picture, whatever tool is armed.
- **The Hand tool** pans on an ordinary drag.
- **The Zoom tool** doubles the magnification on a click, about the pointer, and halves it
  with `Alt`. Dragging a box makes that box the view. `Alt` with a box zooms out by the
  same amount.

A middle-button drag does not pan while a dropper is armed. In a
[3D viewport](/use/camera/#3d-viewport), and with `Alt` held in a composition with
something in depth, it moves the 3D view instead.

### At an effect

Select one effect — its heading in [Effect controls](/panels/effect-controls/), or its box
in the [node graph](/use/nodes/) — and a chip appears at the top right saying *at* that
effect's name. Click it and the Viewer shows the composition with that layer's effect
stack stopping there. Click it again for the finished picture.

The chip names a box in a [node graph composition](/use/node-graphs/) the same way, and
shows the composition with its Output wired to that box.

### The dropper

Some parameters are picked off the picture rather than typed — a colour, or a point. Arm
the dropper from the parameter and a magnifier follows the pointer, with an outline round
the pixels that will be sampled.

- **Shift+scroll** grows the sampled region — one pixel, then 3×3 up to 9×9.
- **The pick is a drag.** The value is previewed as you sweep; the release commits it, as
  one undo step. `Esc` puts back what the drag started from.

## Several views

A Viewer panel holds one, two or four **views**. Each view has its own magnification, pan
and channel, and can show a different composition.

| Command | Shortcut | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **View ▸ View layout ▸ One view** | `Alt+1` | One picture. |
| **View ▸ View layout ▸ Two views across** | `Alt+2` | Two, side by side. |
| **View ▸ View layout ▸ Two views down** | | Two, one above the other. `Alt+3` turns a two-view layout between across and down. |
| **View ▸ View layout ▸ Four views** | `Alt+4` | Four, two by two. |
| **View ▸ Share view options across views** | | Every view takes the active view's magnification, pan and channel. |
| **View ▸ Lock this view** | `Ctrl+Alt+L` | Keeps the view on what it shows when something else is opened. |
| **View ▸ Always preview this view** | `Ctrl+Alt+P` | Names the active view as the one playback runs in, whichever view is active. Choosing it again takes the naming away. |
| **View ▸ Fill the window with this view** | `Ctrl+Alt+F` | Shows the Viewer alone in the window, and puts the arrangement back. |
| **Window ▸ New Viewer** | `Ctrl+Alt+V` | Adds another Viewer panel beside this one. |
| **Window ▸ New 3D viewport** | | Adds a Viewer that looks at the composition through a free view. See [Cameras and 3D](/use/camera/#3d-viewport). |

A new view starts on what the panel's first view shows. Click a view to make it the
**active** one. It takes an accent edge, and the Timeline, Effect controls and the other
panels follow its composition. `Ctrl+Shift+.` and `Ctrl+Shift+,` step to the next and
previous view.

One view plays. While the playhead is moved, every view of that composition follows it. A
view of another composition holds the frame that composition was left on.

An item opened from the Project panel lands in the active view. Where that is locked it
takes another unlocked view, then an empty place in the layout. With every view locked and
the layout full, it opens in a new Viewer panel.

The layout is saved with the workspace. Locks, and what each view shows, are saved with
the project.

**Pop out**, in the menu on a right-click of the panel's name, moves the Viewer into a
window of its own. See [Workspaces](/use/workspaces/#rearrange).

### Compare

**View ▸ Compare** puts the panel's first two views together, with a divider to drag.

| Row | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Off** | The views sit in their own places. |
| **Wipe** | Both pictures are drawn at the whole panel's size, the first shown left of the divider and the second right of it. The second takes the first's magnification and pan. `Ctrl+Alt+C` switches it on and off. |
| **Split screen** | Each picture is fitted into its own side of the divider. |
| **Snapshot** | Wipes the active view against its own snapshot, the snapshot right of the divider. Greyed until a snapshot is taken. |
| **Composition ▸** | Puts another composition on the second side and locks it there, as a wipe unless a split is up. |
| **Swap sides** | Draws the second picture left of the divider. Listed while two views are compared. |

Choosing a compare on a one-view layout makes it two views across, and **Off** puts the
layout back. No export sees a compare.

Double-click the divider to centre it. In a wipe, where the two sides differ in 3D view or
channel, each side is named at its foot, and a second side showing another composition
names it.

### Footage in a view

Double-click footage in the [Project panel](/panels/project/) and a view shows the file on
its own: the **source view**, with a strip to drag through it, **In** and **Out** marks,
and **Insert** and **Overwrite**. It has no layer controls or guides. See
[Cutting](/use/cutting/#the-source-view).

## 3D views

The **3D view** mark on the bar picks what the view looks through. It is lit while the
view is anything but **Active camera**, and the view names what it looks through at the
top of the picture.

Its menu lists the views, **Reset 3D view**, how Model layers are shown, and **Open in new
3D viewport**. See [Cameras and 3D](/use/camera/#3d-views) for the views and their
shortcuts, the [3D viewport](/use/camera/#3d-viewport) and the
[3D handles](/use/camera/#3d-handles).

## In a shared project

In a [shared project](/use/projects/#shared-projects) the Viewer shows where the others
are pointing: a dot in each person's colour, with their name.

While following someone, the Viewer wears a frame in their colour and a tab at its top
edge naming them. **Stop** on the tab, or `Esc`, ends it.

## The surround

The area round the picture is a neutral grey. **Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Viewer ▸
Surround** sets it to **Theme colour**.

## Notices

- **Missing files**: a count at the bottom left of the picture, while footage in the
  composition has lost its file. See [Importing](/use/importing/#missing-files).
- **Cut to the largest picture**: at the bottom right, while the frame was cut to
  **Largest picture**. A click opens that row in
  [Settings ▸ Preview and cache](/use/settings/#preview-and-cache).
- On Linux, a machine rendering on an NVIDIA card while drawing its window with another
  shows a message in place of the picture. Switch to NVIDIA Performance mode, or start
  Lumit with PRIME render offload.
