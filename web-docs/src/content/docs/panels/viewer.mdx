The Viewer shows the composition at the playhead. It has a strip above the picture and a
strip below it, and nothing on either of them changes what an export writes.

<Shot file="viewer.png" caption="The Viewer, with a text layer selected and its transform gizmo showing." eager/>

## The strip above

The panel's name sits at the left. Three pickers sit at the right, and each one answers a
different question.

| Picker | Asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Magnification** | How big the picture is drawn. Fit, or a fixed step from 25% to 400%. The wheel zooms too, anchored on the pointer, and the face then reads the true percentage. |
| **Preview quality** | How much quality this preview may spend. It carries two things: the **preview resolution** — full, half, third, quarter or auto — and the **playback** behaviour, every frame or adaptive. |
| **Colour pipeline** | What you are looking at. It names the display transform the picture is shown through, and where the project loads a colour configuration it grows a section per display, each of its views a row. |

Preview resolution and playback used to be asked in two places, which is how a soft picture
and a slow transport came to look like two unrelated faults. They are one question now.

The colour picker turns to the accent and adds **· preview** whenever exposure or the tone
map is engaged, because both live inside the display transform and neither reaches an
export. **Tone mapping** is a row in the same menu; it appears once you switch it on in
**Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Viewer**.

## The strip below

<Shot file="viewer-bar.png" caption="The Viewer's bottom bar: the ways of looking, the snapshot pair, the transport, and the composition's own reading."/>

Left to right: the ways of looking, then a hairline and the snapshot pair, the transport
with its clock in the middle, and the composition's own reading at the far right.

| Control | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Transparency board** | Draws a chequerboard behind alpha, so you can see what is transparent. |
| **Guides** | One menu for everything drawn over the picture, and the ground behind it: the grid, title and action safe, layer controls, the region of interest, and the composition background. |
| **Channel** | RGB, red, green, blue, or alpha. The mark takes the colour of its own answer, so a key can be read at a glance; the menu still spells the names out. |
| **Exposure** | The aperture mark and the number beside it. Drag the number, ten stops either way; click the mark to go back to nought. |
| **Take snapshot** | Photographs the picture. |
| **Show snapshot** | Puts the photograph back over the live picture while it is held. Muted, and says so, until there is one. |
| **Transport** | First frame, previous frame, play or pause, next frame, last frame. |
| **Clock** | The playhead's time. Click it to type one; a time past either end lands on that end. |
| **Reading** | The composition, the time, the source size, the size the preview was made at, and the magnification. |

A **region of interest** is swept out with a drag once it is armed from the Guides menu.
Only that rectangle is rendered, which is the quickest way to keep a heavy shot responsive
while you work on one corner of it.

The snapshot photographs **what is on screen**, at the resolution it is drawn at. Panned
into a corner of a comp at 400%, the photograph is that corner rather than a shrunken copy
of the whole frame — and holding Show puts it back over exactly the part of the picture it
came from, however you have zoomed or panned since. A picture panned entirely off the panel
is not photographed at all.

### When the Viewer is narrow

A Viewer docked into a sidebar has less width than the bar wants, so pieces leave in order,
and the transport is the last to go: the two gaps close, the reading sheds its own
statements and then goes entirely, the ways of looking fold into one overflow mark, the
clock goes, and finally the five transport buttons stand alone. Nothing is ever clipped
into unreadability.

The two strips can also be gathered into one, at the top or at the bottom, under
**Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Viewer**.

## In the picture

Select a layer and the Viewer draws its wireframe with a transform gizmo. The handles move,
scale and rotate it. See [Transforming layers](/use/transform/). The tag at the top left
names what is selected.

### At an effect

Select one effect — its heading in [Effect controls](/panels/effect-controls/), or its box
in the [node graph](/use/nodes/), which are the same pick — and a chip appears
at the top right saying *at* that effect's name. Click it and the Viewer shows the composition
with that layer's effect stack stopping there: the blur applied and nothing after it. Click
it again for the finished picture.

Nothing is soloed, bypassed or switched off by this, and the document is not touched. It
costs one render, the same as stepping a frame.

### The dropper

Some parameters are picked off the picture rather than typed — a colour, or a point such as
a focal distance. Arm the dropper from the parameter and a magnifier follows the pointer: a
nine-by-nine block of the pixels under it, each blown up to a square you can aim at, with a
solid outline round the ones that will actually be taken and a strip underneath saying what
would be picked.

- **Shift+scroll** grows the sampled region — one pixel, then 3×3, 5×5, 7×7, 9×9 — so a
  noisy patch averages out instead of grabbing one grainy pixel.
- **The pick is a drag.** Pressing writes nothing. Sweep across the picture and the value
  is previewed as you go; the release commits it, as one undo step. `Esc` puts back what
  the drag started from.
