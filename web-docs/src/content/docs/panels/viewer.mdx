The Viewer shows the composition at the playhead. It has a strip above the picture and a
strip below it, and nothing on either of them changes what an export writes.

<Shot file="viewer.png" caption="The Viewer, with a text layer selected and its transform gizmo showing." eager/>

## The strip above

The panel's name and the composition's sit at the left. Three pickers sit at the right.

| Picker | Sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Magnification** | How big the picture is drawn. Fit, with its percentage beside it, or a fixed step from 25% to 400%. The wheel zooms too, anchored on the pointer. |
| **Preview quality** | The **preview resolution** — full, half, third, quarter or auto — and the **playback** behaviour, every frame or adaptive. |
| **Colour pipeline** | The display transform the picture is shown through. Where the project loads a colour configuration it grows a section per display, each of its views a row. **Project settings…** sits at its foot. |

## The strip below

<Shot file="viewer-bar.png" caption="The Viewer's bottom bar: the ways of looking, the snapshot, the exposure, the transport, and the composition's size."/>

| Control | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Transparency board** | Draws a chequerboard behind alpha. Off, the mark is filled and the composition's background shows. |
| **Guides** | One menu for everything drawn over the picture: the grid, title and action safe, layer controls, the region of interest, and the composition background. In a 3D composition it adds **3D ground** and **3D axes**. In a node graph, **Picture bounds** outlines how far the picture reaches past the frame, with its size in pixels. The outline is in the warning colour where it reached as far as a box keeps. **File ▸ Project settings… ▸ Node graph overscan** lets a box keep more, at the cost of memory. |
| **Channel** | RGB, red, green, blue, or alpha. |
| **3D view** | With something in depth in the composition. What the picture is looked through: the active camera, Front, Back, Left, Right, Top, Bottom, or Custom 1 to 3. With two or four views, each keeps its own, and on a 3D composition new views open on Top, Front and Right. |
| **Take snapshot** | Photographs the picture. |
| **Show snapshot** | With a snapshot taken. Puts the photograph back over the live picture while it is held. |
| **Grid**, **Reference**, **Matte view** | While a [grading](/use/grading/) grid, reference or the matte view is set. See [Grading tools](#grading-tools). |
| **Exposure** | The number shows on hover, or while it isn't zero. Drag the number; click the mark to go back to zero. |
| **Transport** | First frame, previous frame, play or pause, next frame, last frame. |
| **Clock** | The playhead's time. Click it to type one; a time past either end clamps. |
| **Loop** | What playback does at the end of the work area: loop, play once, or ping-pong. Click to step through the three. |
| **Reading** | The composition's size. While the preview is made smaller than the composition, the size reads as the pair, source → preview, in the warning colour. |

The snapshot photographs **what is on screen**, at the resolution it is drawn at. Holding
Show puts it back over exactly the part of the picture it came from.
**View ▸ Compare ▸ Snapshot** wipes the live picture against it, the snapshot right of the
line. **Compare ▸ Composition ▸** puts another composition on the far side instead.

Every control on both strips has a right-click menu. Right-click an empty stretch of the
bottom strip to arrange the strips.

While a frame takes long enough to notice, a slim bar left of the reading fills as it
is made, with a word for what the engine is doing. It goes when the frame arrives, and
shows nothing during playback.

### When the Viewer is narrow

A Viewer docked into a sidebar has less width than the bar wants, so pieces leave in
order: the reading, then the loop mark. The ways of looking then fold into one overflow
mark, and the clock goes last. The five transport buttons are the last items remaining.

The two strips can also be gathered into one, at the top or at the bottom, under
**Edit ▸ Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Viewer**.

## In the picture

Select a layer and the Viewer draws its wireframe with a transform gizmo. The handles move,
scale and rotate it. See [Transforming layers](/use/transform/). The tag at the top left
names what is selected.

The outline, the handles and the motion path follow the layer's parents. A keyed shape path
is outlined where it is at the playhead.

### Moving a layer

With the Selection tool armed:

- **Drag** a layer to move it. Several selected layers move together, as one undo
  step.
- **Drag** the mark on a selected effect's point, such as a Centre, to move the point.
- **Shift** keeps the drag to one axis.
- **The arrow keys** nudge the selected layers one pixel, or ten with `Shift`, while the
  Viewer is the active panel.
- **Double-click** a text layer to edit it, or a Precomp layer to open its composition.

While **Snap to guides** is on, a moved layer lands on the guides and the edges and middle
of the composition, with **Snap to layers** ticked on the edges and middles of the other layers,
and with **View ▸ Snap to grid** ticked on the grid. A line across the picture marks what it
landed on. `Ctrl` suspends snapping. A locked layer or a guide layer is not snapped to.

A layer whose position is keyframed does not move under a drag or a nudge.

### Effect points

With an effect selected, or one of a point's rows, each of its points is marked on the
picture. Only points in pixels or per cent get a mark. **Guides ▸ Layer controls** hides
the marks.

- Only the Selection tool drags a mark. **Shift** keeps it to one axis.
- A point with a wire on either half does not drag.
- A keyed half takes a key at the playhead.
- The drag is one undo step.

A selected [Denoise](/effects/blur-sharpen/denoise/#the-sample-square) draws its sample
square, which the Selection tool moves and resizes.

### The right-click menu

Right-click the picture, with any tool.

| Row | Does |
| --- | --- |
| The layer's name | The layer on top under the pointer, which the click selects. Opens the menu the layer has in the Timeline. |
| **New**, **Paste** | The **Layer ▸ New** rows, and the clipboard. |
| **Select** | Lists every layer under the pointer, top to bottom. Choosing one selects it, which reaches a layer covered by another. |
| **Align** | Moves each selected layer so an edge or the middle of its box meets the composition's: **Left**, **Centre**, **Right**, **Top**, **Middle**, **Bottom**. |
| **Add marker at playhead** | Adds a composition marker. |
| **Guides** | **Add vertical guide** and **Add horizontal guide** at the pointer, **Snap to guides**, **Snap to grid**, **Snap to layers**, **Lock guides**, **Clear guides**. |
| **Copy frame**, **Save frame as…** | The frame at the playhead at the composition's full size, on the clipboard or as a PNG. **Save frame as…** is also in the Composition menu, on `Ctrl`+`Alt`+`Shift`+`M`. On Linux, **Copy frame** needs `wl-copy` or `xclip`. |
| **Snapshot** | **Take snapshot**, **Show snapshot**, **Clear snapshot**. |
| **Zoom**, **Resolution**, **Channel view** | The same choices as the strips'. |
| **Show** | The marks over the picture, ticked. |
| **View layout**, **Compare**, **Region of interest** | As in the **View** menu. |
| **Composition background…**, **Composition settings…** | The composition's own. |

**Select** appears with more than one layer under the pointer, and **Align** with a layer
selected.

### Moving about

- **The wheel** zooms, anchored on the pointer.
- **A middle-button drag** pans the picture, whatever tool is armed.
- **The Hand tool** pans on an ordinary drag.

A middle-button drag does not pan while a dropper is armed.

### At an effect

Select one effect — its heading in [Effect controls](/panels/effect-controls/), or its box
in the [node graph](/use/nodes/) — and a chip appears at the top right saying *at* that
effect's name. Click it and the Viewer shows the composition with that layer's effect
stack stopping there. Click it again for the finished picture.

The chip names a box in a [node graph composition](/use/node-graphs/) the same way, and
shows the composition with its Output wired to that box.

### Grading tools

In the [Colour workspace](/use/grading/) the Viewer draws the current node's tools over the
picture, in the active view. While a group's grade or a shared node is in view, they work on
its node.

- **Grid**: the current shot's versions, the selected stills or the picked shots, side by
  side in place of the picture. See [The grid](/use/grading/#the-grid). The chip on the
  bottom strip names it: a click or its cross closes it, and a right-click switches what it
  shows. `Escape` closes it too.
- **Reference**: a still or another shot's frame, against the picture, as a wipe, a split, a
  difference or a mix. Drag the divider, and double-click it to put it back in the middle.
  The **Reference** chip on the bottom strip names it: a click shows or hides it, the cross
  clears it. See [Comparing against a reference](/use/grading/#comparing-against-a-reference).
- **Matte view**: the current node's key, as **Highlight** or **Key**. The chip on the
  bottom strip names it, and a click turns it off. A right-click offers the three, then
  what Highlight shows around the key: **Over grey**, **Over black**, **Over white** or
  **Over black and white**. See [The matte view](/use/grading/#the-matte-view).
- **Windows**: the current Correction's windows, their softness rings dashed, the picked one
  in the accent with its handles. They show while a window is picked or the Windows page is
  up. See [Windows](/use/grading/#windows).
- **Pick circle**: while a qualifier pick tool is armed, a drag draws the circle it reads.
  A right-click on the picture puts the tool down. See [The qualifier](/use/grading/#the-qualifier).
- **Strokes**: on a node keyed in 3D, a pick tool draws a stroke in place of the circle. The
  node's strokes so far show faintly while a tool is armed, kept ones in the accent and
  dropped ones in the error colour. See [The 3D qualifier](/use/grading/#the-3d-qualifier).
- **Chart**: with **Show chart** on, a 6 by 4 grid with a handle at each corner. Drag a
  corner to move it, or the middle to move the whole chart. The middle of each patch is drawn
  in, where it is read. Each shot keeps its own corners, which are not saved with the
  project.

### The dropper

Some parameters are picked off the picture rather than typed — a colour, or a point. Arm
the dropper from the parameter and a magnifier follows the pointer, with an outline round
the pixels that will be sampled.

- **Shift+scroll** grows the sampled region — one pixel, then 3×3 up to 9×9.
- **The pick is a drag.** The value is previewed as you sweep; the release commits it, as
  one undo step. `Esc` puts back what the drag started from.
