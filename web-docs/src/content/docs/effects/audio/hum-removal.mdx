## What it does

Removes the hum mains electricity puts on a recording, at 50 or 60 Hz and its harmonics.
Each harmonic's level is measured over the whole of the layer's sound and that much is
taken out, in a band **Width** wide. A voice or an instrument either side of the band is
left in place.

With **Find frequency** on, the hum's frequency is found in the layer's own sound, at 50 or
60 Hz, whichever stands out more, and followed as it drifts. Off, the hum is taken to be at
**Frequency**, and follows its keyframes. Find frequency and Width are read once for the
whole layer, and keyframes on them are not followed.

The display above the controls marks the hum and its harmonics on the sound arriving around
the playhead, with the frequency under it, where most of the harmonics agree on one near 50 or
60 Hz. Where speech covers the hum the display may show none, though the effect, which reads
the whole layer, still takes it out. With Find frequency off, it marks and names Frequency.

## What each control does

- **Find frequency**: finds the hum's frequency in the sound, and follows it as it drifts.
- **Frequency**: the hum's frequency, in hertz, while Find frequency is off.
- **Harmonics**: how many of the hum's harmonics are removed, the hum itself included.
- **Width**: how wide a band is taken out around each harmonic, in hertz.
- **Reduction**: how far the hum is turned down, in dB.
- **Slope**: how much less each harmonic is turned down than the one below it, in dB.
- **Hum only**: plays what is taken away.

**Effect ▸ Only what is removed** (`Alt+N`) switches **Hum only**.
