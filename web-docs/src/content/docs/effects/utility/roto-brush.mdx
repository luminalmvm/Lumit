## What it does

Turns a few strokes into a matte and carries it through the shot. Stroke the subject
with the Roto brush tool (`Alt+W`) — hold `Alt` to stroke the background — and the
matte appears on that frame, which becomes the **base frame**. **Propagate** carries it
outward from the base in both directions, following the shot's motion. Where it drifts,
stroke that frame and the correction carries onward from there.

Strokes live on the source footage, not the composition, so the matte survives
transforms, retimes and preview resolutions, and serves every composition that cuts
the same shot.

Propagation is a background job and currently takes roughly a second per 1080p frame.
You can keep working while it runs; the effect's card shows how far the span reaches.

For the working loop, see [Cutting out a subject](/tutorials/roto-brush/).

## What each control does

- **Propagate**: carries the matte outward from the base frame. Only one propagation
  runs at a time.
- **Cancel**: stops the job and keeps the frames already solved; a later Propagate
  resumes from them.
- **Matte mode**: keep the subject, or drop it.
- **View**: the cut-out picture, the matte itself, or the picture with the matte's
  edge drawn over it.
- **Refine radius**: how wide the softened band round the matte's edge is — room for
  hair, motion blur and smoke. The Refine edge tool paints the band wider where one
  spot needs more.
- **Flow resolution**: the resolution the propagation's motion is measured at. Native
  is slower and rarely better.
- **Flow smoothness**: how strongly that motion is regularised.

## Notes

Frames the propagation has not reached render untouched — the effect never holds a
neighbouring frame's matte over them.
