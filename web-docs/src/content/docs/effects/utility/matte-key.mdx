Matte key is a [Utility](/effects/utility/) effect.

## What it is for

Lumit's keyer. It drives transparency down wherever a pixel matches a chosen
**screen colour**, so a subject shot against green or blue can be composited over
something else.

It is a colour-difference keyer, which is the kind professional keyers use: it
judges each pixel by how far its screen channel stands above the other two,
rather than by simple distance from a colour. That is what lets it hold fine
detail — hair, motion blur, the semi-transparent edge of a fast-moving arm — that
a cruder key turns into a hard cut-out.

The default settings key a typical green screen the moment you add the effect.
Blue screens work too, without changing anything but the screen colour: whichever
channel is largest in the colour you pick becomes the one being keyed.

**Use the View control.** Judging a key by looking at the composite is the single
most common way to end up with a bad one. Screen matte shows the transparency
itself as black and white, where every hole and every soft patch is obvious, and
Status tints the uncertain middle so at-risk areas stand out.

The usual order of work: set the screen colour with the dropper, raise the gain
until the screen goes properly black on the Screen matte view, check the
foreground is still solid white, then use the clips to clean up what remains, and
finally set despill so no green light is left on the subject.

Follow it with [Light wrap](/effects/stylise/light-wrap/), which spills the new
background's light back around the subject's edges and does more for the
believability of a composite than any amount of further keying.

:::note[The spatial controls are not here yet]
Screen pre-blur, shrink and grow, screen softness and despot removal are
specified but not yet built, as are the inside and outside garbage masks. Until
they arrive, use [masks](/use/masks/) on the layer to hold out areas the key
should not touch.
:::

## What each control does

- **View** — what the Viewer shows. Final result is the keyed picture; Screen
  matte shows the transparency as greyscale, white where the picture is kept and
  black where it is removed; Status shows the same thing as a heat map with the
  uncertain mid-tones tinted, so edges and holes at risk of tearing stand out.
  Set this back to Final result before you export.
- **Screen colour** — the colour being removed. Sample it off the screen itself
  with the dropper, from a mid-brightness area rather than a bright or shadowed
  corner. Its largest channel decides which primary is treated as the screen, so
  a blue screen needs nothing else changed.
- **Screen gain** — how aggressively the matte falls off. Raise it until the
  screen is genuinely black on the Screen matte view; too far and it starts
  eating into the foreground's edges.
- **Screen balance** — how the two non-screen channels are weighted against each
  other when judging a pixel. This is the control that fixes a key which is
  almost right but holds on to a particular colour of clothing or skin. Its ends
  take the lower and the higher of the two; the middle takes their average.
- **Despill bias** — shifts the colour the despill pulls the screen channel down
  to. Neutral does nothing; tinting it is how you stop despilled skin going grey
  or magenta.
- **Alpha bias** — shifts what counts as neutral when judging the matte itself.
  Neutral does nothing. Reach for it when the whole plate carries a cast that is
  confusing the key.
- **Despill amount** — how much screen colour is drained out of the subject. Green
  light bouncing off a screen lands on everything in front of it, and leaving it
  there is the loudest tell of a bad composite.
- **Mix** — how much of the keyed result replaces the original.

The **Screen matte** group cleans up what the key leaves:

- **Clip black** — the matte level at or below which a pixel becomes fully
  transparent. Raise it to close translucent patches left in the screen area.
- **Clip white** — the level at or above which a pixel becomes fully opaque. Lower
  it to make a foreground that has gone slightly transparent solid again.
- **Clip rollback** — eases both clips back toward the unclipped matte. Clipping is
  blunt and takes fine edge detail with it; this is how you recover some of the
  hair the clips just removed.
- **Replace method** — what colour is put where spill was removed. Source keeps the
  original colour, Hard colour and Soft colour blend in the replace colour below —
  Soft in proportion to the pixel's brightness, which is the gentler of the two —
  and None simply leaves the despilled colour as it fell.
- **Replace colour** — the colour those two methods blend in. Setting it to
  something sampled from the new background is a quick way to make edges agree
  with the plate they are being composited onto.
