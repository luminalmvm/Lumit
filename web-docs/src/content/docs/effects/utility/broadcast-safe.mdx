Broadcast safe is a **Utility** effect.

## What it is for

Analogue television carried brightness and colour along a single wire, added
together, and a transmitter would distort or refuse a signal whose total swung
too far. A saturated yellow is the classic offender: its brightness is already
nearly white, and its colour adds a large amount on top, so the two together
reach about a third again past the legal peak — even though neither half is
unusual on its own.

This effect measures that total for every pixel and either pulls the pixel back
until it is legal, drains colour out of it until it is, or simply shows you which
pixels the problem is in. It is a delivery tool rather than a look, which is why
it sits here with [Set matte](/effects/utility/set-matte/) rather than with the
colour effects.

## What each control does

- **Standard** — which television system's signal is being measured. NTSC's black
  sits a little way up the scale (the "setup" pedestal) and PAL's does not, so the
  same Maximum signal number means a slightly different thing in each. Pick the
  one your delivery specification names.
- **How to treat** — what happens to a pixel that is over the limit.
  - **Reduce brightness** darkens it until it fits, keeping its colour. This is
    the safe default and the one that usually looks right.
  - **Reduce saturation** drains colour out of it until it fits, keeping its
    brightness. Better for graphics, where a logo's brightness matters more than
    its exact hue.
  - **Key out unsafe** removes the illegal pixels, leaving the legal picture with
    holes in it — a diagnostic view.
  - **Key out safe** removes the legal ones, leaving *only* the problem. Put this
    on an adjustment layer above your shot and the offending areas stand out.
- **Maximum signal** — the largest total allowed, in IRE. 110 is the usual
  broadcaster's figure and the default; 100 is the strict limit; above 110 is for
  a delivery spec that explicitly allows more.
- **Mix** — how much of the clamped picture replaces the original. Leave it at
  100 for a real delivery; anything less is not safe.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — limit the clamp to a region; see
  [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Put it last.** This belongs at the very end of the chain, on an adjustment
layer above everything, after the grade and after every title. Anything applied
after it can push the signal back out of range.

**Check before you fix.** Set **How to treat** to *Key out safe* first: whatever
is left on screen is what is illegal. If it is a corner of a logo, fix the logo;
if it is half the shot, fix the grade. Reaching straight for a clamp hides the
problem rather than solving it.

**Reducing saturation cannot rescue a pixel that is simply too bright.** If the
brightness alone is over the limit, the colour runs all the way out and the pixel
is still hot — you get a legal-luma grey that is not legal. Use Reduce brightness,
or go back and fix the grade. The effect does not pretend otherwise.

**This is a limiter, not a legaliser for HDR.** It measures an analogue composite
amplitude, which is the specification most broadcasters still quote. It is not a
substitute for a proper HDR delivery pass.

**A frame that is already legal comes back untouched.** Both repair modes leave a
pixel under the limit exactly as it arrived, so leaving this effect on a shot that
does not need it costs nothing but the pass.
