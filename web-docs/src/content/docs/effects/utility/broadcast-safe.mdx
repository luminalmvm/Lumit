## What it does

Analogue television carried brightness and colour along a single wire, and a 
transmitter distorts or refuses a signal where the total peak is too great.

This effect measures the peak for every pixel. It can then pull pixels back
to legal values, or display which pixels aren't legal.

## What each control does

- **Standard**: which television system's signal is being measured.
- **How to treat**: what happens to a pixel that is over the limit.
  - **Reduce brightness** darkens until it fits, keeping colour.
  - **Reduce saturation** drains colour until it fits, keeping brightness.
  - **Key out unsafe** removes illegal pixels and leaves transparent holes.
  - **Key out safe** removes legal ones, leaving *only* illegal pixels.
- **Maximum signal**: the largest total allowed, in IRE.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).
