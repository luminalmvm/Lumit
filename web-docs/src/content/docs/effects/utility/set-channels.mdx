## What it does

Four dropdowns, one per output channel, each saying where that channel is fetched from:
one of this layer's own four channels or its brightness, the same five off a **Source**
layer you pick, or flat on and flat off. It is how a depth pass is moved into the alpha,
how a luma pass becomes a red channel, and how a colour is thrown away without a Fill.

At its defaults it copies each channel to itself, so adding it changes nothing until you
tell a channel where to come from.

## What each control does

- **Source**: the other layer the five **Source** options read. Leave it unset and those
  options read nothing at all — black, and clear in the alpha.
- **Red from**, **Green from**, **Blue from**, **Alpha from**: where each output channel
  comes from. The first five options are this layer's own red, green, blue, alpha and
  brightness; the next five are the Source layer's same five; **Full on** and **Full off**
  are 1 and 0 everywhere.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's
  brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).

Note that the Source layer may be this layer, which is worth knowing when you are
converting an After Effects project: After Effects gives every output channel its own
layer picker, and Lumit's one Source row plus the five *this layer* options cover the same
ground for every project we have measured.
