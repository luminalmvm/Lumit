Linear wipe is a **Transition** effect.

## What it is for

Taking the layer away along a straight line. **Completion** is how far the line
has travelled — keyframe it from 0 to 100 and the layer leaves the frame; from
100 to 0 and it arrives.

It is the plainest transition there is, and the most useful, because the two
controls beside Completion turn it into a dozen different moves. Angle it and it
becomes a diagonal sweep. Feather it and it becomes a soft dissolve with a
direction. Put a second one on the layer at the opposite angle and you have a
band that opens.

It is also not only a transition. A hard-edged Linear wipe at a fixed Completion
is a **split-screen** — the fastest way to reveal half of one shot over another
— and one with a wide feather is a directional falloff for anything you want to
fade out towards an edge.

## What each control does

- **Wipe centre x** and **Wipe centre y** — where the line pivots. At the
  default (the middle of the frame) Completion 50 puts the edge dead centre.
  Moving the centre shifts where "halfway" is, which is how you make a wipe
  linger over one part of the shot.
- **Completion** — how far the edge has travelled, 0 to 100. At 0 the whole
  frame is there, exactly; at 100 none of it is. It starts at 50 so that
  dropping the effect on a layer shows you what it does.
- **Wipe angle** — which way the edge runs, and therefore which side goes first.
  **Measured from straight up, turning clockwise**: at 90° (the default) the
  edge is vertical and the **left** half goes first; at 270° it is vertical and
  the right half goes; at 0° the top goes first.
- **Feather** — how soft the edge is, in pixels. At 0 it is a hard line. Wide
  feathers turn the wipe into a gradient sweep, and are how a transition stops
  reading as a wipe and starts reading as light passing over the shot.
- **Mix** — how much of the wipe takes effect. It is not the same control as
  Completion: Completion moves the edge, Mix fades the *whole* result back
  towards the untouched frame, so at Mix 50 the removed half is half there.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The wipe genuinely starts and finishes.** At Completion 0 the frame is
untouched to the last bit even with a wide feather, and at 100 it is completely
gone. That takes a little care internally — the edge has to travel slightly past
each end of the frame — and it is what stops a soft wipe from leaving a faint
ghost at the end of the keyframe pair.

**Ease the keyframes.** A linear Completion ramp is the most obvious-looking
transition in the world. An Easy ease at both ends, or an ease out of the last
key only, is what makes it feel deliberate.
