Card wipe is a **Transition** effect.

## What it is for

Taking the layer away as a grid of cards, each one turning edge-on and
disappearing. A wave of flips crosses the frame in the order you choose, and
because each card turns in perspective — the near edge growing, the far edge
shrinking, the picture on it sliding as it goes — the whole thing reads as a
departures board changing rather than as a grid of rectangles fading out.

Keyframe **Completion** from 0 to 100 and the shot flips away. The two controls
that decide what it feels like are **Transition width**, which says how much the
cards overlap in time, and **Randomness**, which shuffles the order: at 0 the
wave is a clean sweep, at 100 the cards go in no order at all and it reads as a
scatter.

## What each control does

- **Completion** — how far through the wipe we are, 0 to 100. At 0 the frame is
  untouched exactly; at 100 every card has gone. It starts at 50 so the effect
  shows you what it does the moment you add it.
- **Transition width** — how much of the whole wipe one card's own flip takes.
  At 100 every card turns together and the effect is a single move; at a few per
  cent they go strictly one after another and it is a hard travelling wave. The
  middle is where it looks like a departures board.
- **Rows** and **Columns** — how many cards there are. A coarse grid reads as
  panels turning, a fine one as a texture dissolving. Both go past their sliders
  by typing, up to 256.
- **Flip axis** — which line each card turns about. **Horizontal axis** tips the
  card's top and bottom edges (a departures-board flap); **Vertical axis** swings
  its left and right edges (a page turning). **Random** picks one per card from
  the Seed, which stops the grid reading as a machine.
- **Flip direction** — which way each card turns. **Forwards** brings the leading
  edge towards you, so it grows before the card vanishes; **Backwards** sends it
  away. **Random** picks per card. The difference is visible because the cards
  are drawn in perspective — with a flat projection this control would do
  nothing.
- **Flip order** — which side the wave starts from.
- **Randomness** — how far each card's turn is shuffled away from Flip order. At
  0 the order is exactly what Flip order says, which is After Effects' default;
  at 100 it is entirely the Seed's.
- **Seed** — which shuffle you get, and which way the Random axis and direction
  fall. Same seed, same answer, on any machine and in any render.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold, as distinct from how far the wipe
  has got. See [Linear wipe](/effects/transition/linear-wipe/).
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**There is no camera, and that is deliberate.** After Effects' Card Wipe carries
a whole camera system — camera position, corner pins, lights, materials, position
and rotation jitter — so that its grid can be looked at from an angle. Lumit
keeps cameras on the composition rather than inside an effect, so every card here
is drawn in its own little frame from the same fixed viewing distance. What you
lose is the ability to fly around the grid; what you keep is the flip itself, its
direction, and a control set you can read in one glance. An imported After
Effects instance converts its grid, timing and flip settings and reports the
camera controls as not carried.

**There is no back layer either.** In After Effects a card that passes ninety
degrees shows whatever you put on the Back Layer, and the default is nothing.
Lumit's cards turn to nothing, which is that default exactly. To flip *to*
something, put the second layer underneath and let the cards reveal it.

**Cards never overlap.** Each one is drawn inside its own cell, which is what
keeps the grid clean. At shallow angles a card's near edge would reach about six
per cent past its cell and is trimmed there instead — briefly, and while the card
is nearly full, so it is not something you will catch.
