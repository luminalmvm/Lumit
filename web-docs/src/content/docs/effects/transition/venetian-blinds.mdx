Venetian blinds is a **Transition** effect.

## What it is for

Taking the layer away in stripes. The frame is cut into slats of equal width and
every slat opens from its own middle at the same moment, so the picture closes
the way a window blind does.

As a transition it wants **Completion** keyframed from 0 to 100 and it is done —
one of the two or three moves that reads instantly as a change of scene without
anybody having to think about it. At a fixed value it is something else
entirely: a rank of hard stripes over a title, a shutter, a set of louvres to
put a light behind.

It is [Linear wipe](/effects/transition/linear-wipe/) repeated. Everything that
wipe does — the direction convention, the clean start and finish, the feather —
works here in exactly the same way; the only difference is that there are many
edges instead of one.

## What each control does

- **Completion** — how far the slats have closed, 0 to 100. At 0 the frame is
  untouched exactly; at 100 it is gone. It starts at 50 so the effect shows you
  what it does the moment you add it, and at 50 exactly half of every slat has
  gone.
- **Direction** — which way the slats run, measured from straight up and turning
  clockwise. At 0° they are horizontal and the frame closes from the top and
  bottom of each slat; at 90° they are vertical.
- **Width** — how wide one slat is, in pixels at composition size. Small numbers
  give a fine grille; large ones give a few broad bands, which is usually the
  better transition because the eye can follow it.
- **Feather** — how soft each slat's edges are, in pixels. A feather wider than
  the slat itself turns the effect into a gentle overall fade, which is
  occasionally what you want and more often a sign the width is too small.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold, as distinct from how far the
  blinds have closed. See [Linear wipe](/effects/transition/linear-wipe/).
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**There is no centre, and there does not need to be.** The slats sit on the
middle of the frame. Moving a rank of identical stripes by one slat gives back
exactly the picture you started with, so a centre control would only ever be a
way of nudging the phase — Direction and Width are the whole geometry. (After
Effects has no centre here either.)

**Width is measured at composition size**, not in raster pixels, so a
Half-resolution preview shows the same slats as the export rather than twice as
many.
