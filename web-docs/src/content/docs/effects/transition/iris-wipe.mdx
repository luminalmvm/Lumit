Iris wipe is a **Transition** effect.

## What it is for

Opening a hole in the layer. The hole is a regular polygon — six sides by
default, up to thirty-two — and switching **Use inner radius** on pulls every
other corner inwards, which turns it into a star.

It is the one transition in the family with no Completion, because it does not
need one: **the radius is the transition**. Keyframe **Outer radius** from 0 to
something larger than the frame and the layer opens out of its middle; reverse
the keyframes and it closes on it. That is how the shutter irises of old film
titles were made, and it is still the quickest way to point at the centre of a
shot.

At a fixed radius it stops being a transition at all and becomes a shape: a
hexagonal or star-shaped hole to put another layer through, or — with the whole
thing on a solid and used as a matte — a star you can animate the points of.

## What each control does

- **Iris centre x** and **Iris centre y** — where the hole opens. A fresh
  instance starts in the middle of the composition.
- **Iris points** — how many corners the polygon has, 6 to 32. Six is a
  recognisable hexagon; by about twenty it is indistinguishable from a circle
  unless the radius is very large. With **Use inner radius** on, this is the
  number of *points on the star*, so twelve points means a twelve-pointed star.
- **Outer radius** — how far the corners reach, as a per cent of the composition
  diagonal. This is the control that animates. At 0 the frame is untouched
  exactly.
- **Use inner radius** — turns the polygon into a star by pulling every other
  corner in to **Inner radius**.
- **Inner radius** — where those pulled-in corners sit. The closer it is to zero
  the sharper and thinner the star's points become; set it *above* Outer radius
  and the star turns inside out, which is a perfectly good shape and occasionally
  a useful one.
- **Rotation** — which way the shape is turned, from straight up and clockwise.
  Worth animating slowly under a growing radius: a turning iris reads as
  mechanical, a still one as graphic.
- **Feather** — how soft the edge is, in pixels. It is a true width measured
  across the edge, so it stays the same thickness all the way round, including
  in the crooks of a star's points.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold, as distinct from how far the iris
  has opened. See [Linear wipe](/effects/transition/linear-wipe/).
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The iris removes what is inside it.** That is the way an iris wipe has always
worked, and it is After Effects' behaviour too. To reveal *through* the shape
instead, the Matte row's Invert is the wrong tool — it inverts the strength of
the effect, not the shape it makes. Put this effect on a solid above the layer
and use [Set matte](/effects/utility/set-matte/), or simply push Outer radius
past the frame so only the star's points are left.

**The radii are per cents of the diagonal and the centre is in pixels**, which
looks inconsistent and is not. A radius is a *size*, and a size ought to survive
someone reframing the composition from 1080p to 4K; a centre is a *place*, and a
place is something you click on. [Twirl](/effects/distortion/twirl/) makes the
same split for the same reason.
