Radial wipe is a **Transition** effect.

## What it is for

Taking the layer away around a circle. A wedge opens from **Start angle** and
sweeps until it has swallowed the frame — the clock-hand wipe, and the one every
countdown timer, loading dial and radial progress bar is made of.

As a transition it is the obvious use: keyframe **Completion** from 0 to 100 and
the shot spins away. But it earns its place at fixed values just as often, on
top of a shape layer, as a dial: put it on a ring and Completion *is* the
percentage.

**Both** turns it into a pair of curtains opening from the start ray, which is a
different move again — good for revealing something in the centre of frame.

## What each control does

- **Wipe centre x** and **Wipe centre y** — where the hand pivots. Off-centre
  wipes read as light sweeping past rather than as a clock, and are worth trying
  when the shot has an obvious source of light in it.
- **Completion** — how far round the sweep has got, 0 to 100. At 0 the frame is
  untouched exactly; at 100 it is gone. It starts at 50 so the effect shows you
  what it does the moment you add it.
- **Start angle** — where the sweep begins, measured from straight up and
  turning clockwise. 0° starts at twelve o'clock, which is what a dial wants.
- **Wipe** — which way the wedge opens. **Clockwise** and **Anticlockwise** open
  one way from the start ray; **Both** opens symmetrically either side of it, so
  the removed area is a wedge centred on Start angle. All three remove the same
  amount of the circle at the same Completion.
- **Feather** — how soft the sweeping edge is, in pixels measured across the
  arc. Because it is measured at the arc rather than as an angle, the soft edge
  stays the same thickness as it sweeps outward instead of fanning open.
- **Mix** — how much of the wipe takes effect, as distinct from how far it has
  swept. See [Linear wipe](/effects/transition/linear-wipe/).
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The middle of a heavily feathered wipe is mush.** A feather is a fixed width
measured across the arc, and near the centre an arc of any fixed width wraps the
whole circle — so the soft edge has nowhere to go and the few pixels at the
pivot smear. That is geometry rather than a fault, and it is true of every
radial wipe in every application. If it shows, move the centre off frame or drop
the feather.

**For a progress dial, put it on a shape.** A Radial wipe on a footage layer is
a transition; on a ring or an arc drawn as a shape layer it is a readout, and
Completion can be driven straight from an expression.
