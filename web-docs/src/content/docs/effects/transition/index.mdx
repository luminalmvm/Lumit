## What this family does

These effects take the picture away, a bit at a time. Every other family changes
what a frame looks like; a transition changes **how much of it there is**, on a
number the timeline animates — so a cut can be made out of a movement.

That number is nearly always called **Completion**, it runs 0 to 100, and 0
always means "the frame, untouched". Keyframe it upward and the layer leaves;
keyframe it downward and the layer arrives. Everything else in a transition
effect decides the *shape* of the taking:
[Linear wipe](/effects/transition/linear-wipe/) uses a straight line at an
angle, [Radial wipe](/effects/transition/radial-wipe/) a wedge sweeping round a
centre, [Venetian blinds](/effects/transition/venetian-blinds/) a rank of slats
closing at once, and [Card wipe](/effects/transition/card-wipe/) a grid of cards
turning edge-on one wave at a time.

[Iris wipe](/effects/transition/iris-wipe/) is the exception, and it is worth
knowing why: its shape is a polygon opening out of a point, so the thing you
animate is the **radius**. Keyframe that instead and everything above still
applies.

They are not only for transitions, which is worth saying because the name gets
in the way. Held at a fixed value, a Linear wipe is a split-screen, a Radial
wipe is a progress dial, Venetian blinds is a grille, and an Iris wipe is a star
you can animate the points of. None of those is a trick.

**Ease the keyframes.** A transition on a linear ramp is the most obvious-looking
move in motion graphics. It is the easing, not the shape, that makes one feel
intentional.
