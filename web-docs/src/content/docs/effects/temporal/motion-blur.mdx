Motion blur is a [Temporal](/effects/temporal/) effect.

## What it is for

The correct motion blur, and the expensive one. Rather than smearing a finished
frame, it renders the whole scene beneath it several times at moments *inside*
one frame and averages the results.

Because every sample is a real render, everything moves in it: footage motion,
animated layer transforms, animated effect parameters, the camera. There are no
artefacts from blurring something that should not have been blurred, and no
guessing about where a pixel came from.

Its natural home is an adjustment layer over a whole montage. There, the scene
below is what re-renders, and every moving thing in it blurs together as one
picture.

The cost is roughly the sample count times a full render of everything
underneath, which makes it the heaviest thing in the application. Work with a low
sample count and raise it before you export.

For the cheap kind that smears a finished frame instead, use
[Fast motion blur](/effects/temporal/fast-motion-blur/). For one layer's own
transform only, the layer's motion blur switch is free — see
[Layers](/use/layers/).

Temporal effects inside the scene being sampled — an
[Echo](/effects/temporal/echo/), a
[Datamosh](/effects/distortion/datamosh/), a Fast motion blur — hold still across
the samples rather than being sampled through. That is a documented boundary, not
a fault.

## What each control does

- **Samples** — how many times the scene below is rendered inside one frame. This
  is the quality-against-cost dial, and it is the whole expense of the effect. Too
  few and fast movement shows as separate stepped copies rather than a smooth
  smear; the faster the motion, the more you need.
- **Shutter angle** — how much of the frame the imaginary shutter is open for, as
  a fraction of a full turn. The film-standard half-turn covers half the frame's
  movement, which is the natural-looking setting; larger values smear further and
  smaller ones sharpen toward no blur at all.
- **Shutter phase** — where that open window sits relative to the frame's own
  moment. The default centres the blur on the frame, so a moving object is
  smeared equally either side of where it nominally is rather than trailing
  behind or running ahead of itself.
- **Force on all layers** — turns on every layer's own transform motion blur for
  the duration of each sample render, using this effect's shutter settings. It
  saves toggling the switch on every layer individually, and because each sample
  is itself smeared the result stays smooth at lower sample counts. The document
  itself is not changed; the switches only apply inside the sampling.
- **Mix** — blends the averaged, blurred result against the ordinary frame, which
  is a way to ease the blur back without changing the shutter.
