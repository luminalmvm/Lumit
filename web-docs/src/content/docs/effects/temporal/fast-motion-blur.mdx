Fast motion blur is a [Temporal](/effects/temporal/) effect.

## What it is for

Game capture has no natural motion blur. Every frame is a perfectly sharp instant,
which is the reason engine footage reads as a game and not as film, and it is
what makes fast movement strobe rather than flow.

This effect measures where every pixel moved between one frame and the next, and
smears each one along its own path. Unlike the layer's motion blur switch, which
only knows about the layer's own animated transform, this catches motion that is
*inside* the picture: a player running across frame, a car passing, a camera whip
that was recorded rather than keyframed.

It is the cheap kind. For the correct, whole-scene kind that re-renders
everything at sub-frame moments, use
[Motion blur](/effects/temporal/motion-blur/) — at several times the cost.

Measured motion is never perfect. Where the measurement is unsure — at the edges
of moving objects, where things pass behind one another — the effect does not
switch off and leave a sharp patch in the middle of a blurred frame. It borrows
the motion of the surrounding area instead, so uncertain pixels follow their
neighbourhood. Blur therefore survives everywhere except where the neighbourhood
itself is genuinely still, which is where you want sharpness anyway.

It works on footage layers, since it needs real frames to measure between.

:::note[One flow measurement per layer]
Measuring motion is expensive, so a layer computes it once per frame and shares
it. If a layer carries both this and
[Datamosh](/effects/distortion/datamosh/), whichever comes first in the stack
gets the measurement and the other does nothing. Put them on separate layers if
you need both.
:::

## What each control does

- **Shutter angle** — how long the imaginary shutter is open, and so how long the
  streak is. The streak covers that fraction of a full turn of the movement
  between frames, which means the film-standard half-turn gives you half the
  inter-frame motion — the setting that looks like footage rather than like an
  effect. Larger angles smear further; a full turn and beyond is a stylised
  choice.
- **Samples** — the *cap* on how many taps the smear may spend. The effect already
  spends fewer on short streaks, so this is a ceiling for the expensive frames
  rather than a fixed cost on every one. Raise it if long streaks show as
  separate stacked copies; lower it to buy back time.
- **Quality** — High halves the spacing between taps and bends each streak to
  follow the curve of the motion rather than running straight. On rotating or
  swirling movement the difference is obvious; on straight-line motion it is
  slight.
- **View** — a diagnostic display. Motion vectors shows the measured movement as
  colour, Confidence shows in greyscale where the measurement is trusted, and
  Dominant motion shows the neighbourhood motion an uncertain pixel borrows.
  These are the fastest way to understand why a region smeared the way it did.
  Leave it on Rendered for output.
- **Mix** — how much of the smeared picture replaces the original.
