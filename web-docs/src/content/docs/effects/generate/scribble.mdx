Scribble is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

Shading a shape in by hand. You draw a mask; Scribble fills it with parallel
pencil strokes at whatever angle you choose, running a little past the edges the
way a hand does and wavering as they go.

It is the fastest route to anything that should look drawn rather than rendered:
a highlight scribbled over a word, a rough fill behind a title, an annotation
that appears to have been done with a marker. Keyframe **End** from 0 and the
shading draws itself on, stroke by stroke, because the strokes are one continuous
line rather than a stack of separate ones.

Scribble reads the mask's **shape**, not the hole it cuts. That is why it needs a
mask rather than a matte: a matte says which pixels are inside, and a hatch has to
know where the boundary *runs* to know where each stroke stops.

## What each control does

- **Mask** — which of the layer's masks to fill. **First mask** means whichever
  is first, so you can add the effect before you draw the shape. A mask set to
  mode **None** is offered like any other: that mode means "drawn, but gates
  nothing", which is exactly the mask you draw for an effect to use.
- **Colour** — the pencil's colour. Values above 1 make it bloom under a
  [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/).
- **Angle** — which way the strokes run.
- **Stroke width** — how thick one pencil stroke is, in pixels at composition
  size.
- **Spacing** — how far apart the strokes are laid. Below the stroke width they
  merge into a solid fill; well above it the shape reads as open hatching.
- **Path overlap** — how far each stroke runs past the mask's edge. Positive is
  the loose, hand-drawn look; negative keeps the strokes short of the edge, which
  reads as deliberate and neat.
- **Start** and **End** — where the pen begins and stops, as percentages of the
  whole drawing's length. Keyframe **End** from 0 to draw it on.
- **Wiggle type** — how the waver moves. **Static** holds one arrangement;
  **Jagged** snaps to a new one several times a second, which is the pencil-test
  look; **Wiggly** drifts between them continuously.
- **Wiggles per second** — how fast it moves. Greyed while the type is Static,
  which is not moving at all.
- **Seed** — which waver. Two Scribbles on one shape with different seeds are two
  different hands.
- **Opacity** — how strong the pencil is.
- **Composite on original** — on, the layer that arrived stays under the
  scribble. Off, the scribble is all there is, which turns the fill into its own
  element.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The strokes are one continuous line.** The pen crosses the shape, hops down the
edge and comes back, which is what makes Start and End behave like somebody
drawing rather than like a pair of clipping edges. Where a shape has a notch in
it the pen lifts rather than flying across the gap, so a C or a star shades
correctly.

**The waver is always there, and it is tied to the Spacing.** There is no
amplitude control because the waver must never walk one stroke into its
neighbour: it is a fixed fraction of the gap you set, so it stays proportionate
whatever density you shade at. **Static** does not mean "no waver" — it means the
waver holds still.

**A very fine hatch opens its own spacing.** There is a ceiling on how many
strokes one Scribble can lay down. Past it the spacing widens slightly rather
than the fill stopping half way down the shape, so you get a slightly coarser
whole picture instead of half of a fine one.

**After Effects' extras are not carried.** Its Curviness, the three Variation
sliders and the multi-mask Fill Types have no counterpart here — the single
waver does the job of the variations, and a fill spanning several masks at once
needs a control that names more than one, which Lumit's does not.
