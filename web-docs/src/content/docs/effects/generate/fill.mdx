Fill is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

Flooding a layer with a single colour without losing what makes it that layer.

The shape stays: a title keeps its letterforms, a mask keeps its curve, a keyed
subject keeps its outline, and every soft edge stays soft. Only the colour is
replaced. That is because the layer's transparency is what decides where the
colour goes, and Fill never touches it — a pixel that was half covered comes out
half covered in the new colour, which is why the result has clean edges rather
than the jagged stencil a threshold would give.

Reach for it when the same artwork is wanted in a different colour and you would
rather not go back to the source, when a shape needs to become a flat silhouette,
or when a keyed element has to be reduced to a solid so it can be blurred into a
shadow.

If you want the colour to *vary* across the frame rather than sit flat, that is
[Gradient](/effects/generate/gradient/); if you want to keep the picture and just
push it toward a colour, that is [Tint](/effects/colour/tint/).

## What each control does

- **Colour** — the colour the layer becomes. It is scene-linear and the range
  runs past 1, so a fill can be brighter than white and will bloom under a
  [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/) the way a real light source would. The colour
  swatch's own transparency is ignored; the layer decides that.
- **Mix** — how much of the fill replaces the original. At 0 nothing changes; at
  50 the layer is half its own colour and half the fill, which is the way to
  ease a fill in over time. This is where an imported After Effects Fill's
  Opacity ends up — the two are the same number.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Filling one mask rather than the whole layer** is not something this effect
does. After Effects' Fill can be aimed at a single mask and then needs its own
feather controls to soften the hard mask edge that results. The same picture here
comes from putting the Fill on the layer that carries the shape, and softness
from a [Gaussian blur](/effects/blur-sharpen/gaussian-blur/) after it. An
imported mask-targeted Fill is reported rather than approximated.
