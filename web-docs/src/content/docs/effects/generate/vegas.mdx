Vegas is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

Outlining what is in the picture, and then making that outline *move*. It finds a
contour — the line where the picture's brightness, or its transparency, crosses a
level you choose — and runs a stroke along it. Set **Length** below 100 and the
stroke becomes dashes; keyframe **Rotation** and the dashes march along the
contour like the lights around a theatre sign, which is what the effect is named
for.

It is the quickest way to make a shape announce itself: a logo traced by a
running highlight, a diagram whose lines light up in turn, a technical readout
drawing itself around the thing it is measuring.

Point **Source** at Alpha and it outlines the layer's own shape rather than
anything in its picture, which is usually what you want on a title or a logo.

Point it at **Mask/Path** and it marches round a mask you have drawn instead of
hunting for a contour at all — and that half is the more precise of the two, for
the reason in the notes below.

## What each control does

- **Source** — **Luminance** finds the contour in the picture's brightness;
  **Alpha** finds it at the edge of the layer's own shape; **Mask/Path** ignores
  the picture and runs the stroke round one of the layer's masks.
- **Mask** — which mask, while Source is Mask/Path. **First mask** means whichever
  is first, so you can add the effect before you draw the shape. A mask set to
  mode **None** is offered like any other.
- **Threshold** — which contour. On Luminance it is a position on the tone range,
  so 50 traces the mid-greys and lower values trace the darker parts; on Alpha,
  50 sits exactly on the shape's edge. Greyed on Mask/Path, where there is no
  picture to take a level of.
- **Width** — how thick the stroke is, in pixels at composition size.
- **Hardness** — how crisp its edges are. 100 is a hard-edged stroke, 0 one that
  fades out over its own width.
- **Segment length** — one dash and its gap together, in pixels at composition
  size. This is where After Effects' *number* of segments becomes a length, for
  the reason in the notes below — except on Mask/Path, where the two mean the
  same thing.
- **Length** — how much of one segment is lit, as a percentage. 100 is a
  continuous outline with no gaps at all.
- **Rotation** — marches the dashes along the contour. One full turn moves them
  on by exactly one segment, so a straight keyframe from 0° to 360° over a second
  advances them by one segment a second, and looping it is seamless.
- **Colour** — the stroke's colour. Values above 1 make it bloom under a
  [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/).
- **Opacity** — how strong the stroke is.
- **Composite on original** — on, the layer that arrived stays under the stroke.
  Off, the stroke is all there is, which turns the outline into its own element.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/). This is how you run the lights round only part of
  a shape.

## Notes

**A soft picture gives a soft result.** The contour is found from the picture's
own gradient, so a heavily compressed or very smooth image gives a stroke that
wanders and breaks up. A small [Gaussian blur](/effects/blur-sharpen/gaussian-blur/)
before Vegas is the standard cure and is exactly what After Effects' Pre-Blur
control is for.

**On a contour, Segment length is a length rather than a count.** After Effects
traces the contour into a path and can therefore count segments *around* it; Lumit
finds the contour where each pixel stands, which is what makes the effect one
cheap pass, and the price is that spacing has to be a distance instead of a
number. On a straight contour the two are the same picture; on a tightly curved
one Lumit's dashes drift in phase where After Effects' stay evenly spaced.

**On a mask, they mean the same thing.** A mask is a real curve, and Lumit
measures the distance round it on the way in — so the Mask/Path source spaces its
dashes evenly all the way round, however tight the bend. This is the half to use
when the dashes must stay regular on a curve, and it is why an imported After
Effects project using Mask/Path converts its Segments count exactly rather than
approximately.

**The contour is found on what the eye sees**, not on raw light, so Threshold
spends its range evenly instead of doing nothing for its first eighty per cent.
