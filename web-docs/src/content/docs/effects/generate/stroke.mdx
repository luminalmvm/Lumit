Stroke is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

Drawing a line on. You draw a mask; Stroke runs a round brush along it, from one
percentage of the way round to another. Keyframe **End** from 0 and the line draws
itself, which is most of what the effect is used for: an underline that sweeps in,
a route tracing across a map, a circle closing around something.

Point **Paint style** at **Reveal original** and the same animation becomes a
wipe: the picture survives only where the brush has been, so a stroke drawing
itself on paints a title into view.

Stroke reads the mask's **line**, not the hole it cuts. A cut-out cannot say which
way is *along* it, and a brush that starts at 20% and stops at 80% has to know.

## What each control does

- **Mask** — which of the layer's masks to walk. **First mask** means whichever
  is first, so you can add the effect before you draw the path. A mask set to mode
  **None** is offered like any other, and is usually what you want here: the shape
  is there for the brush, not to cut the layer out.
- **Colour** — the brush's colour. Values above 1 make it bloom under a
  [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/). Ignored by **Reveal original**, which paints
  with the picture underneath.
- **Brush size** — how wide the brush is, in pixels at composition size. A width,
  not a radius.
- **Brush hardness** — how crisp its edge is. 100 is a hard-edged marker, 0 an
  airbrush that fades out over its own width.
- **Spacing** — how far apart the brush stamps are, as a percentage of the
  brush's width. Leave it low for a solid line; push it past about 50 and the
  stamps come apart into separate dots, which is a deliberate look.
- **Start** and **End** — where the brush begins and stops, as percentages of the
  way round the path. Keyframe **End** from 0 to draw the line on.
- **Paint style** — what the stroke lands on. **On original** paints over the
  picture that arrived; **On transparent** keeps the stroke and drops the picture;
  **Reveal original** keeps the picture *only* where the brush went and hides the
  rest.
- **Opacity** — how strong the brush is.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Spacing changes the shape of the stroke, not just its texture.** Under about
half the brush width the stamps overlap and you get a continuous line — Lumit
draws it as the shape the brush sweeps, which is the same picture for a fraction
of the work. Above that they genuinely separate, and you get the dotted line the
control exists for.

**Brush size is a width.** After Effects' Brush Size is a radius; every length in
Lumit is the thing itself, so a project imported from After Effects has this
number doubled for you.

**Reveal original takes the transparency with it.** What the brush covers keeps
its colour *and* its coverage, so the result composites correctly over whatever
is below. It is a proper wipe, not a brightness trick.

**One mask at a time.** After Effects' All Masks and Stroke Sequentially are not
carried: the mask row names one mask by design. Two Strokes on one layer, each
pointed at its own mask, is the equivalent and gives you separate control of
both.
