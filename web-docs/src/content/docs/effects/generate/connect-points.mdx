## What it does

Wire a producer's teal **Points** socket into this effect and every pair of points closer
together than **Max distance** is joined by a line. Particles drifting past each other web
up and let go again; a [Grid](/effects/generate/grid/) becomes a mesh; a
[Scatter](/effects/generate/scatter/) inside a silhouette becomes a constellation of it.

Nearness is measured on the frame rather than in space, so on a 3D layer two points join
when they look close through the composition's camera.

Each line takes the average of the colours of the two points it joins, so a producer whose
particles change colour as they age still reads along the web. **Colour** multiplies that,
which is why it is white by default.

With nothing wired in, the picture passes straight through and the box carries the
no-stream mark.

## What each control does

- **Max distance**: how far apart two points may be and still be joined. Nought joins
  nothing.
- **Max connections**: the most lines that may meet at any one point, which is what stops a
  dense crowd turning into a solid sheet. A pair is joined only when both ends still have
  room.
- **Taper**: how much a line thins as it lengthens. At nought every line is the same width;
  at 100 a line at exactly Max distance has no width left.
- **Fade**: how much a line fades as it lengthens. At 100 a line at exactly Max distance is
  invisible, so the web comes and goes rather than switching on and off. That is the
  default, because a plexus that pops is the first thing anyone has to go and fix.
- **Width**: the thickness of a line.
- **Feather**: how soft a line's edge is.
- **Colour**: multiplies the colour each line inherits from its two ends. White leaves it
  alone.
- **Max points**: the most points that may be considered. A stream longer than this is
  trimmed to its newest, which bounds the pairing as well as the drawing. A capacity
  declaration, so it does not animate.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the picture underneath.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).
