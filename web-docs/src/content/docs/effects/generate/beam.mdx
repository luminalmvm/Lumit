Beam is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

Drawing a shaft of light across the frame: a laser, a sabre, a searchlight, a
tracer round, the line a satellite draws on a map.

It is two points and a taper. The beam runs from **Start** to **End**, fat at
one end and thin at the other, with the **Inside colour** down its core and the
**Outside colour** around its rim. That taper is what stops it reading as a
drawn line: real light falls off, and a shaft that is the same width all the way
looks like a rectangle.

The other half of the effect is that it can be **fired**. **Time** says how far
the beam's head has travelled and **Length** how far its tail trails behind, so
a short beam with Time keyframed from 0 to 100 shoots across the frame and off
the far side. Both are ordinary controls the timeline animates, which means the
shot you preview is the shot you export.

Put it on a solid or an adjustment layer with **Composite on original** off and
the beam becomes an element in its own right — one you can blur, glow and blend
like any other layer.

## What each control does

- **Start x** and **Start y** — where the beam begins, in pixels at composition
  size. The panel draws the pair as one row with a crosshair you can click into
  the viewer.
- **End x** and **End y** — where it is aimed. At the default Length and Time
  the beam runs the whole way between the two.
- **Length** — how much of that run the beam occupies, as a percentage. 100 is a
  shaft from one point to the other; 15 is a short bolt travelling along the
  same line.
- **Time** — where the beam's **head** has got to along the run. Keyframe it from
  0 to 100 and the beam fires. At 0 nothing is drawn at all, and the layer comes
  through exactly as it arrived.
- **Start thickness** — how wide the beam is at its tail, in pixels at
  composition size.
- **End thickness** — how wide it is at its head. Making the head the thinner of
  the two gives the classic comet; swapping them gives something that reads as
  coming towards you.
- **Softness** — how much of the beam's half-width the two colours cross over
  in. At 0 the shaft is a flat slab of the inside colour and the outside colour
  has nothing to colour, which is what After Effects does at the same setting. At
  30 or so both are clearly visible, which is why that is the default.
- **Inside colour** — the core. Values above 1 are allowed and are what make it
  bloom under a [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/).
- **Outside colour** — the rim. Setting it to a saturated version of the inside
  colour is the usual way to make a beam read as hot.
- **Composite on original** — on, the layer that arrived stays under the beam.
  Off, the beam is all there is, which is what you want on its own solid.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold, as distinct from how far the beam
  has travelled.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The taper runs along the drawn beam**, not along the whole Start-to-End run, so
a short beam is a comet wherever it is rather than only at the end of its travel.
At the default Length of 100 the two are the same thing.

**There is no 3D perspective.** After Effects offers one, which foreshortens the
beam from a camera of its own; Lumit keeps cameras on the composition, so an
imported beam gets the flat version and the difference is reported rather than
guessed at.

**Everything is measured at composition size**, so a Half-resolution preview
shows the same beam as the export rather than one half as thick.
