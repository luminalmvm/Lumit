Gradient is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

Painting a smooth run from one colour to another over the whole frame.

Two points decide it. Everything at the first point is the first colour,
everything at the second is the second, and in between the two are mixed by how
far along you have got. **Linear** measures that along the straight line between
the points, so the ramp runs in whatever direction you drag them. **Radial**
measures distance out from the first point instead, so the ramp becomes rings —
the first colour at the centre, the second at the radius the second point sits
on.

It replaces the frame rather than grading it, so it belongs on a solid, on an
adjustment layer, or on a layer whose picture you no longer want. Backgrounds are
the obvious use; the less obvious and more valuable one is as a **matte source**.
Point another effect's Matte row at a layer carrying a Gradient and that effect
now fades in across the frame — a blur that softens toward one corner, a grade
that only reaches the top of the shot.

## What each control does

- **Shape** — **Linear** runs the ramp along the line between the two points.
  **Radial** runs it outward from Start, with End marking the outer edge.
- **Start x** and **Start y** — where the first colour is. Both are in pixels at
  composition size, so the ramp sits in the same place at every preview
  resolution and after a resize.
- **Start colour** — the colour at that point. Scene-linear and open past 1, so a
  ramp can run out into highlight territory.
- **End x** and **End y** — where the second colour is. In Radial this is a point
  on the outer ring, so dragging it further out makes the ramp wider.
- **End colour** — the colour at that point.
- **Scatter** — jiggles each pixel's position along the ramp by a hair. A long,
  gentle ramp shown on an ordinary display tends to break into visible bands;
  a few per cent of scatter dissolves the bands into grain without moving the
  ramp or softening its ends. Above about 20 it stops being a fix and starts
  being a texture.
- **Seed** — which random pattern the scatter follows. Change it if two
  gradients in the same shot happen to scatter identically.
- **Mix** — how much of the ramp replaces the original picture. This is where an
  imported After Effects Ramp's "Blend with original" lands.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The ramp is even in light, not in appearance.** Colours are blended in
scene-linear light, the space the whole engine works in, which means a
white-to-black ramp spends more of its width looking dark than an eye expects.
That is the correct behaviour and the reason the ramp can be trusted to drive
another effect: half way along really is half the light.

**Start and End in the same place** does not break anything — the ramp has no
length, so the frame fills with one flat colour.
