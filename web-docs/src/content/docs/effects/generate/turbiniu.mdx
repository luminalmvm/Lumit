## What it does

Turbiniu draws a family of strokes along a vector mask on the layer, or a family that runs
from one mask to another, filling the space between them. Each stroke can be a line or a row
of polygon markers, with its own width, coverage, colour and tips, so a few controls give
ribbons, hatching, flowing lines, dotted trails or arrows.

1. Draw one or two masks, open or closed. Set their **Mode** to **None** to use them only as
   guides, without cutting the layer.
2. Add Turbiniu and pick **First path** under **First guide**. Leave **Second path** on
   **First mask** to stack the strokes along the first path, or pick another mask to draw
   them between the two.
3. Set **Stroke count** and **Path resolution**, and under **Mark shape** pick **Strokes** or
   **Markers** in **Draw as**.

The guides follow the masks' keys, so the strokes move as the masks do.

## What each control does

### Strokes

- **Stroke count** is how many strokes there are, and **Path resolution** how many points
  each one is built from.
- **Spacing profile**, under **Stroke spacing**, spreads the strokes between the guides:
  the diagonal spaces them evenly, a curve gathers them on one side.
- **Stroke variation** gives each stroke a little of its own: **Spacing jitter**, **Travel
  jitter**, **Palette jitter**, **Width jitter** and **Coverage jitter**. **Variation seed**
  picks another set.
- **Overlap mode** is how strokes meet: **Source over** covers the ones beneath, **Additive**
  adds their light. **Edge sampling** **Five samples** smooths the edges.
- **Effect amount** fades the strokes only; the layer shows unchanged where there is no
  stroke.

### Guides

- **First guide** and **Second guide** each have **Edge softness**, **Stroke width**,
  **Base coverage** and profiles along the path for width and coverage, with a shift, a
  span and a repeat. A negative span reads its profile backwards.
- **Sample spacing profile** spreads the points along a path, and **Reverse guide** runs it
  the other way.
- Each guide has two waves that push the path sideways. **Wave 1 depth** and **Wave 2
  depth** are in pixels, the rate counts whole waves along the path and the phase is in
  waves. **Wave combine** **Sum** adds the two, **Product** multiplies them.
- **Inherit first guide style** makes the second guide take the first one's style, but keep
  its own **Sample spacing profile** and **Reverse guide**.

### Path warp and heading

- **Path warp** pushes each path across itself with noise: **Warp distance** is how far.
  **Sampling domain** reads the noise in **Image coordinates**, **Path coordinates** or
  **Sample order**. The frequency, stretch and shift shape the noise, **Pattern phase**
  animates it and **Phase period** makes it loop. **Strength profile** sets how strong the
  warp is along the path.
- **Heading variation** turns markers with the same kind of noise, by up to **Heading
  deviation**.

### Markers

- **Draw as** **Markers** draws polygons along the path instead of a line. **Marker sides**
  is their shape and **Marker fill rate** how many show. Each place on the path keeps its own
  chance, so a trim shows or hides markers without reshuffling the rest.
- **Marker heading** turns them, and **Follow tangent** turns them along the path.

### Coverage

- **Coverage texture** thins the strokes with noise, by up to **Coverage variation**, and
  **Coverage wave** with a wave along the path. **Phase step per stroke** makes each stroke a
  little different.
- Coverage only goes down from **Base coverage**, which starts at full: lower it, to 0.5 say,
  to see the texture or the wave at low values.

### Brush texture

These make the strokes look painted with a real brush. All of them start at 0, which leaves
the strokes as they were.

- **Dry brush** is paint running out: gaps open in each stroke, as the paper shows through a
  dry brush. **Dry brush profile** says where along the stroke it runs dry, from none at the
  start to the full amount at the end by default, and **Dry brush grain** is how long the gaps
  are. With many strokes, each runs dry in its own places, like the bristles of a brush.
- **Edge roughness** tears the strokes' sides, each side by its own pattern, as Roughen Edges
  does. A single stroke is torn whole; with several, **Rough edge depth** is how far in from
  the outer strokes the tearing reaches. **Rough edge grain** is how long each tear is.
- **Coverage hardness** goes from soft, wet coverage to crisp, dry coverage: the coverage
  from the texture, the wave and **Dry brush** is pressed towards fully on or off, as Levels
  squeezes a matte.
- **Density colour** recolours the strokes by how much paint they carry: thin paint takes
  **Thin paint colour**, middling paint **Mid paint colour** and full paint **Dense paint
  colour**, a three-tone colour map read from the coverage.

The patterns are laid along the stroke in pixels, so they stay where they are as **Trim out**
draws the stroke on, and **Variation seed** gives other ones.

### Trim and tips

- **Trim in** and **Trim out** are the part of the path that shows, **Path travel** moves it
  and **Wrap trim** lets it go round. A stroke covers its path once at most, and keeps the
  outline it has on the whole path as it is trimmed or moved.
- **Leading tip** and **Trailing tip** are **Flat**, **Rounded**, **Open arrow** or **Solid
  arrow**. An arrow's arms land on the stroke's end, its reach is how long it is and its
  spread is in degrees.

### Colour

- Each guide has a palette of up to five swatches along its path, set by **Palette size**,
  with **Along-path colour profile**, **Along-path palette shift**, **Palette shift jitter**
  and **Repeat along-path palette**.
- **Across-stroke colour** is a second palette across the family of strokes, which
  **Palette combine** **Sum** adds to the first and **Product** multiplies with it.

When a frame would need more geometry than the renderer allows, it lowers the path
resolution and the marker sides, and keeps fewer strokes at even intervals across the family.
