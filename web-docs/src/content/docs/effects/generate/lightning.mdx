Lightning is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

A bolt of lightning: jagged, branching, with a bright filament inside a coloured
halo. Also arcs between electrodes, magic, a shorting cable, energy running along
a shape.

Two points set it up — **Origin**, where the bolt leaves, and **Direction**,
where it is aimed — and **Type** decides what happens between them: a bolt whose
far end wanders free, one that lands exactly on its target, five radiating out at
once, or two meeting in the middle.

The control that makes it *lightning* rather than a jagged line is
**Conductivity state**. It is the bolt's shape, not its speed: hold it and the
bolt holds still, animate it and the bolt writhes, and scrubbing back to a frame
you have already seen gives exactly the same bolt. That is why an export and a
preview never disagree about what the lightning did.

## What each control does

- **Origin x** and **Origin y** — where the bolt leaves from, in pixels at
  composition size.
- **Direction x** and **Direction y** — where it is aimed. Under **Omni** only
  the *distance* between the two points is read, since those bolts go every way
  at once.
- **Type** — **Direction** lets the far end wander, which is what a bolt going
  off into the sky does. **Strike** pins both ends, so the lightning lands on
  whatever is at the Direction point. **Omni** sends five bolts out in all
  directions from the origin. **Two-way strike** sends one from each point,
  meeting in the middle.
- **Conductivity state** — the bolt's shape. Keyframe it and the lightning
  writhes; the rate you keyframe it at is how fast it moves.
- **Seed** — which bolt. Two Lightning effects with the same seed and the same
  points draw the same bolt; change one of them if they are meant to look
  independent.
- **Amplitude** — how far the bolt wanders off the straight line, as a percentage
  of its own length. Small numbers give a taut arc, large ones a wild one.
- **Forking** — how many branches leave the main bolt, as a percentage of the
  twelve there is room for. The branches are thinner and dimmer than what they
  left, and they re-roll as Conductivity state moves, so a writhing bolt sheds
  and grows branches rather than waving a fixed set about.
- **Decay** — how much dimmer the far end is than the root. It is what makes a
  bolt read as coming *from* somewhere.
- **Core radius** — how thick the bright filament is, in pixels at composition
  size. Small is right: the filament is meant to be the hot centre of the halo,
  not the bolt itself.
- **Core colour** — the filament's colour. White or near-white is usual; values
  above 1 make it bloom under a [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/).
- **Glow radius** — how far the halo reaches. This is where most of the bolt's
  visible size comes from.
- **Glow colour** — the halo's colour. The blue-white default is lightning; warm
  it up for fire, green it for something else entirely.
- **Glow opacity** — how strong the halo is.
- **Composite on original** — on, the layer that arrived stays under the bolt.
  Off, the bolt is all there is.
- **Mix** — how much of the effect takes hold.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The bolt is worked out once a frame, not once a pixel.** That is worth knowing
because it is why the effect is affordable at all: the branching shape is decided
on the processor and handed to the graphics card as a list of straight segments,
which the card then only has to measure distances to. It also means the CPU and
the GPU cannot possibly draw different bolts.

**Four of After Effects' eight lightning types are here.** Breaking, Bouncey,
Anywhere and Vertical are not: two of them are Direction with a different
wobble, one needs something to bounce off, and one is Direction with half its
aiming point ignored. An imported instance gets the nearest of the four and says
so.

**Alpha Obstacle is not carried either.** Asking the bolt to route around the
layer's own shape is a search rather than a formula, and it is the one thing in
After Effects' version that would change the effect's cost by an order of
magnitude.
