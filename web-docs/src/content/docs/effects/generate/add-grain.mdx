Add grain is a **Generate** effect.

## What it is for

Putting film grain on a picture that has none, or matching the grain of a picture
that has. It is the thing that makes a clean graphic sit convincingly on top of
shot footage, and the thing that stops a computer-made image announcing itself as
computer-made.

[Noise](/effects/generate/noise/) does the plain version — one random value per
pixel. Three things separate grain from that, and they are the first three
controls here. Grain has a **size**, because a silver clump is bigger than a
pixel. It has a **softness**, because a clump is not a square. And it follows the
**tone**: real film is grainiest in the mid tones and nearly clean at both ends
of the range, which is why the three tonal controls are here rather than in a
matte.

## What each control does

- **Intensity** — how strong the grain is. 50 is a visible but polite stock;
  single figures are the "stop it looking computer-made" dose. At 0 the picture
  comes through exactly as it arrived.
- **Size** — how big one grain is, in pixels at composition size. 1 to 2 is
  35 mm; 4 or 5 is a fast stock or a blow-up.
- **Softness** — 0 gives hard-edged square cells, which reads as digital
  scan-grain; 100 gives a soft organic mottle. Halfway is film.
- **Red**, **Green** and **Blue** — how much grain each channel gets, as a
  percentage of Intensity. Real stocks are not even: the blue layer is usually the
  grainiest, and pushing Blue up while pulling Red down is the quickest way to
  make grain read as film rather than as noise.
- **Monochrome** — off, the three channels get three independent grain fields,
  which is what colour film does. On, one field is used for all three, so the
  grain is purely light and dark and cannot tint anything.
- **Shadows**, **Midtones** and **Highlights** — how much grain each part of the
  tone range gets. At the default 100 each they are exactly neutral and the grain
  is even. Pull Shadows and Highlights down and the grain lives in the mid tones,
  which is what film does. Pull Midtones and Highlights to 0 and you have grain
  only in the blacks, which is what a pushed stock does.
- **Animate** — on, a fresh draw every frame, which is what grain does. Off, one
  draw frozen in place, which reads as dirt on the lens rather than grain in the
  stock.
- **Seed** — which grain. Two Add grain effects with the same seed produce the
  same grain; change one if they are meant to be independent.
- **Mix** — how much of the grained picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The grain is added where the eye is.** Added to raw light instead, the same
amount of grain would be invisible in the shadows and a blizzard in the
highlights, because linear light spends most of its range on the top stop. The
effect works on a perceptual version of the picture and puts it back afterwards,
which is what makes Intensity mean one thing across the whole frame.

**The three tonal weights add up to one.** At 100/100/100 they are provably
neutral — no combination of them can put a seam in a gradient, and turning one
down cannot brighten or darken anything.

**Softness is not a blur.** It is a crossfade between the same random field read
two ways: one value per cell for the hard end, and the same lattice smoothly
interpolated for the soft one. That costs one extra lookup instead of a second
pass over the frame.

**Grain is applied before the layer's transparency**, so it lies evenly over the
picture rather than fading out across a soft edge along with the coverage.

**There is no Remove Grain.** Taking grain *off* is a denoiser, which is a
different piece of software living inside an effect's clothes; it is not
scheduled as one.
