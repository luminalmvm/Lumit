## What each control does

The **Simulation** group, particles worked out step by step on the graphics card:

- **Simulate**: each frame's particles are worked out from the frame before, up to Max
  particles. **Turbulence amount**, **Turbulence scale** and **Turbulence speed** become a
  flow that pushes particles about wherever they are. Density layer and Trails are not read,
  and a Layer emitter gives birth only on a model's surface. A flock steers by the cells round
  a particle, up to 64 cells a side, rather than by each neighbour.
- **Sub-steps**: how many times a frame the particles move.
- **Bake**: writes the frames the layer shows to the folder the project caches in. A change
  to anything the walk reads leaves the bake unread. A change to how particles look does not.
- **Cancel**: stops a running bake. The frames it reached stay baked.
A simulation may differ slightly between graphics cards. On one machine a frame is the same however it is reached.

The **Emitter** group, where particles come from and travel:

- **In the scene**: the particles live in the composition's 3D space, lit, focused and
  scaled like a 3D layer, with 3D layers and models passing in front of and behind them.
  Turning it on puts the layer in 3D. Masks, track matte and blend mode do not apply.
- **Shape**: which emitter. **Box** fills a box of Width, Height and Depth. **Sphere** fills a
  ball as wide as Width. **Layer** gives birth over the whole frame where the Emitter
  layer's picture is bright, and with no Emitter layer nothing is born. In the scene, a
  Model layer as the Emitter layer gives birth all over its model's surface.
- **Position x**, **Position y** and **Position z**: where the emitter sits.
- **Width** and **Height**: how big the emitter is.
- **Depth**: how far the emitter reaches through the plane.
- **Emitter angle**: turns Line, Ellipse and Rectangle about Position.
- **Follow**: what a Mask path emitter walks. **Mask** is one of the layer's masks. **Shape**
  is the outline the Shape layer the effect is on draws, after any trim, offset or combine,
  and dashes as drawn, one line after another, repeater copies included. On any other layer
  Shape emits nothing.
- **Mask path**: which of the layer's masks particles are emitted from.
- **Emitter layer**: the layer a Layer emitter gives birth from.
- **Density layer**: a layer whose brightness weights where particles are born inside the
  emitter. A birth with nowhere bright enough to go is skipped. While it is set, the
  points effects below Particulate on the same layer can read its particles, and
  [Points sample](/effects/drivers/points-sample/) and
  [Layer points](/effects/drivers/layer-points/) cannot.
- **Map placement**: where the Density layer's picture sits on this layer. **As placed in
  comp** reads it where that layer sits in the composition. **Stretch to fit** stretches
  it over this layer. **Centre** centres it at its own size.
  An effect saved before this row existed stays on Stretch to fit.
- **Emit rate**: new particles per second.
- **Direction**: which way particles travel.
- **Direction z**: how far they lean out of that plane.
- **Spread**: the cone about Direction.
- **Spread z**: the lean's own cone.
- **Initial speed**: how fast a particle leaves.
- **Speed jitter**: how much particles differ from each other in that speed.

The **Bursts** group, particles thrown out all at once beside the steady flow:

- **Burst**: **Off**, **Once at start** on the layer's first frame, **Every N frames**, or
  **On trigger**, once each time **Trigger** rises past 0.5.
- **Burst count**: how many particles one burst throws out.
- **Burst interval**: how many frames apart the bursts are.
- **Trigger**: the number On trigger watches. Wire a beat driver to it, or keyframe it.

The **Particle** group, how a particle looks:

- **Life** and **Life jitter**: how long a particle lasts, and how much that varies.
- **Size** and **Size jitter**: how big it is at birth, and how much that varies.
- **Size over life**: multiplies Size against the particle's age, from birth on the left to
  death on the right. Flat by default, so a particle stays the size it was born.
- **Opacity over life**: the same curve for opacity. It starts solid and ends invisible.
- **Colour** and **End colour**: the colour at birth and at death, blended across the
  particle's life.
- **Rotation**: the rotation of particles.
- **Rotation jitter**: how much particles differ from one another in that rotation.
- **Spin**: how fast that rotation turns.
- **Align to motion**: turns each particle to face the way it is travelling, with Spin added
  on top.

The **Variation** group, what runs those changes:

- **Size by**, **Opacity by** and **Colour by**: what runs Size over life, Opacity over life
  and the blend to End colour. **Age** runs from birth to death. **Speed** runs from Speed
  min to Speed max. **Random** is a number of the particle's own that stays the same all
  its life.
- **Speed min** and **Speed max**: the speeds, in pixels per second, at the left and right
  ends of the curve or blend.

The **Fields** group, read with Simulate on:

- **Attractor**: how hard particles are pulled to the field's middle. Below nought it pushes
  them away.
- **Vortex**: how fast particles are swept round the field's middle.
- **Vortex axis**: the line the Vortex turns about.
- **Sphere**: how hard particles are thrown out of a ball of Field radius.
- **Field x**, **Field y** and **Field z**: where the field's middle is.
- **Field radius**: how far the three reach.

The **Forces** group, what moves them:

- **Gravity**: a constant pull.
- **Wind x**, **Wind y** and **Wind z**: the air's speed.
- **Drag**: how quickly a particle's speed comes round to the wind's.
- **Turbulence amount**: how far a rolling noise pushes a particle off its path.
- **Turbulence scale**: how far apart two particles have to be before the noise pushes them
  different ways.
- **Turbulence speed**: how fast that noise evolves as a particle ages.

The **Edges** group, what happens at the edge of the layer's frame:

- **Edge**: **None** lets particles carry on out of sight. **Kill** ends a particle the first
  time it leaves the frame, and it does not come back. **Wrap** brings it back in on the
  opposite side.
- **Edge margin**: how far past the frame a particle goes before Edge acts.

The **Collide** group, what particles hit:

- **Collide with**: a layer whose shapes and masks particles hit. A layer with neither is
  hit at its rectangle. While it is set, each frame's particles are worked out from the
  frame before. A particle keeps the place it was born when the emitter moves, and a
  change of force acts from the frame it happens on. Without Simulate, Density layer and
  Particle blend are not read, and at most 100,000 particles are alive at once.
- **On contact**: **Bounce** sends a particle off the other way. **Slide** carries it on
  along the surface. **Stick** holds it where it hit, moving with that layer. **Kill**
  ends it.
- **Bounciness**: how much of its speed a particle keeps as it bounces.
- **Friction**: how much a particle slows along the surface.
- **All 3D layers**: particles hit every 3D layer that is showing, up to 32. Simulate only.
- **Ground**: particles hit the floor the 3D views draw. Simulate only.
- **Ground height**: how far above that floor they hit it.

A layer facing the same way as this one is hit across and down, at every depth. One turned
against it in 3D is hit where particles pass through its shapes.

With Simulate on, any 3D layer in Collide with is hit where particles pass through it,
whichever way it faces. A layer moving into particles throws them. A Model layer is hit all
over its surface, in the pose its take starts in. The hit doesn't follow the take from
there, or a moved part.

The **Flock** group, particles steering by their neighbours:

- **Separation**: how strongly a particle steers away from neighbours that are close.
- **Alignment**: how strongly it steers to go the way its neighbours go.
- **Cohesion**: how strongly it steers towards the middle of its neighbours.
- **Flock radius**: how far away another particle can be and still count as a neighbour.

Steering turns a particle and leaves its speed as it was. A particle stuck to a layer
neither steers nor is steered by.

The **Sparks** group, small particles thrown out where one dies:

- **Sparks**: how many sparks a particle throws out when its life ends, Edge kills it or
  it is killed on contact. Sparks throw out none.
- **Sparks from**: **Death** throws them as a particle dies. **Bounce** throws them each time
  it hits something. **Flight** throws that many each second. Simulate only.
- **Spark speed**: how fast they fly out, every way at once. From a hit, they fly off the side
  the particle came from.
- **Spark inherit**: how much of its parent's speed a spark starts with. Simulate only.
- **Spark life**: how long a spark lasts.
- **Spark size**: how big a spark is, against the particle it came from.

Sparks fade and change colour by the Particle group's rows, over their own life.

With Separation, Alignment, Cohesion or Sparks above nought, each frame's particles are
worked out from the frame before, as with Collide with. Without Simulate, Density layer and
Particle blend are not read. A row that is keyframed, has an expression or is wired counts as above
nought.

The **Trails** group, a picture kept from frame to frame:

- **Persistence**: how much of the kept picture is left after one second.
- **Zoom**: how much the kept picture grows each second, about the emitter's position.
- **Turn**: how far the kept picture turns each second, about the emitter's position.

With Persistence above nought, each frame's particles are drawn over the picture kept from
the frame before, after it is faded, zoomed and turned, and that picture goes over the layer.
Every frame from the layer's in point goes into it. Density layer and Particle blend are
not read, Streak draws its tail back along each particle's speed, and the Sprite layer's
picture at the current frame is stamped on every earlier frame.

The **Render** group, how they are drawn:

- **Mode**: Disc, Sprite or Streak. In the scene, motion blur draws a disc across the open
  shutter. Sprites, model copies and the layer's and camera's own moves do not blur.
- **Feather**: how soft a disc's edge is. Disc mode only.
- **Sprite layer**: the layer stamped at each particle. Sprite mode only, and discs are
  drawn until you set it. In the scene, a Model layer as the Sprite layer draws its model at
  up to 20,000 particles, sized, turned and tinted by each.
- **Streak length**: how long the tail is, in seconds.
- **Particle blend**: how particles combine with each other where they overlap, by the layer
  blend modes. They then go over the picture together, as Normal particles do. In the
  scene every mode but Add and Screen draws as Normal, nearest last.
- **Max particles**: the most particles that may be alive at once.

The remaining properties:

- **Seed**: changes the randomness.
- **Composite on original**: on, the particles are drawn over the layer's own picture. Off,
  the layer is clear wherever no particle is.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the picture underneath.
- **Blend**: how the particles composite with the layer they are drawn over.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's brightness.
  See [Effects](/use/effects/).
- **Channel**: the channel of the Matte layer that is read.
