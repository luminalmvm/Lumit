## What it does

A loudness that arrives as 0 to 1 and a blur radius that wants 0 to 40 do not speak the
same language. Remap is the translator: say what range comes in and what range should come
out, and it maps one onto the other in a straight line. It is the piece that makes every
other driver usable on every parameter, and most rigs have one in front of the thing being
driven.

Putting the output ends the other way round — a high Out low and a low Out high — inverts
the response, so a level that rises makes a value fall.

An input range of no width has no line through it, so it answers the output's low end
rather than dividing by nought.

## What each control does

- **Value**: the number coming in. Usually wired from another driver rather than typed.
- **In low** and **In high**: the range the number arrives in. Set these to what the thing
  feeding you actually produces — an [Audio level](/effects/drivers/audio-level/) rarely
  reaches 1, so an In high of about 0.4 uses the whole of the response.
- **Out low** and **Out high**: the range it should leave in, in whatever units the
  parameter you are driving counts in.
- **Clamp**: holds the result inside the output range instead of letting it run past the
  ends. On by default, because whatever you are driving usually has a range of its own.
