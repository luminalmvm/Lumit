## What it does

Points at a layer that carries sound and gives you its loudness at the frame being drawn.
It has two sockets: **Amplitude**, the level of the whole sound, and **Low**, the level of
the bottom end alone — the one that follows a kick drum rather than a hi-hat. Wire either
into a scale, a glow or a brightness, usually through a
[Remap](/effects/drivers/remap/), and the picture moves with the track.

A single instant of sound is a meaningless number, because a wave crosses zero constantly,
so the level is measured across a short stretch of time centred on the frame rather than
read off one sample. That makes it one of the two drivers that look at times other than
their own, and Lumit folds the stretch into what it caches so a held frame cannot outlive
the sound it was measured from.

With no layer chosen, a layer that has no sound, or a layer somebody deleted, the level
reads nought. That is the quiet answer, not a fault.

## What each control does

- **Audio**: the layer whose sound is measured. Wires never cross between layers, so this
  is a layer picker rather than a socket — the canvas draws the layer you name and runs the
  wire from there.
- **Window**: how long a stretch the level is measured over, centred on the frame. Short
  follows every transient and twitches; long rides the tune and reacts late. A twentieth of
  a second is a good place to start.
