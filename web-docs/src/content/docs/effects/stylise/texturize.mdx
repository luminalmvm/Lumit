Texturize is a **Stylise** effect.

## What it is for

Pick a layer — a canvas, a sheet of paper, a wall, crumpled foil, a fabric weave
— and this layer comes back looking as though it were printed on it. The chosen
layer is embossed exactly the way [Emboss](/effects/stylise/emboss/) embosses a
picture, and the light and shade that come out are multiplied into this layer's
colour.

The everyday uses are a painted or printed title, an aged-poster look, a fabric
or plaster surface under a graphic, and giving a flat vector logo something to
sit on.

## What each control does

- **Texture** — the layer pressed into this one. Only its *brightness* matters;
  its colour is ignored. Until you pick one the effect does nothing at all. The
  texture layer can be switched off in the timeline — it is read directly, not
  composited.
- **Light direction** — where the light falls across the texture's relief,
  measured from straight up and going clockwise. Turning it 180° turns every
  raised thread into a groove.
- **Relief** — how far apart the two points being compared are, in pixels at
  composition size, which is how deep the texture presses. 1 is a fine weave; 4
  or more is a heavy pressed surface.
- **Texture contrast** — how strongly the relief shows. 0 leaves the picture
  alone; above 150 the texture starts to overwhelm the image under it.
- **Placement** — what happens *outside* one copy of the texture: **Stretch**
  holds its edge outward, **Tile** repeats it, **Centre** leaves the rest of the
  frame untextured. At Scale 100 all three are the same picture.
- **Scale** — how big one copy of the texture is, as a percentage of the frame.
  100 fits it exactly; 50 with Tile gives four copies; 200 shows a quarter of it.
- **Mix** — how much of the textured picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — where the effect happens. Point the Matte at a shape
  and only that region gets textured; see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Texture and Matte are two different rows, and they answer two different
questions.** Texture says *what surface*; Matte says *where*. Pressing a canvas
into a whole frame but only over the sky needs both, which is why this effect has
its own layer row rather than reusing the Matte one.

**A flat texture does nothing.** The effect reads the texture's *changes*, not
its brightness, so a plain grey layer presses nothing at any contrast. If nothing
is happening, the texture probably has no detail at this Scale — zoom it in.

**Scale changes the texture's grain, and Relief changes its depth.** They are
easy to confuse. Halving Scale makes the weave twice as fine; doubling Relief
makes the same weave twice as deep. For a coarse canvas, raise Scale *and*
Relief together.

**The texture is read at the composition's size**, whatever the texture layer's
own resolution. That is what makes Scale 100 fill the frame exactly, and it is
why Tile and Centre are described in terms of Scale rather than of the texture's
own pixel dimensions.

**A precomp makes the best texture.** Build the surface once — a
[Fractal noise](/effects/generate/fractal-noise/) with a
[Levels](/effects/colour/levels/) on it makes an excellent paper or plaster —
and point several layers at the same precomp so the whole title sequence sits on
one surface.

**Transparency is untouched**, so a textured logo keeps its shape.
