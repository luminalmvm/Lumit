## What it does

This flare is **simulated**. The ghosts are traced through a real lens
prescription, meaning actual element curvatures, glasses and spacings of a
photographic lens, so they scale, stretch, colour and slide across the frame
exactly as a camera's would. The starburst is the true diffraction pattern of the
aperture.

## What each control does

The light, and how strong the whole thing is:

- **Light x** and **Light y**: position where the light originates.
- **Source width** and **Source height**: the half-size of the emitting area.
- **Intensity**: the master gain on the effect.
- **F-stop**: stops the iris down from the lens's own widest aperture. Wide open,
  ghosts are large and round; stopped down they shrink and take on the shape
  of the blades.
- **Lens**: the prescription being traced. Changing it changes the flare's character.
- **Lens file**: your own lens prescription, in the same format the optical bench tools
  use. When set it overrides the Lens choice.

The **Lens options** group:

- **Focus (m)**: the distance the lens is focused at.
- **Anamorphic squeeze**: the horizontal squeeze of an anamorphic lens.
- **Blades**: how many blades the iris has.
- **Rotation**: turns the iris.
- **Coating**: how well the elements are anti-reflection coated overall.
- **Roundness** and **Softness**: the iris opening's shape and the softness of its
  edge.
- **Element 1** through **Element 20**: the coating on each individual piece of
  glass. Only as many rows appear as the chosen lens actually has elements.

The **Flare options** group:

- **Ghost intensity**: the gain on the ghost train.
- **Ghost softness**: blurs the ghosts.
- **Max ghosts**: how many ghosts render.
- **Detail**: increases/decreases tracing work.
- **Dispersion**: how far the traced wavelengths spread apart.
- **Starburst intensity**: the gain on the diffraction spikes alone.
- **Scale**: scales the whole flare about the optical centre, ghosts and starburst
  together.

The **Source** group:

- **Source**: Manual light is set by the effects position. 
  Matte finds a flare's sources from thresholding a layer. 
  Lights uses the composition's own light layers as sources.
- **Light tint**: multiplies the colour of every light.
- **Use source colour**: in Matte and Lights modes, whether each source flares in
  its own colour or uses the set flare colour.
- **Matte layer**: which layer is used as the source.
- **Threshold**: how bright a pixel must be to count as a light.
- **Threshold softness**: how gradually sources fade in as they cross the
  threshold.

The remaining properties:

- **Quality**: Increasing quality can dramatically improve the lens flare, especially
  when using matte layers, but at a significant render time increase.
- **Blend**: how the flare composites with the layer it's applied to.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).
