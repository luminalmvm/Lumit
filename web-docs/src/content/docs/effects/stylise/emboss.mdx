Emboss is a **Stylise** effect.

## What it is for

The picture is treated as a surface with height, lit from one direction, and what
comes back is the stamped-metal look: a flat grey sheet with the frame's edges
raised out of it, bright on the side facing the light and dark on the other.

It is grey on purpose — After Effects' Emboss suppresses colour and so does this.
The everyday uses are a pressed-metal or letterpress title, a fake bump map to
combine with the original picture through a [blend mode](/use/blend-modes/), and
a quick way to see the shape of detail that is hiding in the shadows.

## What each control does

- **Direction** — where the light is, measured from straight up and going
  clockwise, the same convention [Drop shadow](/effects/stylise/drop-shadow/)
  uses. The slope facing it is the bright one. Turning it 180° swaps every
  highlight for a shadow, which reads as the relief being pressed in rather than
  raised out.
- **Relief** — how far apart the two points being compared are, in pixels at
  composition size, which is how thick the relief reads. Small values give a fine
  etched edge; large ones give a soft, deep, almost three-dimensional swell.
- **Contrast** — the gain on the relief. 0 is a flat grey card; 100 is the
  shipped look; above 150 the highlights and shadows run to white and black and
  the picture reads as a hard stamp.
- **Mix** — how much of the relief replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Relief 0 is flat grey, not "off".** With no separation between the two points
being compared there is no relief to see, and the honest answer is the surface
with no light on it. Use **Mix** to turn the effect down.

**To get the colour back, blend it.** Emboss on a duplicate of the layer, set
that copy's blend mode to Overlay or Soft light, and the colour underneath comes
through with the relief on top. That is the standard recipe and it looks far
better than any "keep colour" switch would.

**For a coloured relief**, put a [Tint](/effects/colour/tint/) or a
[Tritone](/effects/colour/tritone/) after it — the grey is a perfect input for
both.

**Direction and Relief are the same two dials as a bevel.** Animating Direction
slowly makes the light travel across the frame, which is the "light sweep across
a metal logo" move; keep Relief still while you do it, or the metal appears to
change thickness.

**Transparency is untouched**, so an embossed title stays a title rather than
becoming a grey rectangle.
