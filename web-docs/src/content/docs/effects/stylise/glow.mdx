Glow is a [Stylise](/effects/stylise/) effect.

## What it is for

Finds the bright parts of the picture and blooms them outward, the way light
scatters inside a real lens. Highlights bleed into their surroundings; everything
below the threshold is left alone.

This is the single most useful effect in the family. It is what makes neon read
as neon, what gives a muzzle flash weight, and what turns a flat capture into
something that looks lit. On a montage it usually lives on an adjustment layer,
so every layer beneath it blooms together as one picture rather than each
glowing separately.

The reason it looks like light rather than like a blurred grey copy is that it
works in scene-linear light: a pixel blooms in proportion to how far *above* the
threshold it sits, so a highlight at several times white blooms far harder than
one that has only just crossed. Stock glows that work on display-referred pixels
cannot do that, which is why theirs come out grey.

Because it uses a chain of progressively smaller copies rather than one wide
blur, a large radius costs close to what a small one does. There is no reason to
be shy with it.

It blooms transparency along with colour, so a glow spreads over transparent
areas the way real light does — a bright logo on an empty layer will glow into
the space around it rather than being cut off at its own edge.

In the Effect Controls panel:

- **Matte** gates which pixels may seed the halo, before the bright pass: light only escapes from where the matte is bright, but it still spills outward across dark matte — which fading the finished glow cannot do. **Invert** reads the matte the other way round, light for dark.

## What each control does

- **Threshold** — how bright a pixel must be before it blooms. This is the control
  that decides *what* glows, and it is where to start. Low values bloom most of
  the picture into a haze; high values pick out only genuine highlights. Values
  above white are allowed, and useful, when only the truly over-range parts
  should glow.
- **Softness** — how abruptly a pixel starts to bloom as it crosses the threshold.
  A narrow value gives a hard boundary between glowing and not, which can pop as
  a moving highlight crosses it; widening it eases the transition so brightening
  areas fade into the bloom.
- **Radius** — how far the bloom spreads, in pixels. It is authored in real pixels
  and scaled with the preview resolution, so a draft matches the export. There is
  no ceiling: a very wide atmospheric bloom is a matter of typing a larger number.
- **Intensity** — how strong the bloom is when it is added back. At zero the
  picture passes through untouched, which makes it the control to keyframe for a
  bloom that swells on a beat.
- **Tint** — the colour of the bloom. Neutral keeps each highlight's own colour;
  tinting is how you get a warm haze off a cool picture, or a single-colour bloom
  regardless of what is glowing.
- **Mix** — how much of the bloomed picture replaces the original.
