Sprite flare is a [Stylise](/effects/stylise/) effect.

## What it is for

A flare that is drawn rather than simulated. You give it a position, and it puts
a glow there, marches a train of ghosts along the line from that point through
the centre of the frame, and lays an anamorphic streak across it.

It is deliberately a separate effect from
[Lens flare](/effects/stylise/lens-flare/) rather than a mode of it, because the
two answer different questions. That one asks what a real lens would do; this one
asks you what you want and does exactly that, every time, for almost no cost.

Reach for it when the flare is a design element: on a title, over a graphic, on a
tracked point in a montage where predictability matters more than physics. Reach
for the simulated one when the flare has to behave like glass.

Two things make it read as a flare rather than as three overlapping shapes. The
ghosts march through the frame's *centre*, which is where a real lens puts its
reflections — so as the light crosses the middle of frame the train swings round
to the far side, exactly as it should. And their spacing is a fraction of the
distance from the light to the centre, so the train stretches and gathers as the
light moves rather than sliding along rigidly.

**Nothing is read from the picture.** There is no brightness threshold for a
source to cross, so nothing pops in and out as grain or motion changes the frame
underneath. That predictability is the point of the effect.

## What each control does

- **Light x** and **Light y** — where the light is, in pixels. Both are open past
  the frame's edges, because a light just off-screen still throws its ghosts
  across the picture, and that is much of what this effect is for. Animate the
  pair and the whole flare follows the light.
- **Intensity** — the master gain on everything the effect draws. At zero nothing
  is added at all, so this is the control to keyframe.
- **Tint** — the colour of the whole flare. It is scene-linear and can be set
  above white, which pushes the flare hotter than the plate beneath it.
- **Mix** — how much of the flared picture replaces the original.

The **Glow** group is the halo on the light itself:

- **Glow size** — its radius, in pixels.
- **Glow intensity** — its gain. Turn it down to nothing when you want only the
  ghost train and the streak.

The **Ghosts** group is the train of iris shapes:

- **Ghosts** — how many discs march along the axis. None is a legal setting.
- **Ghost spacing** — how far apart they sit, as a fraction of the distance from
  the light to the centre of frame rather than as a fixed length. That is what
  makes the train behave: it stretches as the light moves out and gathers as it
  comes in.
- **Ghost size** — their base radius, in pixels.
- **Ghost intensity** — their gain. Ghosts read best well below the glow; at equal
  strength the train stops looking like reflections and starts looking like a row
  of circles.

The **Streak** group is the anamorphic bar through the light:

- **Streak length** — how far it reaches from the light, in pixels.
- **Streak intensity** — its gain.
- **Streak angle** — which way it runs. Horizontal is the anamorphic look that
  most people picture when they think of a lens streak.
