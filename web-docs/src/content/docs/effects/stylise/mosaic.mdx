Mosaic is a **Stylise** effect.

## What it is for

The frame is cut into a grid of rectangles and every one is painted a single
colour. It is the pixelation effect: the anonymised face, the censored logo, the
retro-console look, and the transition where a shot dissolves into blocks before
it cuts.

Two flavours. By default each block takes the **average** colour of the picture
underneath it, which is smooth and is what most people mean by a mosaic. Turn
**Sharp colours** on and each block takes the colour of the single pixel at its
centre instead, which is crisper on graphic material and noisier on film.

## What each control does

- **Horizontal blocks** and **Vertical blocks** — how many blocks span the frame
  across and down. The defaults are near-square on a 16:9 frame; matching them to
  the frame's shape is usually what you want, and deliberately not matching them
  gives long letterbox blocks.
- **Sharp colours** — off, each block is the average of what is under it; on,
  each block is its centre pixel's colour.
- **Mix** — how much of the blocked picture replaces the original. Animate it
  from 0 to 100 for a mosaic that fades in without the block size changing.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  this is how you blur a single face rather than the whole frame. See
  [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Animate the block counts, not the Mix, for a pixelate-out.** Taking Horizontal
and Vertical blocks from a few hundred down to two over a second is the
transition; fading the Mix instead cross-dissolves a blocky picture over a sharp
one, which reads as a double exposure rather than as a pixelation.

**Blocks stay put when the layer moves.** The grid is fixed to the frame, not to
the picture, so a moving subject slides through a still grid — which is exactly
what you want for anonymising, and exactly what makes an animated block count
look like a transition rather than a texture.

**To anonymise something that moves**, put Mosaic on the layer and point its
**Matte** at a shape layer you have animated over the face. The mosaic then only
exists where the shape is.

**The alpha is blocked too.** A cut-out layer gets blocky *edges* rather than
smooth edges around a blocky middle, which is the honest reading of "the picture
is made of blocks".

**Very fine grids are an exact average.** Once a block is under about eight
pixels across, the average is taken over every pixel of it. Above that the block
is sampled on a grid rather than read in full — the same flat colour for a
fraction of the work.
