Light wrap is a [Stylise](/effects/stylise/) effect.

## What it is for

The oldest trick in compositing, and the cheapest way to make a keyed subject
stop looking pasted on.

In a real camera the light behind a subject spills round its edges — off the
hair, along the shoulders, anywhere the outline is thin. A matte cut in software
has none of that, and its absence is most of what makes a composite read as fake,
even when the key itself is clean.

This effect takes the layer you name as the **Background**, softens it, and
paints that softened light into a narrow band just inside the foreground's own
edge. Put it after your key — after
[Matte key](/effects/utility/matte-key/) — and point it at the plate the subject
is being composited onto.

It needs no mask of its own. The foreground's own transparency is the edge, so
the band is found automatically and follows the subject as it moves. It also
never paints outside the matte, which is the classic way to get this effect
wrong: a halo drawn around a subject looks worse than no wrap at all.

The light is screened on rather than added, so a bright background brightens the
edge *toward* itself rather than past white and into a rim of blown highlight.

Nothing happens until you choose a background, and with none chosen the picture
passes through untouched.

## What each control does

- **Background** — the layer whose light spills. Normally this is the plate the
  subject was keyed onto. It should not be the layer the effect is on, which is
  why it does not start pointed there: a layer is never its own background, and
  wrapping a picture in its own light gives you nothing.
- **Width** — how far the wrap reaches inside the edge, in pixels, and also the
  distance the background is softened by. They are deliberately the same number:
  light that spills a long way round an edge is also light that has been
  scattered, so a wide wrap should be a soft one. At zero there is no wrap at
  all. Keep it small — a wrap wide enough to notice as a band is a wrap that has
  gone too far.
- **Intensity** — how strong the spill is. There is no ceiling, so a deliberately
  hot rim against a bright plate is available, but the convincing settings are
  low.
- **Mix** — how much of the wrapped picture replaces the original.
