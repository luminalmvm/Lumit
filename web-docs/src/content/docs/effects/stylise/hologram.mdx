## What it does

Turns the layer into a hologram on a flat film. As the film tips, the picture inside it
moves with depth, in one replay colour.

The layer is the recorded subject. Its colour is what was recorded, and black is empty.
Its alpha is the film's outline: a round layer is a round sticker. A **Depth map** layer
pushes each part of the subject behind the film. Without one the subject is a flat picture
at **Subject depth**. Behind it sits a recorded grid or star field, or any layer, and the
film can carry line patterns of its own that split light into rainbows.

Each light replays the picture. A reflection hologram replays in one colour that turns
bluer at steep angles. A rainbow hologram replays in a colour that follows the eye up and
down. The image fades as a light leaves the reference direction, and its deep parts soften
under a large light. A rainbow hologram lit from one side smears them sideways.

<Shot file="hologram-types.webp" caption="Top: a reflection hologram with Tilt x at -8, 0 and 8. Bottom: a rainbow hologram with Tilt x at -4, 0 and 4." />

The film shares its sheet, lights, surface and output with
[Holographic foil](/effects/stylise/holographic-foil/).

The model of the hologram follows the open-source holospot kit by Dmitry Kurash (MIT
licensed), reimplemented for Lumit. It also uses Wyman, Sloan and Shirley's colour-matching
fit, Brian Karis's environment BRDF fit, Jarzynski and Olano's pcg3d hash, the Khronos PBR
Neutral curve and AgX as published for three.js.

## What each control does

### Film

- **Type**: **Reflection** is a thick hologram on a black backing, in one colour.
  **Rainbow** is an embossed hologram on a mirror.
- **Efficiency** is how much of the light the film sends into the image.
- **Glare** is how strongly the film's mirror shows the lights. 100 is physical.

### Subject

- **Subject colour** is how the layer's colour reaches the image. **Replay colour only**
  takes its brightness, in the replay colour. **Tinted by the layer** multiplies its colour
  by the replay colour. **Full colour** keeps its own colours.
- **Subject depth (mm)** is how far behind the film the subject sits. Above 0 it stands in
  front.

<Shot file="hologram-subject-colour.webp" caption="Replay colour only, Tinted by the layer and Full colour." />

### Depth

- **Depth map** is a layer whose brightness is the subject's depth. White is near.
- **Placement** is where the Depth map's picture sits on this layer. **As placed in comp**
  reads it where the layer sits in the composition. **Stretch to fit** stretches it over
  this layer. **Centre** centres it at its own size.
- **Depth scale (mm)** is how far the Depth map's black lies behind its white. The two sit
  either side of **Subject depth**.
- **Invert** reads the Depth map with black near.

The subject is a surface with no back. A steep edge in the Depth map hides what lies
beyond it.

<Shot file="hologram-depth.webp" caption="Tilt y at -8, 0 and 8. Top: a portrait with a Depth map, in Full colour over the grid. Bottom: the same over a Backdrop layer 45 mm behind the film, tinted." />

### Backdrop

- **Backdrop**: **Grid** and **Stars** were recorded behind the subject. **Seal**,
  **Rosette**, **Spiral**, **Lattice**, **Sunburst** and **Sparkle** are line patterns on
  the film itself, which split light into colour. **None** leaves both out.
- **Backdrop depth (mm)** is how far behind the film the grid, the stars and the Backdrop
  layer sit.
- **Backdrop layer** is a layer recorded behind the subject. It shows whatever **Backdrop**
  is set to.
- **Placement** places the Backdrop layer as the Depth map's does.
- **Pattern depth (mm)** is how far behind the surface the line patterns lie.
- **Pattern size (mm)** is how wide the grid, the stars and the patterns are. Seal, Rosette
  and Spiral end at half of it from the centre. The others cover the film.
- **Line width** is how much of each line spacing a pattern's line fills.
- **Pitch (nm)** is the groove spacing inside the pattern lines.
- **Strength** is how strongly the pattern lines split light into colour.

<Shot file="hologram-backdrops.webp" caption="None, Grid, Stars, Seal and Rosette, then Spiral, Lattice, Sunburst, Sparkle and a Backdrop layer. The patterns are at a Pattern size of 60." />

### Replay

- **Colour (nm)** is the colour the image replays in when lit from the reference direction.
- **Reference x** and **Reference y** are where a light must be for the clean image, as
  seen from the film, from -1 to 1 across and up. They start on the Spot rig's light.
- **Bandwidth (nm)** is how wide the reflection hologram's band of colour is.
- **Selectivity** is how far a light can stray from the reference before the reflection
  hologram's image fades.
- **Slit** is how wide the rainbow hologram's slit was. Narrower gives purer colour.

### Recording

- **Recording**: **Optical** is one continuous recording. **Stereogram** is made from
  separate views, side by side.
- **Speckle** is how grainy the laser light makes the image.
- **Views** is how many views the stereogram was made from.
- **View blend** is how softly one view hands over to the next.
- **View zone** is how wide a fan of angles the views cover. Outside it the image is cut.

### Tilt, Sheet, Lights, Surface and Output

These groups work as they do on Holographic foil:
[Tilt](/effects/stylise/holographic-foil/#tilt),
[Sheet](/effects/stylise/holographic-foil/#sheet),
[Lights](/effects/stylise/holographic-foil/#lights),
[Surface](/effects/stylise/holographic-foil/#surface) and
[Output](/effects/stylise/holographic-foil/#output). What differs:

- **Spin** turns the backdrop and the patterns within the film.
- Turned past edge-on, the film shows the same plain back, with no image.
- **Motion blur** blurs the film's own colours. The replayed image is not blurred.
- **Lighting** has no Auto and starts on **Spot**. One light replays one clean image, and
  Studio's four lights replay four.
- **Environment** and **Amount** sit in **Lights**, and **Height map** with its
  **Placement**, **Repeats** and **Wrap** in **Surface**. They read a layer as
  [Maps](/effects/stylise/holographic-foil/#maps) does.
- **Relief** has no Bevel from alpha. Relief and Waviness bend the film's mirror and its
  coat, and leave the image steady. **Wear** makes the replay patchy as well.
- **Quality**: **Draft** takes fewer, longer steps through the Depth map and replays the
  brightest light alone. **High** takes more steps.
- **Seed** picks another speckle, wobble and wear.

The rows every effect ends with:

- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Blend**: the blend mode the result is laid over the original with.
- **Matte** scales the replay for each pixel: white keeps the image, black leaves bare
  film. **Invert** reads it the other way round.
- **Channel**: the channel of the Matte layer that is read.

## Speed and colour depth

The effect is worked out on the graphics card. The times here were measured on an RTX 5080
on Windows.

- The first frame waits about 8 seconds while the shader is built. **Wear**, a Height map
  and an Environment layer use a larger shader, built in the background after the first
  frame.
- A 1920×1080 frame takes 1 to 6 ms and a 3840×2160 frame up to 19 ms. **Draft** is about
  a tenth faster.
- A still film is drawn once and cached for the layer's span. **Auto tilt** above 0 draws
  every frame. So does **Motion blur** with **Follow layer rotation** on, or with keyframes
  on a tilt, **Spin**, **Rig rotation** or a light's direction.
- In a project at 8 bpc [colour depth](/use/colour-management/#colour-depth) nothing is
  brighter than white, the glints included.
