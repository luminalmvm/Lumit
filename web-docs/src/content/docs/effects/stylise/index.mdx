## What this family does

Everything in this family changes **how the picture looks** rather than where it
is or what colour a pixel is. Two groups sit under that heading, and it is worth
knowing which you are in.

**The ones that add light** — a bloom, a flare, a wrap, a strobe — put energy
into the frame that the footage did not contain, and they depend on Lumit's
headroom above white (see below).

**The ones that redraw the surface** — [Median](/effects/stylise/median/),
[Mosaic](/effects/stylise/mosaic/), [Find edges](/effects/stylise/find-edges/),
[Emboss](/effects/stylise/emboss/), [Texturize](/effects/stylise/texturize/),
[Roughen edges](/effects/stylise/roughen-edges/) and
[Drop shadow](/effects/stylise/drop-shadow/) — leave the exposure alone and change
what the frame is *made of*: flat blocks, drawn outlines, grey relief, a printed
surface, a chewed edge, a cast shadow.

- [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/) blooms the picture's own bright areas outward.
  It is the one to reach for first, and the one that does the most work per
  control.
- [Lens flare](/effects/stylise/lens-flare/) simulates a real photographic lens —
  ghosts traced through an actual prescription, a starburst that is the true
  diffraction pattern of the aperture. It is the expensive, physically honest
  one.
- [Sprite flare](/effects/stylise/sprite-flare/) draws a flare instead of
  simulating one: a glow, a train of ghosts and an anamorphic streak, placed
  where you say. It is cheap, art-directed and completely predictable.
- [Light wrap](/effects/stylise/light-wrap/) spills a background's light around a
  keyed subject's edges, which is most of what stops a composite looking pasted
  on.
- [Flash](/effects/stylise/flash/) is the strobe: a hit of colour on a beat, or
  on a keyframe.

## Why these need light above white

Lumit works in scene-linear light, where values brighter than white are legal and
carry real information. That is what these effects depend on. A glow blooms
proportionally to how far a pixel is *above* its threshold, so a highlight at
four times white blooms four times as hard as one just over it — which is the
difference between a bloom that reads as light and one that reads as a blurred
grey smear.

The practical consequence: keep the [Colour](/effects/colour/) effects that clip
or compress highlights *after* these, not before. A grade that flattens the
brights first leaves a glow nothing to find.

## Which flare

Both flare effects place light from a point, and they answer different
questions. Lens flare asks *what would this lens actually do*, and its answer
changes with the f-stop, the focus and the glass. Sprite flare asks *draw me a
flare here*, and its answer is whatever you set. When you need a flare that
behaves like a camera's, take the first; when you need one that does exactly the
same thing every time and costs almost nothing, take the second.
