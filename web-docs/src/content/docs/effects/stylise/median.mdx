Median is a **Stylise** effect.

## What it is for

Every pixel is replaced by the **middle** value of the little square of pixels
around it: line the neighbours up in order of brightness and take the one in the
centre.

That one idea does something no blur can. A stray white speck has no neighbours
agreeing with it, so it never gets anywhere near the middle of the queue and
disappears completely. A real edge has half its square on each side, so the
middle value is still on the correct side of the edge, and the edge stays exactly
where it was and exactly as crisp. Blur softens everything including the edges;
median removes the specks and leaves the edges alone.

The everyday uses are sensor speckle and single-pixel dropouts, salt-and-pepper
noise from an aggressive codec, cleaning up a chroma key's fringe, and — at the
top of the Radius range — a deliberate look: the picture flattens into paint-like
patches with the outlines still drawn in.

## What each control does

- **Radius** — how far the square reaches. 1 is a 3×3 vote and removes single
  stray pixels; 2 is a 5×5 vote and cleans up small clusters; 3 is a 7×7 vote and
  starts to read as a look rather than a repair. **0** turns the effect off
  exactly.
- **Operate on alpha** — off, the layer's transparency is left exactly as it
  arrived and only the colour is filtered. On, the transparency is filtered too,
  which moves the shape's outline. Leave it off unless the speckle is *in* the
  matte.
- **Mix** — how much of the filtered picture replaces the original. Useful at 40
  or 50 for a clean-up that keeps some of the grain.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Radius stops at 3, and it really stops.** Everywhere else in Lumit a slider is
a suggestion and a number you type is obeyed. Here it is not, and the reason is
cost: the work grows as roughly the *fourth power* of the radius — about 45
comparisons per pixel at radius 1, 325 at 2, 1 225 at 3, and seventeen thousand
at 6. A control that quietly gave you radius 3 when you asked for 30 would be
answering a different question from the one you asked, so it refuses instead.

**It is the most expensive single pass in the catalogue.** Expect it to slow a
preview down noticeably at radius 3 on a large composition. Drop the preview
resolution while you work, or leave it until the grade is settled.

**For a look rather than a repair, stack it.** Two Medians at radius 2 flatten
further than one at radius 3 and cost less, and the outlines stay crisper. A
[Find edges](/effects/stylise/find-edges/) or a
[Posterize](/effects/colour/posterize/) after a Median is where the illustrated
look comes from.

**It is not a denoiser.** Median removes *outliers* — pixels that disagree with
everything around them. Even, film-like grain has no outliers to find, so a
Median will flatten it rather than remove it. That is a different problem and a
different programme.

**A half-resolution preview medians a half-size window**, because Radius is a
length in pixels at composition size. That is the same rule every spatial control
follows, and it means the preview is a fair picture of the export.
