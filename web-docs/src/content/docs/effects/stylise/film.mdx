## What it does

Film exposes the picture onto a film stock through each of the stock's layers' measured
colour sensitivity, develops it along the stock's curves, prints it on a print stock and
shows the print in the light it was made for. Grey stays grey and at the same brightness;
the stock decides the contrast, the colour and the highlights.

Halation, bloom, grain, gate weave and flicker are parts of it, each with its own **On**
switch.
With **Film colour** off the picture keeps its own colour and tone and only the parts that
are on are added.

Grain, halation and bloom are sized on the film, so the **Format** decides how large they
look: a smaller frame is enlarged more.

The stocks and the model are from spektrafilm by Andrea Volpato
([github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm](https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm)). The
stock data is CC BY-SA 4.0.

- A camera white balance: a tungsten stock never turns blue in daylight.

## What each control does

- **Stock**: the film in the camera.
- **Format**: how big the frame is on the film.
- **Exposure**: stops of light on the negative.
- **Highlights**: stops the brightest parts of the picture are lifted by, from just below
  white, as if the camera had not clipped them.
- **Film colour**: off keeps the picture's own colour and tone.
- **Push or pull**: stops the negative is pushed or pulled in development. A push adds
  contrast, a pull takes it away.
- **Interlayer**: how hard each layer holds back the others and itself as it develops. It
  sets the colour separation and the contrast at edges.
Each part has an **On** switch at the top of its group.

- **Print**: off shows a scan of the negative, inverted, instead of a print.
- **Print stock**: what the negative is printed on: two motion picture print films and two
  papers.
- **Brightness**: stops brighter or darker the print is made.
- **Contrast**: the print's contrast, per cent of the stock's own.
- **Red**, **Green** and **Blue**: printer points, each a twelfth of a stop more of that
  colour in the print.
- **Halation**: light that passes through the film, bounces off its base and lands on the
  red layer again, a red and orange glow round bright lights. **Amount** and **Size** are
  how strong the glow is and how far it reaches.
- **Backing**: the layer behind the film that stops the bounce. **Removed** glows the most.
- **Bloom**: a diffusion filter on the lens. Highlights spread a soft glow and the shadows
  lift. **Amount** is how much of the light is spread, **Size** how far.
- **Grain**: the dye clouds the picture is made of, strongest where the stock's grain is.
  **Amount** and **Size** are how strong the grain is and how large. **Colour** at 100 gives
  each layer its own grain, at 0 all three the same grain.
- **Animate**: on, the grain changes every frame.
- **Seed**: which grain, weave and flicker are drawn.
- **Gate weave**: the frame wanders a little in the gate. **Amount** is how far, **Speed**
  how quickly.
- **Flicker**: the exposure changes a little from frame to frame.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Blend**: the blend mode the result is laid over the original with.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).
- **Channel**: the channel of the Matte layer that is read.

## Presets

The **Film** group in the [Effects & presets](/panels/effects-presets/) panel holds
**Halation**, **Cinema 500T**, **Cinema 250D**, **Night lights**, **Super 16**, **Home
movie**, **Portrait**, **Slide** and **Film grain**. **Halation** is Film with only its
halation on and the backing removed.
