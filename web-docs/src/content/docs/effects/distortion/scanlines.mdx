Scanlines is a [Distortion](/effects/distortion/) effect.

## What it is for

Darkens every other band of the picture, the way a cathode-ray display drew it.
It is the cheapest and most recognisable way to say "this is on a screen" —
useful on a monitor inside a shot, on a whole sequence for a retro treatment, and
in short bursts as part of a glitch.

It reads each pixel and darkens it where it falls, without looking at its
neighbours, so it is the least expensive effect in this family and it never
softens the picture.

The lines can roll, which is what an unsynchronised analogue signal does, and
they can alternate between fields for the true interlaced look. Both are worth
trying before reaching for anything more elaborate: a slow roll under an
otherwise clean picture is remarkably convincing.

Stack it after [Block glitch](/effects/distortion/block-glitch/) so the lines lie
over the torn picture rather than being torn up themselves.

## What each control does

- **Intensity** — how dark the dark lines get. At zero the picture passes through
  untouched; at full the darkened lines go to black. Only the dark half is
  affected — the bright lines are left exactly as they were — so this is a single
  honest darkening dial rather than a contrast between two bands.
- **Line period** — the height of one light-and-dark pair, in pixels. Small values
  give the fine texture of a high-resolution display; larger ones give the
  chunky banding of an old one. It is authored in real pixels, and scaled with the
  preview resolution so a draft looks like the export.
- **Roll speed** — how fast the pattern travels up or down the frame, in lines per
  second. Zero holds it still. Either sign works, and a slow drift is far more
  convincing than a fast one.
- **Interlace offset** — alternates which half of each period is darkened from one
  period to the next, the classic interlaced-field look. It changes the character
  of the pattern rather than its strength.
- **Mix** — how much of the lined picture replaces the original.
