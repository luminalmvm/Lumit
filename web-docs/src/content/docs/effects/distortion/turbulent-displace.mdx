Turbulent displace is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Pushing the picture around with a pattern of invisible swirls. Every pixel asks
the pattern "which way, and how far?", then fetches its colour from there instead
of from where it sits — so the picture bends and churns without anything being
added to it.

That one idea covers a surprising amount of work: heat haze over a road, water
and reflections, flags and fabric, smoke curling, wobbly hand-drawn edges on text
and shapes, and the loose organic distortion that makes a clean composite look
photographed rather than assembled.

The pattern is the same one [Fractal noise](/effects/generate/fractal-noise/)
draws, so the two work together: give both the same **Seed**, **Size**,
**Complexity** and **Evolution** and the swirls in the warp line up with the
blobs in the noise.

Three controls do nearly all the work. **Size** is how big a swirl is. **Amount**
is how far it can pull. **Complexity** is how much fine detail rides on the big
swirls — one is a gentle swell, three is turbulence, ten is froth.

## What each control does

- **Displacement** — **Turbulent** pushes pixels in both directions at once,
  which swirls. **Horizontal** and **Vertical** keep only one direction, which
  shears the picture into ripples running the other way — the look for a
  television interference band, or for water seen edge on.
- **Amount** — how far a pixel can be pulled, in pixels at composition size. Small
  numbers wobble an edge; large ones dissolve the picture into smears. Negative
  simply reads the pattern the other way round, which is a quick way to get a
  second, different-looking warp out of the same settings.
- **Size** — how big one swirl is. This is the control that decides what the
  effect *reads as*: a size near the width of the frame is a slow drifting bend,
  a size of thirty pixels is a shimmer. Set Size first, then Amount.
- **Complexity** — how many layers of finer swirl ride on the big ones. 1 is a
  smooth swell, 3 is turbulence, 10 is froth. Each extra layer costs time.
- **Offset x** and **Offset y** — where the pattern sits under the frame. Animate
  them to make the whole warp travel — heat haze drifting along a road — which
  looks quite different from the churning that Evolution gives.
- **Evolution** — the depth dial. Keyframe it to animate the warp. One full turn
  moves one step through the pattern, so a slow simmer is a few turns across a
  shot and a hard boil is many.
- **Cycle evolution** and **Cycle** — make Evolution loop. With Cycle at 3, the
  warp at 0 turns and the warp at 3 turns are identical, so animating Evolution
  from 0 to 1080° gives a clip that tiles end to end with no seam. Useful for a
  looping background.
- **Pinning** — which edges are held still. A warp that pulls the frame's border
  around drags transparency in from outside, which shows as a soft ragged edge —
  pinning stops that by fading the warp to nothing near the edges it names.
  **All edges** is the default and is almost always what you want; **Left and
  right** or **Top and bottom** hold one pair only, for a full-frame element that
  is going to be tiled or cropped in one direction.
- **Seed** — which pattern this instance uses. Two Turbulent displaces with the
  same settings and different seeds warp differently, which is how you stop a
  row of elements moving in unison.
- **Mix** — how much of the warped picture replaces the original. Note that this
  *blends* the two, so a mid Mix shows both — a ghost. To warp *less*, turn
  Amount down; to warp less **in places**, use a Matte.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — see below.

## Notes

**What the Matte does here is special.** On most effects a Matte fades the
finished result in and out. Here it scales the *push* itself, so a grey area of
the matte is genuinely warped less rather than being a full warp faded back.
The difference is easy to see: a faded warp shows two overlapping copies of every
edge, and a scaled one shows a single edge in a place between the two. This is
what makes "wobble only this part of the shot" possible.

The classic pairing is a [Fractal noise](/effects/generate/fractal-noise/) on a
solid used as the Matte, which gives a warp that comes and goes across the frame
in patches — heat haze that is stronger over the bonnet than over the sky.

**Pinning and Amount are linked.** The pinned edges fade the warp out over a band
as wide as Amount, so a large Amount pins a wide border and a small one pins only
a thin strip. That is deliberate: the band is exactly as wide as it has to be to
guarantee nothing is dragged in from outside.
