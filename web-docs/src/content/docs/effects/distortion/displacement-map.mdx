Displacement map is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Warping one layer with the *shape of another*. Point the **Matte** row at a layer
— that layer is the map — and every pixel is pushed according to how bright the
map is underneath it. Bright pushes one way, dark the other, mid-grey does not
move at all.

It is the effect behind heat haze over a road, water refracting what is under it,
glass distorting a background, a logo rippling as a wave passes through it, and
text being pushed around by a painted gradient. Anywhere the warp has to *line up*
with something — a rendered pass, a ripple you drew, another layer's luminance —
this is the one to reach for.

## What each control does

- **Matte** — **the map**, and the one control the effect cannot work without.
  Until you pick a layer here, Displacement map does nothing at all. That layer is
  rendered on its own at this composition's size, so you never have to worry about
  fitting it.
- **Horizontal channel** and **Vertical channel** — which part of the map steers
  which direction. The defaults read **Red** for sideways and **Green** for up and
  down, which is how a purpose-made map is normally painted; choose **Luminance**
  when the map is a plain grey picture such as a [Fractal
  noise](/effects/generate/fractal-noise/) or a ramp.
- **Horizontal amount** and **Vertical amount** — how far a push can go, in pixels
  at composition size. A map channel at full white pushes the whole amount one
  way, full black the whole amount the other, and mid-grey nothing. Set one to
  zero to confine the warp to a single axis — vertical only for a heat shimmer,
  horizontal only for a glass ripple. Negative values simply read the map the
  other way round on that axis.
- **Edges** — what a pixel pushed in from beyond the frame reads. **Repeat**, the
  default, smears the border pixel and keeps the frame full; **Transparent** eats
  a bite out of the edge; **Mirror** reflects the picture back, which hides the
  seam best on a textured plate.
- **Mix** — how much of the warped picture replaces the original.
- **Invert** — reads the map the other way round, which reverses every push.

## Notes

**Mid-grey means "do not move".** This is the single most useful thing to know
about the effect. If your map is mostly black, everything will be shoved hard in
one direction; grade it so its middle sits around 50 % grey and the warp will move
*about* its rest position instead of away from it.

**The map is a layer, so it can be anything.** A Fractal noise with Evolution
animated gives a living heat haze. A solid with a [Gradient](/effects/generate/gradient/)
gives a controllable lens. A blurred copy of the layer itself makes the picture
distort along its own edges. Switch the map layer's own visibility off — the Matte
row renders it whether or not it is showing.

**How it differs from [Turbulent displace](/effects/distortion/turbulent-displace/).**
Turbulent displace *invents* its warp from a noise field it generates itself and
animates for free. Displacement map is *given* the warp and has none of its own.
Use Turbulent displace when you want churn and do not care exactly where it goes;
use this one when the warp has to match something.
