Ripple is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

A stone goes into the picture. Rings of distortion spread out from where it
landed, strongest about a third of the way to the rim and dying away to nothing
by the time they reach it.

Turn **Evolution** and the rings travel outward. That is where the motion comes
from — there is no speed control, because a control that meant "cycles per
second" would have to read the clock, and an effect that reads the clock renders
one picture in the preview and a different one in the export. Keyframe Evolution
instead: one full turn sends one whole wave out.

The everyday uses are water — a puddle, a lake, a drop landing — plus shockwaves
and impact hits on a title, a heat-haze pulse, and the moment a portal opens.

## What each control does

- **Radius** — how far the rings reach, as a percentage of the composition
  diagonal. Outside it the picture is untouched, exactly, so the effect is safe
  to drop on a full frame. Being a percentage rather than a pixel count, it
  covers the same part of the picture whatever size the composition is.
- **Centre X** and **Centre Y** — where the stone landed, in pixels at
  composition size. A fresh Ripple starts in the middle of the frame.
- **Type** — **Symmetric** slides each pixel in and out along the radius, which
  reads as a lens breathing. **Asymmetric** adds a sideways component a quarter
  wave out of step, so each pixel walks a small circle instead. That is what a
  particle of water actually does under a passing wave, and it is why Asymmetric
  is the default.
- **Wave height** — the farthest a pixel moves, at the peak of the envelope. It
  is a literal distance: the number you set is the distance you get.
- **Wave width** — the distance from one crest to the next. Small values give
  fine concentric ripples; large ones give a single slow swell.
- **Evolution** — where the rings have travelled to. One turn is one wave. Put a
  linear keyframe on it and the ripples run outward for as long as you like.
- **Mix** — how much of the rippled picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Nothing happens at the epicentre, and that is deliberate.** The wave's strength
rises from zero at the centre, peaks about a third of the way out, and falls back
to zero at the rim. A ripple with a flat amplitude would have to push the very
middle pixel in some direction, and there is no direction there — the result is a
pinched blob where the stone went in. It is also simply what a spreading wave
looks like: the crests are strongest in a ring, not at the point of impact.

**Wave height is honest.** The envelope above scales the wave, so the number on
the slider is scaled up to compensate. Set 20 pixels' worth and the farthest a
pixel travels is 20 pixels.

**Keep Wave height below about a quarter of Wave width.** Past that the wave
starts to fold over itself and the picture goes from rippled to churned. That is
sometimes what you want — for a hard impact — but it is not water any more.

**The edges repeat.** A Radius wider than half the frame height puts crests over
the top and bottom borders. Rather than punch transparent bites out of the
picture there, the border pixel is held; the same choice
[Turbulent displace](/effects/distortion/turbulent-displace/) and
[Wave warp](/effects/distortion/wave-warp/) make.
