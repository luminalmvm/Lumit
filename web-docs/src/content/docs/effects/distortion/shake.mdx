Shake is a [Distortion](/effects/distortion/) effect.

## What it is for

Wobbles the layer as though the camera were handheld, or struck. The movement
comes from seeded noise rather than from keyframes, so it never repeats
mechanically and you never have to draw it.

This is the beat-shake workhorse. A short burst on a drop, a constant low-level
drift under a whole sequence to stop it feeling locked off, a violent hit on an
impact frame — all of it comes from the two master controls and a keyframe or
two on **Amplitude**.

It is a transform effect, not a pixel effect: it moves a virtual camera and
resamples the layer once, so the picture stays as sharp as it was rather than
being smeared by repeated sampling. That also means it reveals whatever lies
beyond the layer's edge as it moves, which is what **Edges** is for.

Put it on an adjustment layer to shake a whole montage at once, including
everything composited beneath it. See [Layers](/use/layers/).

The movement is a pure function of time and the seed, so it is identical every
time you play it and identical between a preview and an export.

## What each control does

- **Amplitude** — how far the layer travels, across the composition diagonal.
  This is the control to keyframe: a spike on the beat easing back to a low
  constant is most of what this effect is used for.
- **Frequency** — how fast the wobble happens, in cycles per second. Low is a
  slow drift, high is a rattle. It is independent of Amplitude, so a large slow
  sway and a tiny fast buzz are both a few seconds' work.
- **Rotation amount** — how much the frame rolls as it moves. A little of this is
  what separates a camera shake from a picture sliding around; too much reads as
  a spin.
- **Edges** — what fills the border the movement reveals. Mirror reflects the
  picture back in and hides the seam best on textured footage, which is why it is
  the usual choice; Repeat holds the edge pixel; Transparent leaves it clear,
  which is what you want when something will be composited behind.
- **Seed** — which random wobble you get. Two layers with the same seed shake in
  perfect unison; give them different seeds and they move independently. Change
  it when a shake happens to land badly rather than trying to fight it with the
  other controls.
- **Mix** — how much of the shaken picture replaces the original.

The **Per-axis wobble** group biases the movement rather than adding to it:

- **X amount** / **Y amount** — multipliers on the master amplitude for each axis.
  Leaving both at one reproduces the plain uniform shake; dropping Y to zero
  gives a purely horizontal judder, which suits a wide frame.
- **X frequency** / **Y frequency** — multipliers on the master frequency per
  axis. Setting the two axes to different rates is what stops a shake looking
  like it is travelling along a diagonal line.
- **Z amount** and **Z frequency** — the depth shake: a pump in and out of the
  frame rather than across it. A little of this on an impact reads as the camera
  being knocked backwards.

The **Motion blur** group adds the shake's own smear:

- **Motion blur** — samples the wobble several times across the shutter and
  averages the results, so a fast shake streaks rather than stepping. This is
  only the shake's own movement; it is unrelated to the layer's motion blur
  switch or to the [Motion blur](/effects/temporal/motion-blur/) effect.
- **Shutter** — how far across the shutter window those samples spread, and so how
  long the streak is. At zero the smear is off and the result is the plain single
  resample. It is a fraction of the frame's own shutter window rather than a
  length in seconds, so the streak keeps its character whatever rate the
  composition runs at.
