Block glitch is a [Distortion](/effects/distortion/) effect.

## What it is for

Cuts the picture into a grid and pushes blocks around: some displaced sideways,
some split into colour channels, some repeating a slice of themselves. It is the
corrupted-video look, and it is built to be driven from a single dial.

Everything the effect does is scaled by **Intensity**, so at zero it is a genuine
passthrough and a keyframe pair on that one control gives you a clean burst on a
beat without touching anything else. That is how it is meant to be used: hard for
two or three frames, then gone.

Held constantly at a low value it is a subtler thing — an intermittent
instability under a whole sequence, as though the signal were not quite holding.

The pattern varies from frame to frame on its own, so a held glitch keeps moving
rather than freezing into one arrangement.

Stack it before [Scanlines](/effects/distortion/scanlines/) for the standard
broken-screen pair, and add an [RGB split](/effects/distortion/rgb-split/) over
both if the colour fringing needs to go further than this effect's own.

## What each control does

- **Intensity** — the master dial. Every other control below is scaled by it, so
  it is a real off switch at zero and the only thing most projects need to
  animate.
- **Block size** — how large the torn blocks are, in pixels. Small values give a
  fine digital hash; large ones give a few big slabs, which reads more like a
  broken frame than like noise. It is measured in real pixels because the look is
  deliberately pixel-scale, and it is scaled with the preview resolution so a
  draft matches the export.
- **Rows/columns jitter** — how far the grid's own lines wander, as a share of the
  block size. Without it the tear lines sit on an obvious regular grid; with it
  the blocks stop lining up and the damage looks less designed.
- **Displacement** — how far a torn block is pushed from where it belongs, across
  the composition diagonal. This is the control that decides whether the effect
  reads as texture or as the picture coming apart.
- **Channel offset** — how far a torn block's red and blue are split apart. It is
  the per-block version of an [RGB split](/effects/distortion/rgb-split/), and a
  little of it is what makes the damage look like video rather than like paper
  being cut up.
- **Slice repeat** — how often a block repeats a short strip of itself rather than
  showing its own content, the stuttering horizontal banding of a stalled decode.
- **Seed** — which random arrangement you get. Two layers sharing a seed break
  identically; change it when a particular burst lands badly.
- **Mix** — how much of the broken picture replaces the original.
