Chromatic aberration is a [Distortion](/effects/distortion/) effect.

## What it is for

The colour error real glass has. A lens does not bend every wavelength by quite
the same amount, so a picture taken through one shows coloured fringes that grow
toward the edges of the frame and vanish at its centre.

That is exactly what this effect does: three tinted samples offset radially from
the middle of the frame, so the centre stays clean and the corners fringe most.
It is the difference between footage that looks captured and footage that looks
generated, and a small amount of it goes a long way — most of the time you want
it to be felt rather than seen.

Use it on a whole montage from an adjustment layer to give everything the same
lens, and push it hard for a few frames as an impact effect when you want the
fringe to be the point.

Its sibling [RGB split](/effects/distortion/rgb-split/) does the same trick along
a straight angle you choose. Reach for that one on a beat and this one for
glass.

Like the split, custom tint colours recolour only where the three taps disagree,
so flat areas keep their original brightness.

## What each control does

- **Amount** — how far the taps are offset at the frame's edge, in pixels. It is
  authored in real pixels rather than as a share of the diagonal because a lens's
  colour error is a physical size; the value is scaled with the preview
  resolution so a draft scrub still frames like the export.
- **Colour 1**, **Colour 2** and **Colour 3** — the tints on the inward, centred
  and outward taps. The defaults reproduce the ordinary red-outward,
  blue-inward look of real glass; other colours give fringes that no lens would
  produce but that a montage might want.
- **Wavelength** — trades the three discrete taps for a smooth dispersion across
  the same offset. Worth turning on once the amount is large enough that the
  three taps read as three separate copies.
- **Samples** — how many taps fill the offset while Wavelength is on. It does
  nothing otherwise.
- **Mix** — how much of the fringed picture replaces the original.
