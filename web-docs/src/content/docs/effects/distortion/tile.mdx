Tile is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Making one picture fill a frame many times over. Tile cuts a rectangle out of
the layer and stamps it side by side until the frame is full — the fastest route
to a repeating pattern, a video wall, a kaleidoscope base, or the classic
"multiply the shot and offset the rows" music-video look.

It is also the quiet workhorse behind seamless textures: with **Mirror edges**
on, alternate stamps are flipped, so the copies meet edge to edge with nothing
visible at the join even when the source is not seamless at all.

A fresh Tile is a 2×2 repeat, so you can see what it does the moment you add it.

## What each control does

- **Tile centre x** and **Tile centre y** — where in the picture the rectangle is
  cut from. Move it to choose *which part* of the shot gets repeated; by default
  it is the middle.
- **Tile width** and **Tile height** — how big the rectangle is, as a percentage
  of the frame. 50 gives a 2×2 grid, 25 a 4×4, and the two can differ, which
  gives tall or wide tiles. Above 100 the rectangle is larger than the frame, so
  the source reaches beyond the picture and the outermost row of pixels is held
  outward to fill it.
- **Output width** and **Output height** — how much of the frame gets stamped, as
  a percentage, measured out from the centre. Below 100 the tiling sits in a
  window with transparency around it, which is how a tiled panel is placed
  inside a larger composition. Above 100 makes no visible difference: the frame
  is all there is to cover.
- **Mirror edges** — flips alternate stamps instead of butting copies together.
  Turn this on whenever the joins are showing. It halves the apparent repetition
  as well, because neighbouring tiles are no longer identical.
- **Phase** — slides every row of tiles sideways by a fraction of a tile, which
  is what stops the result reading as a grid. 180° is the brickwork offset;
  smaller angles give a gentle stagger.
- **Horizontal phase shift** — makes Phase slide the *columns* vertically instead
  of the rows horizontally. Same idea, turned ninety degrees.
- **Mix** — how much of the tiled picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Tile does not change the layer's size.** Everything happens inside the frame
the layer already renders at, so Output width above 100 has nothing extra to
cover. If you want tiles that spill beyond the composition, scale the layer down
first and let Tile fill what is left.

**The tile is cut from the picture as it arrives**, which means effects above
Tile in the stack are inside the tile and effects below it are applied to the
whole tiled result. Ordering it deliberately is most of the craft here.
