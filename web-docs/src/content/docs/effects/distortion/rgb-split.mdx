RGB split is a [Distortion](/effects/distortion/) effect.

## What it is for

Takes three samples of the picture, offset from one another along a straight
line, and tints each one. With the default colours that comes out as the
familiar red-and-blue fringe: red pulled one way, blue the other, and green left
where it is.

This is the impact-frame staple. A frame or two of a large offset on a hit,
animated back to nothing, reads as force in a way that almost nothing else this
cheap does. Held small and constant it is instead a subtle analogue imperfection
that stops a graphic feeling too clean.

The colours are yours to change. Because each tap is sampled in full colour and
then tinted, setting them to something other than red, green and blue gives a
fringe in those colours — a cyan and magenta split, a warm and cool one — while
the default primaries reproduce the classic channel-separated look exactly.

Custom colours only recolour the places where the three taps *disagree*, which
is the fringe itself. Flat areas of the picture, where all three taps land on
the same colour, pass through at their original brightness rather than being
tinted, so choosing unusual colours does not wash the whole frame.

For a fringe that grows radially out of the frame's centre instead of running
along one angle, use
[Chromatic aberration](/effects/distortion/chromatic-aberration/), which is this
effect's always-radial sibling.

## What each control does

- **Amount** — how far the taps are offset, across the composition diagonal so it
  survives a resize. This is the control to animate on a beat.
- **Angle** — which way the split runs. Horizontal is the familiar one; vertical
  reads quite differently, and an animated angle spins the fringe around the
  subject.
- **Red**, **Green** and **Blue** — per-tap scales on the offset, as percentages,
  so the three taps can fringe by different amounts. Red and Blue displace in
  opposite directions and Green sits at the anchor, which is why the defaults
  give the classic split. Bring Green off zero to knock the middle tap off its
  anchor too, and the whole picture starts to smear rather than just its edges.
  These apply to the ordinary mode only, not to Wavelength.
- **Colour 1**, **Colour 2** and **Colour 3** — the tints on the three taps. In
  the ordinary mode they colour the three taps directly. With Wavelength on they
  become a gradient across the offset instead, from the first at one end through
  the second in the middle to the third at the far end.
- **Wavelength** — switches from three discrete taps to a smooth dispersion. At
  small offsets the two look much alike; at large ones the three-tap version
  reads as three stacked copies while this reads as a continuous spectral fringe.
  It costs more, so turn it on when the offset is big enough to need it.
- **Samples** — how many taps fill the offset while Wavelength is on. More is
  smoother and slower; it has no effect in the ordinary mode.
- **Mix** — how much of the split picture replaces the original.
