Wave warp is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

A wave travels across the picture in the direction you choose, and the picture
slides *across* that direction as the wave passes — a flag rippling, a reflection
in moving water, a bad video signal.

Five wave shapes are on offer, and they are not decoration: a **Sine** is a soft
ripple, a **Square** is a stack of hard slices offset left and right, a
**Sawtooth** tears, a **Triangle** zigzags and a **Circle** scallops.

As with [Ripple](/effects/distortion/ripple/), the motion comes from a keyframed
**Phase** rather than a speed control, so what you see in the preview is what the
export writes.

## What each control does

- **Wave type** — the shape of the wave. Sine and Circle are smooth; Triangle is
  angular; Square and Sawtooth carry a step, which arrives as the hard slice it
  is meant to be.
- **Wave height** — how far the picture slides at a crest, in pixels at
  composition size. Negative is the same wave half a turn of Phase along.
- **Wave width** — the distance between crests. Together with Wave height this
  sets how violent the wave is: a tall wave in a narrow width folds the picture
  over itself.
- **Direction** — the direction the wave **travels**, in degrees from straight up,
  turning clockwise. The picture slides across it, a quarter turn round. At the
  default 90° the wave runs left to right and the picture slides up and down.
- **Phase** — where along the wave this frame is caught. Animate it linearly and
  the wave travels; the speed is yours to set.
- **Pinning** — which edges of the frame are nailed down. A pinned edge cannot
  move, so the wave fades out over the last **Wave height** pixels before it. All
  eight combinations are here, from a single edge to all four.
- **Mix** — how much of the waved picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Pinning is what makes it a flag rather than a ripple.** Pin one edge and the
picture is fixed there and free at the other, which is exactly how cloth behaves
when it is nailed to a pole. Pin all four and the wave lives in the middle of the
frame and the borders stay put.

**The edges repeat rather than fading.** An unpinned wave carries the picture off
the side of the frame, and something has to stand behind it; a transparent edge
would leave a hole where the crest was, which is not what a waving flag looks
like. If you want a clean edge, pin it.

**Direction names the wave's travel, not the picture's slide.** They are a quarter
turn apart. If the picture is sliding the wrong way, turn Direction by 90°, not
by 180°.

**Two shapes AE has are not here.** Its Noise and Smooth Noise wave types are a
noise field wearing a wave's clothes; if that is what you want, reach for
[Turbulent displace](/effects/distortion/turbulent-displace/), which is built on
the real thing and has a seed, an evolution and a complexity of its own.
