Offset is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Sliding the picture sideways or up and down, with the frame treated as a loop:
what leaves the left edge comes back in on the right. Nothing is ever revealed
and nothing is ever lost.

That wrap is the whole point. It gives you two things nothing else does:

- **A scrolling background out of a single still.** Keyframe Shift x across the
  shot and a seamless texture scrolls forever, with no second copy to line up and
  no join to hide.
- **A way to move a pattern without breaking it.** A tiled or procedural texture
  can be repositioned to sit better under the subject, and it stays seamless
  because the wrap is exact.

It is also the standard first step when checking whether a texture really is
seamless: offset it by half the frame, and any join you cannot see was not there.

## What each control does

- **Shift x** — how far the picture moves to the right, in pixels at composition
  size. Negative moves it left. A shift of exactly the frame's width puts the
  picture back where it started, so a scroll that loops is a keyframe pair from 0
  to the frame width.
- **Shift y** — how far the picture moves down; negative moves it up.
- **Mix** — how much of the shifted picture replaces the original. Because this
  *blends* rather than shortens the shift, a mid Mix shows both pictures at once,
  which is a double exposure rather than a smaller move. To move less, use a
  smaller shift.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**This is not the Transform effect.** [Transform](/effects/utility/transform/)
moves a layer and leaves emptiness behind it; Offset moves the picture *within*
its own frame and fills the gap with the other side of itself. Reach for
Transform to reposition a layer, and for Offset to reposition a texture.

**A whole-pixel shift is exact** — no resampling, no softening, however many times
it is applied. A fractional shift is sampled and therefore very slightly softer,
like any sub-pixel move.

**After Effects users:** AE's Offset asks for "Shift Center To", the point that
should end up in the middle. Lumit stores the shift itself, which is that point
minus the frame centre, because a shift is what keyframes sensibly — two linear
keys give a constant scrolling speed. An imported AE Offset is converted.
