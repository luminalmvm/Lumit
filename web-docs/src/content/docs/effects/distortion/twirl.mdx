Twirl is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Spinning a circular patch of the picture around its own centre so that straight
lines become spirals. The twist is strongest at the middle and fades to nothing at
the edge of the circle, so the effect blends into the untouched picture rather
than leaving a visible ring.

The obvious use is the whirlpool — water, smoke, a galaxy. The less obvious ones
are more common: a Twirl animated from a large angle to zero is a classic reveal
for a logo or a title, and a small Twirl on a noise layer turns straight streaks
into curling wisps for use as a displacement map or a matte.

## What each control does

- **Angle** — how far the middle is turned. Positive turns the picture clockwise
  on screen. Past about 180° the centre starts to smear rather than turn, which is
  a look in itself; for a clean spiral stay under a full turn.
- **Radius** — how wide the twirled circle is, as a percentage of the composition
  diagonal. Because it is a percentage rather than a pixel count, the same value
  covers the same part of the picture whatever size the composition is, and a
  half-resolution preview matches the export.
- **Centre X** and **Centre Y** — where the middle of the twirl sits, in pixels at
  composition size. A fresh Twirl starts in the middle of the frame.
- **Mix** — how much of the twirled picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Nothing outside the radius moves, at all.** The twist eases to exactly zero at
the rim, so you can drop a Twirl on a full frame and be certain the rest of the
shot is untouched. That also means stacking several Twirls at different centres
and radii is safe and is the usual way to build a churning surface.

**Animate the Angle, not the Radius.** Radius changing over time makes the
boundary of the circle sweep across the picture, and the eye follows it. Angle
changing over time makes the picture itself move, which is what you want. For a
reveal, keyframe Angle from something large down to 0 with an ease out.

**Its circle never reaches outside itself.** Turning a point about the centre does
not change its distance from the centre, so a Twirl can only ever rearrange what
is already inside its own circle. If part of that circle hangs off the frame, the
missing part comes back transparent.
