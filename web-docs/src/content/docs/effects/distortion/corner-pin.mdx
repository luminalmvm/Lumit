Corner pin is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Putting a picture onto a surface that is not facing the camera. Drag the four
corners of the layer onto the four corners of a phone screen, a billboard, a
laptop, a picture frame or a wall, and the layer sits there — narrowing where the
surface recedes, widening where it comes toward you.

That convergence is the whole point. Corner pin is a **projective** transform,
which is the same maths a real camera does: straight lines stay straight, but
parallel ones meet. Scaling and skewing a layer by hand cannot produce it, and the
difference is the difference between a screen replacement that sits in the shot
and one that floats on top of it.

The other everyday use is faking a camera move on a still: pin all four corners
slightly, keyframe them, and a flat plate leans and turns as if the camera had.

## What each control does

- **Upper left**, **Upper right**, **Lower left**, **Lower right** — where each of
  the frame's four corners ends up, in pixels at composition size. Put all four
  back on the frame's own corners and the effect does nothing; move one and the
  whole picture follows. A fresh Corner pin starts as a gentle keystone so you can
  see it working before you touch anything.
- **Edges** — what fills the area a corner has been dragged *away* from.
  **Transparent** leaves it empty, which is what a screen replacement wants and is
  what After Effects always does. **Repeat** smears the border pixel outward and
  **Mirror** reflects the picture back, both of which are useful when you are
  faking a camera move on a plate and want the frame to stay full.
- **Mix** — how much of the pinned picture replaces the original. Below 100 the
  two are blended, which is mostly useful for checking your alignment against the
  plate underneath.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Cross two corners and part of the picture vanishes.** Drag the upper left past
the upper right and the surface you have described folds back behind the camera.
Lumit draws nothing there. Some tools draw a mirrored ghost of the picture
instead, which looks like a bug because it is one — there genuinely is no image
behind the horizon of a perspective transform.

**A quad with no area does nothing at all.** Put three corners in a line, or two
on top of each other, and there is no perspective to compute; the effect renders
the picture untouched rather than filling the frame with noise. Pull a corner back
out and it resumes.

**Pin, then track.** For a moving shot, keyframe the four points to the tracked
corners of the surface. Corner pin is deliberately just the transform — it has no
tracker of its own, and the points are ordinary animatable numbers you can drive
from anywhere.
