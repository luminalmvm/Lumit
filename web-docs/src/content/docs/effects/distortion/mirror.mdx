Mirror is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Drawing a line through the picture and replacing everything on one side of it
with the reflection of what is on the other. **Centre** places the line, **Angle**
turns it and decides which side is replaced.

One Mirror is a symmetry. Several stacked at different angles is a kaleidoscope,
and that is the usual reason to reach for it: three or four Mirrors turned 45° or
60° apart will turn almost any footage into a spinning mandala, and animating the
angles makes it breathe.

The quieter uses are just as common — squaring off a lopsided background,
extending a set that only exists on one side of frame, or building a symmetrical
title element out of an asymmetrical one.

## What each control does

- **Centre X** and **Centre Y** — where the reflection line passes through. By
  default it is the middle of the frame. Sliding it along the line's own
  direction changes how much of the picture survives: move it left and less of
  the left half is kept, and more of the frame is reflection.
- **Angle** — which way the line runs, and which side gets replaced. At 0° the
  line is vertical and the **right** half becomes a mirror of the left; at 180°
  it is still vertical but the **left** half is replaced instead. 90° mirrors
  the bottom onto the top. A positive angle turns the line clockwise on screen.
- **Mix** — how much of the reflected picture replaces the original. Below 100
  the two are blended, so the reflected half shows through the original — a
  ghosted symmetry rather than a hard one.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The reflection can run out of picture.** Put the centre near an edge and the
half being replaced asks for content from beyond the frame, where there is none;
those pixels come out transparent. That is deliberate — repeating the border
pixel outward would smear it into a fan, which reads as a fault rather than as a
mirror. If you need the frame filled, move the centre back toward the middle or
put something behind the layer.

**Stacking is how you build a kaleidoscope.** Each Mirror only ever does one
reflection, so the pattern comes from the sequence: try 0°, then 90°, then 45°,
all on the frame centre, and animate a [Transform](/effects/utility/transform/)
underneath the stack so the source drifts through the symmetry.
