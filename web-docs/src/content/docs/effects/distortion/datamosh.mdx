## What it does

Imitates what a compressed video does when reference frames go missing.

The blurring/melting follows motion in the footage. Moving objects will smear and
static objects don't change at all.

:::note[This and Fast motion blur can share a layer]
Measuring motion is expensive, so a layer measures only what its effects
actually ask for. This effect needs the motion looking *backwards*, to the
previous frame; [Fast motion blur](/effects/temporal/fast-motion-blur/) needs it
looking *forwards*, to the next one. A layer carrying both measures both, and
each effect gets its own — so stacking them works. A layer carrying only one
costs exactly what it always did.
:::

## What each control does

- **Intensity** is how much of the prediction is laid over the current frame.
- **Displacement** the number of frames used to warp across.
- **Bloom** affects the falloff of older frames being used in the datamosh.
- **Reset interval** is how often the melt restarts clean, immitating reference frames.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).
