Warp is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Thirteen named bends, each of them a single slider deep. Pick the shape by its
name, turn **Bend** up, and use the two Distortion sliders to taper the result as
if the bent picture were seen at an angle.

This is the effect to reach for when the bend is a *look* rather than something to
be placed: a title arced over a logo, a lower third inflated like a balloon, a
still bulged for a stinger. When the bend has to line up with something in the
shot, [Bezier warp](/effects/distortion/bezier-warp/) is the one to drag.

## What each control does

- **Style** — which bend:
  - **Arc** bows the whole picture one way.
  - **Arc upper** and **Arc lower** bow only the top or only the bottom edge.
  - **Arch** bows top and bottom apart, fattening the middle.
  - **Bulge** swells the middle on both axes.
  - **Flag** puts one full wave across the width with every row in step.
  - **Wave** is the same wave with the top and bottom edges out of step.
  - **Fish** bows the sides out and tapers the ends.
  - **Rise** lifts the picture diagonally.
  - **Fisheye** magnifies outward from the middle and rounds the corners.
  - **Inflate** is the same swell with a softer, rounder falloff.
  - **Squeeze** crowds the rows toward the middle.
  - **Twist** turns the top one way and the bottom the other.
- **Bend** — how much of the style there is. **0** is no effect at all, for every
  style; the sign turns the bend the other way, which is often a completely
  different-looking result and worth trying.
- **Horizontal distortion** — tapers the picture between its left and right edges,
  as if the bent shape were turned away from the camera about a vertical axis.
- **Vertical distortion** — the same taper between top and bottom.
- **Mix** — how much of the bent picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Bend 0 is the exact identity, whichever style is chosen.** The picture comes
back pixel for pixel, so you can animate a warp on and off from nothing without a
frame of softening at the ends.

**Some of the styles are cousins, on purpose.** Bulge, Fish and Arch are one swell
on both axes, on the horizontal only and on the vertical only; Fisheye and Inflate
differ only in how quickly the swell falls off. They are separate entries because
people reach for them by look and by name, not by formula.

**The round styles follow the frame's shape, not a circle.** On a widescreen frame
a Fisheye reaches the left and right edges at the same Bend that reaches the top
and bottom, which is what makes it usable on a full frame. If you want a true
circular lens sitting on part of the picture, that is
[Spherize](/effects/distortion/spherize/). **Twist** is the exception and has to
be — an angle is not a shape, so a Bend of 100 really is a quarter turn.

**Outside the bent shape is transparent.** As with Bezier warp, the shape is the
point; put whatever should be behind it on a layer below.

**The tapers act on the bend, not the other way round.** A style followed by a
taper is a bent picture seen at an angle. That is the useful order, and it is the
one you get.
