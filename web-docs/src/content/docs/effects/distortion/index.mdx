## What this family does

Moving the picture, or parts of it, away from where it belongs. This is the
impact-frame family: the effects that make a cut hit, and the ones that make
footage look damaged on purpose.

**Splitting a picture into colours.** [RGB split](/effects/distortion/rgb-split/)
offsets the channels along a straight line you choose, which is the classic
impact fringe.
[Chromatic aberration](/effects/distortion/chromatic-aberration/) does the same
thing radially, growing out of the frame's centre the way a real lens's colour
error does. Reach for the first when you are hitting a beat and the second when
you are imitating glass.

**Moving the whole frame.** [Shake](/effects/distortion/shake/) wobbles the layer
on seeded noise — the beat-shake workhorse, and the quickest way to give a static
graphic some life.

**Breaking it.** [Block glitch](/effects/distortion/block-glitch/) tears the
picture into displaced blocks,
[Scanlines](/effects/distortion/scanlines/) lays a CRT line pattern over it, and
[Datamosh](/effects/distortion/datamosh/) melts one frame along the motion of the
next, imitating what a video codec does when its reference frames go missing.

## Restraint, and where these go

These effects are loud, and the two things that make them read as intentional
rather than as a mistake are duration and stacking. Most of them work best held
for two or three frames on a hit and then gone, which means animating them —
usually with **Mix**, which every one of them carries, since it needs nothing
re-measured.

Stacked in order they compound: Block glitch into Scanlines is the standard
broken-screen pair, and either into an RGB split adds the colour fringe that
sells it. To break a whole montage rather than one layer, put them on an
adjustment layer.

Several of these are driven by a seeded random pattern. The seed is a control,
so two copies of the same effect will glitch identically until you change it —
which is what you want when two layers should break together, and what you must
change when they should not.
