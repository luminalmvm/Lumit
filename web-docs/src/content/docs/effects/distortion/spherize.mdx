Spherize is a **Distortion** effect.

## What it is for

Holding a marble over the picture. Inside a circle the image swells outward, and
everything the middle pushed aside is squeezed into the last few pixels before the
rim — which is exactly what a real glass ball does, and why the effect reads as
glass rather than as a zoom.

Turn **Bulge** negative and it does the opposite, pinching the picture inward
toward the centre.

The everyday uses are a fisheye peephole, a water droplet on a lens, a magnifying
glass travelling across a map or a page, and — animated from 0 up and back — a
soft pulse or impact ripple on a title.

## What each control does

- **Radius** — how big the ball is, as a percentage of the composition diagonal.
  Being a percentage rather than a pixel count, it covers the same part of the
  picture whatever size the composition is, and a half-resolution preview matches
  the export.
- **Bulge** — which way the glass bends and how much of it there is. **100** is a
  full sphere; **0** is no effect at all; **−100** is the exact opposite, a pinch.
  The values in between read as a shallower lens rather than a smaller one, which
  is what makes Bulge the control to animate.
- **Centre X** and **Centre Y** — where the ball sits, in pixels at composition
  size. A fresh Spherize starts in the middle of the frame. Animate these to slide
  a magnifying glass across the picture.
- **Mix** — how much of the bulged picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's brightness;
  see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**A bulge and a pinch cancel exactly.** Stack a Spherize at +100 and another at
−100 with the same Radius and Centre and you get the picture back (give or take
the softening of two resamples). They are genuinely opposite transforms rather
than a sign flipped on a number, which is what makes the negative half of the
slider trustworthy.

**The rim is meant to be crowded.** Detail piles up in the last few pixels before
the edge of the ball, because that is where a sphere puts everything it magnified
away from the middle. If it reads as too harsh, lower Bulge rather than Radius —
lowering Radius just makes a smaller ball with the same crowded rim.

**Nothing outside the radius moves.** As with [Twirl](/effects/distortion/twirl/),
the effect is exactly bounded by its own circle, so it is safe to drop on a full
frame. If the circle hangs off the edge of the frame, the part with nothing to
read comes back transparent.

**For a real lens, reach for something else.** Spherize is a local bulge, not an
optical model. To correct or add the barrel distortion of an actual lens across
the whole frame, use [Lens distort](/effects/distortion/lens-distort/), which is
described in field of view rather than in radius.
