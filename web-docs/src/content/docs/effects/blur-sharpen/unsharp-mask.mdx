Unsharp mask is a [Blur & sharpen](/effects/blur-sharpen/) effect.

## What it is for

The controlled way to sharpen. It compares the picture against a blurred copy of
itself and adds back the difference, so what gets lifted is detail at the size
you choose rather than every pixel-to-pixel step.

Reach for it after anything that costs sharpness: a scale-down, a Retime, a
blur elsewhere in the stack, or heavy compression on the source capture. It is
also the honest fix for footage that was simply shot or streamed soft.

The reason to prefer it over plain [Sharpen](/effects/blur-sharpen/sharpen/) is
the pair of controls that keep it from ruining the picture: a radius, so you
choose which scale of detail is lifted, and a threshold, so flat areas are left
alone and compression noise is not amplified into a moving crust.

Sharpening is a local exaggeration, so it is easy to overdo. Bright and dark
halos along high-contrast edges are the tell.

## What each control does

- **Amount** — how strongly the recovered detail is added back. Past a point this
  stops reading as sharpness and starts reading as edge haloes.
- **Radius** — the size of detail that gets lifted, as a share of the composition
  diagonal. Small values sharpen fine texture; larger ones lift broader shapes
  and read more as local contrast than as sharpness.
- **Threshold** — how much difference a pixel must show before it is lifted at
  all. This is what protects flat sky, skin and compression blocks from having
  their noise amplified: raise it until the grain stops crawling, and leave the
  real edges to sail past it.
- **Luminance only** — sharpens brightness while leaving colour alone. On
  compressed capture, whose colour is stored at lower resolution than its
  brightness, this avoids pulling coloured fringes out along every edge. It is
  usually the right choice on game footage.
- **Mix** — how much of the sharpened picture replaces the original.
