## What it does

Removes the noise a camera adds to footage. Frames either side, lined up along the motion
between them, are averaged in where they agree with this one. The noise left is then taken
out of each frame at four sizes, fine to broad, less where there is edge or texture.

Both work from a **noise profile**: how much noise the shot has in brightness and in colour,
at each of the four sizes, and in its shadows, midtones and highlights.

**Learn** measures the profile on the flattest patch of the frame at the playhead, and puts
the sample square on the patch it measured. It reads the layer as Denoise is handed it, and
writes the profile as one undo step. Where it finds nothing it can read, such as a black
frame, a frame with no noise or the playhead outside the layer, the profile stays as it was.
A Denoise on a clip's rack is measured only with the playhead on that clip.

The frames either side are the layer's own, and only a Denoise that is the first enabled
effect on the layer reads them. Anywhere else in the stack it works as at **Frames** 0. A
frame from another clip of a Sequence layer is not averaged in, even across a cut that
removed nothing. Nor is one that repeats a frame already read, as on a held or slowed layer,
or a Precomp or adjustment layer over a freeze, a still or a slower frame rate.

## The sample square

With the effect selected, the square is drawn on the Viewer. With the **Selection** tool,
dragging it moves it and dragging a corner resizes it. The noise is measured again where it
is let go. Where there is nothing to read, the square still moves and the noise rows stay as
they were. Right-clicking it has **Learn** and **Reveal in Effect controls**. A square whose
rows an expression drives cannot be dragged. **Guides ▸ Layer controls** hides it.

## The display

Above the controls are the measured patch and the noise at each size, for luma and for
chroma. The line under them reads **Quality** as a per cent. Below 60 it gives the reason:

| Reason | The sample |
| --- | --- |
| **Small sample** | Is too small to measure the broad sizes. |
| **Detail in the sample** | Has picture in it as well as noise. |
| **Clipped** | Is partly pure black or pure white. |
| **Little noise** | Has too little noise to measure. |

**Nothing to measure** means there was no picture at the playhead, or Learn found nothing
there it could read.

## What each control does

- **View**: **Result** is the picture. **Removed noise** is what was taken away, four times
  over, on mid grey. **Frames blended** is white where every frame either side was averaged
  in and black where none was.
- **Learn**: measure the noise at the playhead, on the flattest patch of the frame.
- **Sample x** and **Sample y**: the centre of the patch the noise is measured on.
- **Sample size**: the length of the patch's side.
- **Luma noise** and **Chroma noise**: how much noise the shot has in brightness and in
  colour, in eight-bit code values.

The **Noise by size** and **Noise by tone** groups hold the rest of the profile:

- **Luma fine**, **Luma medium**, **Luma coarse** and **Luma broad**: the noise at each size,
  against Luma noise.
- **Chroma fine**, **Chroma medium**, **Chroma coarse** and **Chroma broad**: the same,
  against Chroma noise.
- **Shadows**, **Midtones** and **Highlights**: the luma noise at each brightness, against
  Luma noise.

The **Temporal** group:

- **Frames**: how many frames either side are averaged in. 0 turns the averaging off.
- **Follow motion**: lines the frames up along the motion before averaging them. Off, they
  are averaged where they stand.
- **Temporal strength**: how different a frame either side may be and still be averaged in.

The **Spatial** and **Spatial by size** groups:

- **Luma** and **Chroma**: how much of the noise left in brightness and in colour is taken.
- **Fine**, **Medium**, **Coarse** and **Broad**: how much is taken at each size.

The **Finish** group:

- **Detail**: keeps fine texture that stands above the noise.
- **Sharpen**: lifts fine detail where it is picture and not noise.
- **Grain**: puts back this much of the fine noise taken away.

Additional properties:

- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Matte**, **Invert** and **Channel**: scale the reduction by another layer. The Removed
  noise view shows it.

## Commands

**Effect ▸ Learn noise** (`Alt+L`) presses **Learn**. **Effect ▸ Only what is removed**
(`Alt+N`) switches **View** between Result and Removed noise. Both act on the effect
selected in Effect controls. With none selected there, they act on the first effect in the
selected layer's stack that has a Learn button, or a view of what it removes.
