Gaussian blur is a [Blur & sharpen](/effects/blur-sharpen/) effect.

## What it is for

The even softening: every pixel's light is spread the same distance in every
direction. This is the blur to reach for when you have no reason to reach for
another one.

Typical work: pushing a background back so a foreground subject reads against
it, taking the hard digital edge off a graphic or a title, softening a plate
before a grade so compression blocks stop showing, and animating a layer out of
focus over a few frames as a transition.

It blurs the picture and its transparency together, so a blurred logo or a keyed
subject softens at its outline rather than growing a bright or dark fringe
there. Along the frame's own borders it holds the edge pixel, so a full-frame
blur does not darken toward the sides.

In the Effect Controls panel:

- **Matte** scales the blur radius per pixel: white blurs at the full Radius, grey blurs narrowly, black not at all — a blur whose width varies across the frame, which a strength dissolve cannot produce. **Invert** reads the matte the other way round, light for dark.

## What each control does

- **Radius** — how far each pixel's light is spread. It is measured as a share of
  the composition diagonal rather than in pixels, which is what lets the same
  setting look the same after you resize the composition, and lets a Quarter
  preview read as the export does. Low values soften; high ones dissolve the
  picture into fields of colour. Cost barely rises with the number, so a very
  wide blur is a reasonable thing to ask for.
- **Mix** — how much of the blurred picture replaces the original. It is the way
  to ease an effect back without disturbing the setting above it, and it
  animates, so a layer can come into focus over time from Mix alone.
