## What it does

A depth of field blur traced through a real lens prescription. The out-of-focus shape
changes across the frame and through focus as that lens's does: cat's eyes towards the
corners, bright or soft edges either side of focus, and coloured fringes from the glass.
Something near and blurred goes soft at its edges over what is behind it, which shows
through, the way a real lens sees a little round it.
Bright pixels are drawn as whole bokeh shapes.

**Ray traced** follows rays from each pixel back through every surface of the lens to where
they meet the depth instead. It shows the lens's own softness wide open and in the
corners, which Lens shapes leaves out near focus. What a near thing hides is filled in
from what is around it.

Pointed at the effect's own layer, when that layer is a Precomp with 3D layers, **Depth**
gives Ray traced the layers themselves, so what a near layer hides is still there to be
seen round its edge.

:::note[Ray traced in a 3D precomp]
- Up to 12 3D layers. With more, rays meet the precomp's distance instead.
- Masks, track mattes, blend modes and motion blur on the 3D layers are not seen by the
  rays.
- 2D layers are seen as if far away.
:::

**Depth** reads distance in metres from a Precomp with 3D layers in it, and any other
layer as a depth pass from 0 to 1. On an adjustment layer with 3D layers below it, Depth
pointed at the adjustment layer reads how far those layers are. Without it, the whole frame
sits at **Farthest (m)**.

## What each control does

- **Depth** is how far away each pixel is. A Precomp with 3D layers in it gives distance
  in metres, the effect's own layer included. Any other layer is a depth pass, 0 at
  **Nearest (m)** and 1 at **Farthest (m)**.
- **Depth channel** selects the channel read as depth.
- **Invert** reads the depth pass inverted.
- **Method**: **Lens shapes** spreads each pixel by the blur shape traced for it. **Ray
  traced** follows rays from each pixel back through the lens and takes the colour where
  each meets the depth.
- **Rays** is how many rays Ray traced follows for each pixel, shared between red, green
  and blue. The Viewer and the export use the same count.
- **Lens** picks a bundled lens. A lens chosen in the lens window replaces it.
- **Edit lens…** opens the lens window. See [The lens window](#the-lens-window).
- **F-stop** is the working aperture. It cannot open wider than the lens.
- **Focus distance (m)** is where the lens is focused, in metres.
- **Use focus point** with **Focus point x** and **Focus point y** focus on the depth
  under that spot.
- **Sensor** is the sensor size the footage was shot on. A smaller sensor sees less of
  the lens's edge and blurs less.
- **Blur size** scales every blur after the optics.
- **Colour fringing** scales how much the glass splits colour.
- **Glass dirt** smears and specks the front glass. It shows as streaks inside the
  out-of-focus highlights.

The **Iris** group shapes the aperture:

- **Blades** is how many straight edges the iris has.
- **Roundness** blends the blades towards a circle.
- **Rotation** rotates the iris.

The **Highlights** group:

- **Highlight threshold** is the brightness above which a pixel is drawn as its own bokeh
  shape.
- **Highlight boost** multiplies what is above the threshold.

The **Depth map** group:

- **Nearest (m)** is the distance a depth of 0 means.
- **Farthest (m)** is the distance a depth of 1 means.

Additional properties:

- **Display**: **Final** is the blur, **Depth** the distance in metres it used.
- **Zebra**: stripes over what blurs less than a pixel. They are part of the picture, in
  exports too.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Matte**, **Invert** and **Channel** limit the effect to where another layer is bright.

In a [node graph](/use/node-graphs/), **Depth** is a socket that takes a depth or a
picture, the **Depth** view hands on the distance, **Focus distance (m)** hands out where
the lens is focused, for Lens flare's **Focus distance (m)** to follow, **F-stop** its
aperture, for Lens dirt's, and **Glass curvature** how far the lens's front glass bulges,
for Lens dirt's too.
With **Use focus point** on, the **Focus distance (m)** output still hands out the row's
value.

## The lens window

**Edit lens…** opens the **Lens** window, the same on Depth of field pro and on Lens flare.
Its list holds **In this effect**, **Built in** and, once any are saved, **Your lenses**.
**Import…** opens a `.lens` file. A file that is not one is refused: "That file is not a
lens."

Above the open lens are its name and **Focal length**, 1 to 2000 mm. Below them is a
cross-section: the glass, the iris in amber at the current F-stop, and 21 parallel rays,
green where they reach the sensor line and red where an edge or the iris stops them.

- **F-stop** runs from the lens's widest to 22. **Use lens** writes it to the effect when it
  was moved and the effect's F-stop is not keyed.
- **Field angle** tilts the rays off the axis. It changes only the drawing.

Every surface is a row:

| Column | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Radius** | The surface's radius of curvature, in mm. 0 is flat. The stop row has none. |
| **Thickness** | The distance to the next surface, in mm. |
| **Index** | The refractive index of what follows the surface. |
| **Abbe** | The Abbe number of what follows the surface. |
| **Diameter** | How far from the axis the surface reaches, in mm. |
| **Coating** | 0 bare, 1 single layer, 2 and up multicoat. |
| **Stop** | The iris. One row is the stop. |

**Add surface** adds a row, up to 40. **Remove surface**, the cross at the end of a row,
takes one away, down to three.

- **Cancel** closes the window and changes nothing.
- **Use lens** puts the lens on the effect. An edited or imported lens is kept in the
  effect and saved with the project.
- **Save to your lenses** saves it to your own lenses, offered in every project. Bundled
  lenses are never changed. A save that fails says "The lens could not be saved."
