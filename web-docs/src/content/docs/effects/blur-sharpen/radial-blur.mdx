Radial blur is a [Blur & sharpen](/effects/blur-sharpen/) effect.

## What it is for

A blur that runs around a centre, or away from it. Spin smears each pixel along
the arc it would travel if the frame were rotating; Zoom smears it along the ray
from the centre outward, which reads as the camera rushing in.

Zoom on a punch-in is the montage move: put the centre on whatever the cut is
about, animate the amount up for two or three frames and back down, and the
frame appears to slam toward the subject. Spin does the same job for a rotation,
and at a low amount it is a convincing way to suggest a lens that cannot quite
keep up.

The spread is not uniform across the frame. It is smallest at the centre — where
it is nothing at all — and reaches its full size at whichever corner is furthest
away. That is why moving the centre changes the character of the effect and not
just its position: it decides which part of the picture stays readable.

## What each control does

- **Amount** — the peak spread, reached at the frame's furthest corner from the
  centre. Pixels nearer the centre get proportionally less, and the pixel under
  the centre gets none. It may be pushed past the diagonal for a full dissolve.
- **Centre X** and **Centre Y** — where the spin or zoom originates, as a
  percentage of the composition's width and height, so the middle of the frame
  is fifty and fifty. Animate the pair to follow a moving subject and the punch
  stays anchored to it.
- **Type** — Spin sweeps along arcs about the centre, the rotating look; Zoom
  runs along rays out of it, the rushing look.
- **Edges** — what the sweep finds when it reaches past the frame. Transparent
  leaves the border clear, which is right over a plate that will be composited;
  Repeat holds the outermost pixel, which keeps a full-frame shot from darkening
  at the sides; Mirror reflects the picture back in, which hides the seam best
  on textured footage.
- **Mix** — how much of the swept picture replaces the original.
