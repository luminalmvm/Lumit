Gamma is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Bends the middle of the picture while leaving both ends where they are. Black
stays black and white stays white; everything between them slides up or down.

That is what makes it different from [Exposure](/effects/colour/exposure/),
which moves the whole picture including the highlights, and from
[Contrast](/effects/colour/contrast/), which moves the two ends apart. When a
shot is roughly right but the mid-tones are muddy — a dark interior, a night
capture, shadow detail that has fallen away — this is the control that fixes it
without blowing the highlights out.

The number reads the way a display gamma does: above the neutral point lifts the
mid-tones and brightens, below it lowers them. Scene-linear highlights above
white are curved honestly rather than clipped, and a picture that started
between black and white stays between them.

## What each control does

- **Gamma** — the mid-tone bend. Above neutral brightens the middle, below it
  darkens. It cannot reach zero, since the curve has to stay finite, but it can
  be typed well past the top of the slider for a heavily crushed or lifted look.
- **Mix** — how much of the curved picture replaces the original.
