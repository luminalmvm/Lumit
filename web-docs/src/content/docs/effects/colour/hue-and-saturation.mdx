Hue and saturation is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Turns the colour wheel, changes how colourful the picture is, and raises or
lowers how bright it is — either everywhere, or only for one family of colours.

The Master group does it to everything. The six range groups do it to the reds,
yellows, greens, cyans, blues or magentas alone, which is the move this effect
exists for: grass pulled toward teal without touching skin, a red jersey
desaturated without flattening the rest of the frame, a sky lifted while the
ground stays put.

The ranges have no edges to tune, and no edge to catch you out. Each is widest at
its own colour and fades to nothing at its neighbours, so a colour sitting
between two ranges takes a share of each and never more than one range's worth
in total — a gradient crossing from green to cyan changes smoothly rather than
stepping as it passes a boundary.

Greys take the Master adjustment only. A grey has no colour to belong to a range,
so lifting the reds cannot quietly darken every neutral in the frame — which it
would if the effect took a grey's technically-zero hue at face value.

Lightness is a gain, not a fade toward white: there is no white to fade toward in
a scene-linear picture, where a highlight can be many times brighter than paper.
Full negative takes a colour to black, full positive doubles it, and nothing
clips on the way.

[Hue shift](/effects/colour/hue-shift/) is still the effect to reach for when all
you want is one dial: it holds perceived brightness as the hue turns, which this
one does not.

## What each control does

- **Hue** — degrees round the colour wheel. Wraps, and winds through whole turns
  when animated rather than stopping at 360.
- **Saturation** — how colourful. Zero passes the picture through, −100 takes the
  group to grey, +100 doubles it, and there is no ceiling above.
- **Lightness** — a gain on brightness. −100 takes the group to black, +100
  doubles it.
- **Master** applies to every pixel. **Reds**, **Yellows**, **Greens**,
  **Cyans**, **Blues** and **Magentas** apply by how close a pixel's colour is to
  that range, and by how colourful the pixel already is.
- **Mix** — how much of the graded picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's
  brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).
