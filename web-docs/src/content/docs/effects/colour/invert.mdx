Invert is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Flips the picture's colour: light becomes dark, and each colour becomes its
opposite. There is nothing to set — it either inverts or, through Mix, inverts
part of the way.

Most of its use is not as a look. Inverting a layer that is being used as a
matte swaps what is kept for what is removed, which is often quicker than
rebuilding the matte the other way round. Inverting for a few frames is a
classic impact flash. And inverting a duplicate layer under a blend mode is the
basis of several difference-based tricks.

The inverse is taken in scene-linear light exactly as it is stored, which is the
deliberately simple choice. Values above white invert to honest negatives rather
than being clipped first, so an inverted highlight carries real information
rather than a flat patch. Transparency is left alone, so a keyed subject inverts
without its edges fringing.

In the Effect Controls panel:

- **Matte** scales how much of the effect each pixel gets: white applies it in full, black leaves the pixel as it arrived, grey part way. **Invert** reads the matte the other way round, light for dark.

## What each control does

- **Mix** — how much of the inverted picture replaces the original. It is the
  only control, and it is the interesting one: partway between the two is a
  washed, flattened picture, and animating it is how the invert flash is timed
  to a beat.
