Posterize is a **Colour** effect.

## What it is for

A photograph has millions of shades. Posterize keeps a handful of them and
snaps every pixel to the nearest, so a smooth gradient turns into flat bands.
It is the screen-print and poster look, the first half of a cel-shaded cartoon,
and a quick way to make footage read as graphic rather than photographic.

Each of the red, green and blue channels is stepped separately, which is why a
low Levels count gives you bold flat colours rather than a grey staircase.

## What each control does

- **Levels** — how many shades each channel keeps. **2** is a two-tone print,
  **8** is the poster look, and anything past about 32 is hard to see at all.
  Animate it to build a picture up out of its own bands.
- **Mix** — how much of the banded picture replaces the original. Below 100 the
  bands soften back toward the photograph, which is a gentler version of the
  same look.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — band only part of the frame, driven by another
  layer's brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The bands are spread where you can see them.** Lumit works in scene-linear
light, which is a measure of photons rather than of what the eye does — so
steps spaced evenly in light would pile up in the highlights and leave the
shadows almost smooth. The steps here are spaced perceptually instead, so eight
levels give you eight visible bands rather than two and a gradient.

**Bright highlights keep going.** A specular above white is banded on the same
ladder rather than snapped down to white, so a posterized picture still has
values for [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/) to find.

**It works on the pixel's own colour, not its transparency.** A soft edge stays
soft: coverage is untouched, so a title does not gain a jagged outline when you
band it.

**For a two-tone black-and-white stencil**, [Threshold](/effects/colour/threshold/)
is the effect — Posterize at Levels 2 keeps colour, and Threshold does not.
