Exposure is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

The brightness lever, in the unit a camera uses. One stop is a doubling of the
light, so plus one makes the picture twice as bright and minus one halves it.

This is the first move in most grades: get the picture to a sensible brightness
before deciding anything about its colour. It is also the cleanest way to match
two shots captured minutes apart, or to bring a dark capture up without the crushing
that a contrast control would cause.

It scales the light rather than remapping it, so nothing is clipped on the way
through. Highlights pushed above white stay above white and keep their detail,
which matters if anything downstream — a [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/), a
[LUT](/effects/colour/lut/) — is going to read them.

## What each control does

- **Stops** — the doubling count. Whole numbers are doublings and halvings, and
  fractional values are the fine adjustments you will actually spend most of
  your time on. The slider covers the range worth dragging and you can type
  further in either direction for a badly exposed source.
- **Mix** — how much of the exposed picture replaces the original.
