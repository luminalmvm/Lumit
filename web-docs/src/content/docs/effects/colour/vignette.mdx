Vignette is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Darkens the frame away from its centre. It is the oldest attention trick there
is: the eye goes to the brightest part of the picture, so pulling the corners
down keeps it on the subject.

A light one is invisible and does its work anyway — most graded footage carries
one. A heavy one is a look in its own right, and pushed far enough it becomes a
soft round mask through which the shot is seen, which is a serviceable
lens-scope or spyglass effect.

It darkens the picture and its transparency together as a single multiply, so it
sits happily on a layer that will be composited rather than on a full-frame
plate only.

The shape is described in a distance measured from the centre and normalised to
the frame, so it survives a composition resize without being re-set.

## What each control does

- **Amount** — how dark the darkened region gets. At zero the picture passes
  through untouched. This is the control to animate if you want the vignette to
  breathe with the music.
- **Radius** — how far the clear middle reaches before darkening starts. Small
  values close the vignette in toward the centre; large ones push it out to the
  corners so only the very edges are touched.
- **Softness** — the width of the fade between clear and dark. Narrow is a hard
  circular edge, which reads as a mask; wide is the gradual falloff that reads as
  a lens. It can be pushed past the frame's own corner distance for a very
  gradual wash, which is why it has no ceiling.
- **Roundness** — whether the shape is a true circle or follows the frame's own
  proportions. Fully round is a circle, which on a wide frame leaves the left and
  right edges darker than the top and bottom; at zero it becomes an ellipse that
  reaches every edge equally.
- **Ramp** — the shape of the falloff curve, as distinct from its width. At the
  neutral point it is the plain smooth fade. Above it, the darkening arrives
  later and then more suddenly; below it, earlier and more gently. Reach for this
  when the width is right but the vignette is drawing attention to itself.
- **Mix** — how much of the darkened picture replaces the original.
