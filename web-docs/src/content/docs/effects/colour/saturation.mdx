Saturation is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

How colourful the picture is. At zero it is greyscale; at the neutral point it
passes through unchanged; above that the colours intensify.

The scale is even: every pixel's colourfulness is multiplied by the same amount,
whether it started grey or vivid. That is what you want for an overall lift or
for a deliberate desaturated look, and it is what you do not want when the
picture already has saturated areas that will clip while the muted ones are
still catching up. For that,
[Vibrancy](/effects/colour/vibrancy/) is the better tool.

Common uses: a modest lift so a montage reads punchier than the capture, a
heavy pull-down for a moody or monochrome section, and taking a layer to
greyscale so it can serve as a matte or a texture rather than as a picture.

It scales colour about the picture's own brightness, using the standard
broadcast weighting, so pulling saturation out leaves brightness where it was
rather than darkening the greens and lightening the blues.

## What each control does

- **Saturation** — the multiplier on colourfulness, as a percentage. Zero is
  greyscale and the neutral point passes through untouched. There is no real
  ceiling: the slider reaches a heavy boost and typing goes further, because the
  mix between grey and colour keeps extrapolating past it — which is a
  legitimate, if aggressive, look.
- **Mix** — how much of the adjusted picture replaces the original. Between this
  and the control above there are two ways to get a half-strength result; they
  are not the same, since Mix blends against the untouched picture while
  Saturation moves the target.
