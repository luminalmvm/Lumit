Hue shift is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Rotates every colour in the picture around the colour wheel. Red becomes orange,
then yellow, then green, and so on round; a full turn brings everything back to
where it started.

It is a blunt instrument by design — it moves all colours, not a chosen range —
which makes it a look tool rather than a correction one. Animated, it is the
rainbow cycle that half the genre uses on a drop; held at a modest angle, it
shifts a whole palette without the work of grading each part of it. It is also
the quickest way to try a scene in a different colour scheme before committing
to the version built out of [Colour balance](/effects/colour/colour-balance/).

The rotation happens in scene-linear light, like every other grade here.

## What each control does

- **Angle** — how far round the wheel every colour is turned. It wraps, so any
  angle is legal and an animated value can keep spinning through whole turns
  rather than snapping back.
- **Preserve luminance** — whether brightness is held as the hue turns. On, the
  rotation is weighted so a colour keeps its perceived brightness: a saturated
  green stays as bright as it was, a blue stays as dark. Off, brightness is free
  to ride along with the hue, the way a naive colour wheel behaves, so colours
  visibly lighten and darken as they turn. On is the safer choice for grading;
  off is the more energetic one for an animated cycle.
- **Mix** — how much of the rotated picture replaces the original.
