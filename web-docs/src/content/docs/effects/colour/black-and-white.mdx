Black and white is a **Colour** effect.

## What it is for

Turning a colour picture grey throws information away, and *which* information
decides whether the result is a photograph or a mush. A flat conversion gives a
red jumper and the green grass behind it the same grey, and the jumper vanishes.

This effect asks six questions instead — how bright should reds be, and yellows,
and greens, and cyans, and blues, and magentas — so you can darken a sky until
the clouds stand out, lift skin so a face reads, or separate two colours that
happen to have the same brightness. It is what a red filter on black-and-white
film always did, with the filter chosen after the shoot.

## What each control does

- **Reds**, **Yellows**, **Greens**, **Cyans**, **Blues**, **Magentas** — how
  bright each colour comes out in the grey. Raise one to lift everything of that
  colour, lower it to darken. The classic move is **Blues** down to darken a sky
  and **Reds** up to lift skin and brick.
- **Tint** and **Tint colour** — colour the grey picture. A warm brown is a
  sepia print, a mid blue is a cyanotype. Tint colour changes the *hue* of the
  result and not its brightness, so you can try a dark tint without the picture
  going dark with it.
- **Mix** — how much of the conversion replaces the original. Below 100 you get
  a partly desaturated picture, which is a different and often better look than
  pulling [Saturation](/effects/colour/saturation/) down.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — convert only part of the frame, which is how a
  single object is kept in colour; see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The six sliders do nothing at all to a grey.** The maths decomposes each
colour exactly into a grey plus a bit of one colour and a bit of another, so a
neutral pixel has nothing for the sliders to act on. That is what makes them
safe to push: a shot with a lot of neutral in it does not drift while you tune
one colour.

**There are no seams.** A gradient that runs from red through orange to yellow
crosses from one slider's territory to another smoothly, because the two agree
exactly where they meet.

**Nothing clips.** Pushing a slider to 300 lifts a specular highlight well past
white rather than flattening it, so a converted picture still has headroom for
[Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/). The grey is only prevented from going *below*
black.

**For a plain desaturation**, [Saturation](/effects/colour/saturation/) at
&minus;100 is the shorter route. Use this when the picture needs its colours
told apart.
