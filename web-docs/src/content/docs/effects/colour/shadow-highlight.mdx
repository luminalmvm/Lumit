## What it does

The effect lifts dark *regions* of a picture, and can pulls bright regions down.

Whether a pixel counts as a shadow or highlight is based on how bright its 
*surroundings* are, and not just its own brightness.

In the Effect Controls panel:

- **More options** groups **Colour correction**, **Midtone contrast**, and starts closed.
- **Matte** scales how much of the effect each pixel gets: white applies it in full, black leaves the pixel as it arrived, grey part way. **Invert** reads the matte the other way round, light for dark.

## What each control does

- **Shadow amount** — amount to lift dark regions.
- **Shadow tonal width** — range that counts as a shadow. 
- **Highlight amount** — amount to pull down highligh regions.
- **Highlight tonal width** — range that counts as a highlight.
- **Radius** — the area to compare against to determine if a pixel is a shadow/highlight.
- **Colour correction** — how much saturation is added back where the picture was adjusted.
- **Midtone contrast** — adjusts contrast of midtones, since they can be otherwise end up crushed.
- **Mix**: how much of the result replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** drive the strength of the effect from another layer's brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**For a global lift rather than a local one**, use
[Levels](/effects/colour/levels/), [Curves](/effects/colour/curves/) or
[Exposure](/effects/colour/exposure/). They are cheaper and more predictable
if the whole picture needs the same adjustment.
