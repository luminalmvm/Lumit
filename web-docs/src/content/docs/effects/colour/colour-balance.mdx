Colour balance is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

The full grade in one effect: three colours, each steering a different part of
the brightness range. **Lift** moves the shadows, **Gamma** the mid-tones and
**Gain** the highlights, and because each is a colour rather than a number, each
can push its part of the picture toward any hue.

This is the effect to reach for when a single-knob control cannot express what
is wrong. A cast that only affects the shadows, a highlight that has gone green,
the teal-and-orange split where shadows go cool and skin stays warm — all of
those need the ranges separated, and this is where they are.

It is also the slowest to learn, which is why the one-knob effects exist beside
it. If the problem is simply that everything is too warm, use
[Temperature](/effects/colour/temperature/). Come here when the problem is that
only part of the picture is.

The three ranges overlap rather than being cut apart from one another, so a
change to lift still influences the mid-tones a little. That is the point:
grading by hard bands leaves visible boundaries where they meet.

## What each control does

- **Lift** — the shadows. Pushing it toward a colour tints the dark end of the
  picture, and lifting it overall raises the black point, which is the flat,
  filmic look where nothing is fully black.
- **Gamma** — the mid-tones. This is where most of a picture lives, and where a
  cast is most visible, so it is usually the first of the three to touch.
- **Gain** — the highlights. It scales the bright end, so a tint here colours the
  sky, the practicals and the specular hits while leaving shadows alone.
- **Mix** — how much of the graded picture replaces the original.

Each of the three opens the colour picker, which carries a screen dropper — so
you can sample a cast directly off the picture rather than guessing at it. See
[Effect Controls](/panels/effect-controls/).
