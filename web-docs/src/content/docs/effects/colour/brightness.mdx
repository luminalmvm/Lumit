Brightness is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Adds light to every pixel, and spreads the picture about mid-grey — the two
knobs After Effects keeps together under Brightness & Contrast, in one effect
with the same names and the same neutral point of zero.

It exists as its own effect rather than as a mode of
[Contrast](/effects/colour/contrast/) so that a project imported from After
Effects lands as one effect with one animated pair, the way it was authored,
instead of being split across two entries with two different spellings of the
same idea. Lumit's own Contrast is untouched and still reads as a percentage
where 100 is neutral; this one is signed, where 0 is neutral.

Brightness is a straight addition in linear light, so it lifts the shadows as
much as the highlights — flatter than [Exposure](/effects/colour/exposure/),
which multiplies and therefore leaves black where it was. That difference is the
reason to keep both: exposure for a photographic correction, brightness for a
deliberate wash.

Highlights are never clipped on the way through.

## What each control does

- **Brightness** — light added to every channel. At full deflection either way
  it is a whole unit of scene-linear light, which is a lot; small numbers are the
  usual ones.
- **Contrast** — the spread about mid-grey. Zero passes the picture through, −100
  flattens the frame to grey, +100 doubles the spread, and there is no ceiling
  above.
- **Mix** — how much of the graded picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's
  brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).
