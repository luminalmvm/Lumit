Tritone is a **Colour** effect.

## What it is for

The picture's own colours are thrown away and replaced by a ramp of three you
choose. The darkest pixels take the Shadows colour, the brightest take the
Highlights colour, and everything between is mixed through the Midtones colour.

It is how a photograph becomes a cyanotype or a sepia print, how a trailer gets
its two-colour title card, and a fast way to make mismatched footage from four
sources look like one thing.

[Tint](/effects/colour/tint/) does the same job with two colours. The middle
stop is what makes this a duotone *print* rather than a straight fade between
two ends: it is where the character of the look lives.

## What each control does

- **Highlights** — the colour the brightest part of the picture takes. Keep it
  near white for a print, or push it warm for candlelight.
- **Midtones** — the colour mid-grey takes, and the one that decides what the
  look is. A warm brown gives sepia, a mid blue gives a cyanotype, a mid green
  gives night-vision.
- **Shadows** — the colour the darkest part takes. A deep blue rather than
  black is the classic film-print choice, and reads as air rather than as a
  hole.
- **Mix** — how much of the toned picture replaces the original. This is where
  an imported After Effects "Blend with original" lands.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — tone only part of the frame; see
  [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**The three stops are placed where the eye puts them**, so Midtones lands on the
grey a person would point at rather than on half of the light. The default trio
is a split-toned print — deep blue shadows, warm brown midtones, warm white
highlights — so the effect does something worth looking at the moment you drop
it on.

**Highlights above white keep their headroom.** A specular four times brighter
than white comes back as the Highlights colour four times over, rather than
being flattened to it — so a toned picture still has something for
[Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/) to catch.

**Colours are scene-linear and can go above 1**, which is how you get a
highlight stop that glows rather than one that just sits at white.

**For an even simpler two-colour version**, use [Tint](/effects/colour/tint/).
For a two-tone stencil with no ramp at all, use
[Threshold](/effects/colour/threshold/).
