Curves is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Bends the relationship between what came in and what goes out. Five points
on each curve — black, shadows, midtones, highlights, white — say what a pixel
of that brightness comes out as; the curve runs smoothly through them.

This is the grade you reach for when a slider is not shaped enough. The classic
move is the S: shadows pulled down a little, highlights pushed up, midtones left
where they are, which is contrast that spares the middle. The Red, Green and
Blue curves do the same thing one channel at a time, which is how a picture gets
warm shadows and cool highlights without a colour cast across the whole frame.

The curve cannot overshoot between two of your points, so a lifted highlight
never dips the curve below its neighbour and never rings a dark halo into a
roll-off. Values above 1 — the parts of a scene-linear picture brighter than
white — carry on along the slope at the top rather than being clipped, so
pushing a curve hard and pulling exposure back down recovers the detail.

Two things Curves cannot do yet, and both are deliberate. The five points sit at
fixed places along the input: you shape the curve by moving outputs up and down,
not by placing points wherever you like. And the panel draws twenty numbers
rather than a curve you drag — the drawn editor is a later change to the panel,
not to what a project stores, so a curve authored today survives it.

Every one of those twenty numbers keyframes and takes an expression, which is
more than After Effects' own Curves can say: its curve is stored as a blob that
can only be held between keyframes, never animated.

## What each control does

- **Black**, **Shadows**, **Midtones**, **Highlights**, **White** — what that
  channel's curve outputs at inputs 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. At the defaults
  every output is its own input, which is a straight line and no change at all.
- **Master** applies to all three channels; **Red**, **Green** and **Blue**
  apply to one each. The colour curves run first and Master runs over the top of
  them, which is the order Photoshop and After Effects use.
- **Mix** — how much of the graded picture replaces the original.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — drive the effect from another layer's
  brightness; see [Effects](/use/effects/).
