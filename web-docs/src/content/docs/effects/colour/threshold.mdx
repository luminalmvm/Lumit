Threshold is a **Colour** effect.

## What it is for

Every pixel is asked one question — is it brighter than the Level? — and comes
back white if it is and black if it is not. That turns a photograph into a
stencil: the two-tone poster, a stamped or screen-printed title, a quick matte
cut out of a bright object, and the source shape for a
[Roughen edges](/effects/stylise/roughen-edges/) or a
[Drop shadow](/effects/stylise/drop-shadow/) treatment.

## What each control does

- **Level** — where the cut sits. **50** is mid-grey; lower it and more of the
  picture comes back white, raise it and more comes back black. Animating it
  sweeps the stencil open or shut, which is a good title reveal.
- **Softness** — how wide the crossing between black and white is. **0** is a
  hard cut, and raising it gives a gradient between the two tones — useful for
  a smoky or airbrushed stencil.
- **Mix** — how much of the stencil replaces the original picture.
- **Matte** and **Invert** — threshold only part of the frame; see
  [Effects](/use/effects/).

## Notes

**Level is where you think it is.** 50 lands on the grey a person would point
at, not on half of the light — Lumit works in scene-linear light, where half is
very nearly white, so the control is placed perceptually instead.

**Transparency survives the cut.** A thresholded layer keeps its shape: the
effect decides what colour a pixel is, never whether it is there. Drop it on a
keyed layer and you get a black-and-white version of the key, not a white
rectangle.

**Even at Softness 0 the cut is antialiased.** The crossing is a thousandth of
the tone range wide, which is far under a pixel on any real edge, so the stencil
has clean diagonals rather than staircases.

**For a colour version of the same idea**, use
[Posterize](/effects/colour/posterize/) at a low Levels count, or
[Tritone](/effects/colour/tritone/) to map the two tones onto colours of your
choosing.
