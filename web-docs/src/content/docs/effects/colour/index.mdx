## What this family does

Changing what colour the picture is. Every effect here does one thing, so a
grade is a short stack you can read down rather than one panel of forty
controls.

They divide into four jobs.

**Correcting** — getting the picture to neutral. **Exposure** sets brightness in
photographic stops, **Brightness** carries the familiar brightness-and-contrast
pair, **Temperature** and **Photo filter** take a warm or cool cast off — one by
temperature, one as a coloured glass on the lens — **Contrast** and **Gamma** set
the spread and the mid-tones, **Levels** and **Curves** set them per channel with
the whole tone range in view, and **Colour balance** does all of it per channel at
once, with separate control of shadows, mid-tones and highlights.
**Shadow highlight** is the local one: it rescues a backlit shot by judging every
pixel against its surroundings rather than against the frame.

**Adjusting colourfulness** — **Saturation** scales it evenly,
**Vibrancy** scales it more where there is less of it, which protects skin tones
and already-vivid areas from going to mush, and **Hue and saturation** does it
per colour range with a hue shift beside it.

**Removing colour** — **Black and white** converts to grey with six sliders that
say how bright each colour comes out, which is how two colours of the same
brightness are kept apart.

**Looks** — **LUT** applies a grade authored elsewhere as a file, **Tint** and
**Tritone** map the picture onto two or three colours of your choosing,
**Posterize** cuts the tone range into flat bands, **Threshold** cuts it to black
and white outright, **Invert** flips it, **Hue shift** turns the whole colour
wheel, and **Vignette** darkens toward the edges of the frame.

## The order to put them in

Effects run down the stack, so order changes the result. The usual sequence is
the one a colourist would follow: fix exposure first, then the white balance,
then contrast and mid-tones, then colourfulness, and put creative looks — a LUT,
a tint, a vignette — last, on top of a picture that is already neutral. A LUT
applied to an under-exposed plate grades the mistake along with the picture.

Everything here works in scene-linear light, which is why highlights above white
survive rather than clipping the moment you touch them. Values brighter than
white are real and meaningful, and [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/) depends on
them, so nothing in this family throws them away unless you ask it to.

To apply a grade to a whole montage rather than one layer, put the stack on an
adjustment layer. See [Layers](/use/layers/).
