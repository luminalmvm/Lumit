Tint is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Replaces the picture's colour with a gradient between two you pick. Every
pixel's brightness decides where it lands on that gradient: the darkest pixels
take the first colour, the brightest take the second, and everything between is
mixed accordingly.

This is the duotone look — the poster-style two-colour treatment — and it is
also the most direct way to force a section of a montage into one palette
regardless of what was shot. Because the picture's brightness structure is kept
and only its colour is replaced, the result still reads as the original footage
rather than as a flat wash.

Left at black and white it maps every pixel to its own brightness, which is a
clean greyscale. That is a useful thing in itself, and it is the reason the
effect does something visible the moment you add it rather than sitting inert.

For a treatment that shifts existing colours rather than replacing them, use
[Hue shift](/effects/colour/hue-shift/) or
[Colour balance](/effects/colour/colour-balance/).

## What each control does

- **Map black to** — the colour the darkest pixels become. Lifting it off black
  is what stops the result feeling harsh, and tinting it is where most of the
  character of a duotone comes from.
- **Map white to** — the colour the brightest pixels become. Pulling it below
  white flattens the picture; pushing it past white keeps highlights hot enough
  for a [Glow](/effects/stylise/glow/) downstream to still find them.
- **Mix** — how much of the mapped picture replaces the original. Partial values
  are genuinely useful here: a light blend of a duotone over the real footage
  reads as a grade rather than as a filter.

Both colours open the picker with its screen dropper, so a palette can be
sampled from a reference image.
