Vibrancy is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

A saturation boost that pays attention to what it is boosting. Pixels that are
already vivid are lifted very little; pixels that are close to grey are lifted a
lot.

That single difference is what makes it the safer control of the two. A plain
[Saturation](/effects/colour/saturation/) lift takes an already-saturated sky or
a neon sign past what the picture can hold, and the result clips into flat
patches of pure colour. Vibrancy leaves those alone and spends its effort on the
muted parts, so skin tones, concrete, foliage and the general middle of the
picture gain while the vivid areas stay intact.

On game footage, where user-interface elements and effects are often already at
full saturation while the environment is not, this is usually the one you want.

Use plain Saturation when you specifically want an even scale — for going fully
greyscale, or for pulling colour out — and this when you want the picture to
look richer without picking a fight with its brightest colours.

In the Effect Controls panel:

- **Matte** scales how much of the effect each pixel gets: white applies it in full, black leaves the pixel as it arrived, grey part way. **Invert** reads the matte the other way round, light for dark.

## What each control does

- **Amount** — how hard the lift is applied before the per-pixel weighting. At
  zero the picture passes through untouched. The slider reaches a heavy boost
  and can be typed past it; the weighting means even a large value stays kinder
  to saturated areas than a much smaller Saturation value would be.
- **Mix** — how much of the lifted picture replaces the original.
