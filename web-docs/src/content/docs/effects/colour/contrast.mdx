Contrast is a [Colour](/effects/colour/) effect.

## What it is for

Pushes the picture away from mid-grey, or pulls it toward it. Raising contrast
sends the darks darker and the brights brighter; lowering it flattens everything
toward the middle.

This is the punch control. Game capture is often flat, and a modest lift here is
most of what makes a montage feel graded. Lowering contrast is just as useful:
flattening a background layer is a quick way to make a foreground read against
it, and a heavily flattened plate is the starting point for a film-style look
where the black point is lifted deliberately.

The pivot is a plain mid-grey, so the control behaves like the contrast slider
in a photo editor — symmetric about the middle — rather than like a light
meter's grey card. Highlights are never clipped on the way through, so pushing
contrast hard and then pulling exposure back down recovers the detail.

For a change that affects mid-tones without touching the two ends, use
[Gamma](/effects/colour/gamma/) instead.

## What each control does

- **Contrast** — the spread. At the neutral point the picture passes through
  untouched; below it everything converges on mid-grey, and at zero the whole
  frame is flat grey. Above it the picture expands away from the middle, and
  there is no ceiling, so a deliberately crushed look is a matter of typing a
  larger number.
- **Mix** — how much of the graded picture replaces the original.
